# Teajia — Vision & Strategy (April 2026)

## What Teajia Is

Teajia is infrastructure for the global tea community. Not a franchise. Not a SaaS product. A living system that provides the tools, sourcing, education, and scaffolding that tea professionals and enthusiasts build their practice on.

The name "Teajia" means something. On a bag of tea, it's a mark of quality sourcing. On a tea house, it signals a shared tradition. On the app, it means the tools were built by someone who actually pours tea for a living. The identity is rooted in service — to the people who drink tea, to the land that produces it, and to the culture that carries it forward.

The long-term trajectory is a foundation: preserving tea-producing land and giving the tea community a way to give back. This shapes decisions now but gets announced when it's ready.

---

## The Paradox, Resolved

Tea is about presence — being with yourself, with others, with the cup in your hands. Not staring at a phone.

Teajia never sits at the tea table. It works in three moments:

- **Before:** Helps the host source, stock, price, prepare, invite, and set the stage
- **During:** Stays in the pocket. The host pours. The guest receives. Humans connect.
- **After:** Captures what happened. Connects the guest to what they loved. Prepares for next time.

The technology is the supply chain, the education, and the memory. The presence is the product. They never compete because they never occupy the same moment.

## Who Uses Teajia

People interact with Teajia in different ways depending on where they are in their tea journey. These aren't rigid tiers — people move between them fluidly.

### Adrian (Platform Owner)

The person who built the tools because he needed them. Sources tea through 20 years of relationships. Hosts sessions. Designs tea spaces. Writes and commissions editorial content. Manages the wholesale catalog. Onboards operators. Sets the standard by using every feature daily.

**Needs:** Compass for field sourcing, inventory management, event hosting, invoicing, CRM, editorial tools, wholesale catalog management, and eventually platform administration.

### Tea Professionals & Masters

People deep in the tea world who have their own practice, their own sourcing relationships, their own expertise. They may not want a Teajia tea house. They want professional tools for their work — field sourcing with Compass, inventory tracking, event management. Some are the experts that articles are written about. Some contribute content. Some just quietly use the tools.

**Needs:** Compass (sourcing intelligence), inventory, events, possibly contributing to the magazine. They bring their own identity and their own tea. Teajia supports their work without branding it.

### Tea House Operators

People who want to open or run a tea space. They see the value in the increased customers, the shared education, the sourcing network, the operational tools. They may carry the Teajia name alongside their own identity — "Casa do Chá — a Teajia partner" — or the branding may be as minimal as the logo on the tea bag.

These operators typically do NOT want to do their own sourcing. They want access to quality tea through the network, tools to run their space, and the education infrastructure to serve their customers well. Compass is largely irrelevant to them. Inventory, events, and the customer-facing scaffolding are everything.

**Needs:** Inventory, events, customer CRM, invoicing, public-facing shop/event pages, access to wholesale catalog, education content for their customers. Onboarding and training (potentially in-person through consulting, potentially self-serve through the platform).

### Tea Workers & Staff

People working at a tea location. Learning about tea. Setting up events. Managing inventory. Hosting sessions. Selling tea. Their day is: learn, prepare, serve, restock.

**Needs:** Scoped access to their location's inventory, event tools, and educational resources. Simple, functional interfaces. They don't need the full admin complexity.

### Home Practitioners

People who love tea, have a growing collection at home, host friends for tea sometimes, and want to go deeper. They attend events. They buy tea. They read the magazine. They might start hosting small gatherings. Maybe someday they open a space.

This is the pipeline from guest to operator. The app should support their journey without pushing them along it.

**Needs:** Buy tea, attend events, learn, track what they love, host friends simply. A lightweight version of the professional tools — or just the public-facing features done well.

### Guests

Someone who showed up to a tea session because a friend invited them. Or found the website and thought "this is cool." They touch the app lightly. The ideal path: attend → enjoy → maybe read about the teas afterward → maybe buy some → come back → invite a friend → start hosting their own sessions → eventually, maybe, open a space.

Suggestions without pushing. The app should feel like: "Oh, this is convenient... and wow, this all connects... I'd like to come to more of this."

**Needs:** Find events, attend, buy tea they tasted, rate/remember what they experienced, read about what was served, discover more. The experience should be so natural that deeper engagement happens without friction or pressure.

## The Five Layers

### 1. Tools (the engine)

Professional-grade operational software built by a tea professional for tea professionals.

- **Tea Compass** — Field sourcing journal. Capture teas at markets and vendor visits, compare prices across vendors, track purchase intent, promote to inventory. For sourcing professionals and tea masters. NOT for tea house operators who source through the network.
- **Inventory** — Stock management with inline editing, multi-currency cost tracking, vendor relationships, status workflows (Draft → Active → Archived), public/personal/sample visibility controls.
- **Events** — From dead simple ("4 seats, Saturday, my place") to fully curated (approval-based RSVP, briefing cards, tea menu with brew order, post-session archive). Function first. Advanced features available but tucked away. The default should be: invite friends, know who's coming, know what kind of gathering it is.
- **Orders & Invoicing** — Multi-currency, WhatsApp-integrated, stock-deducting. Sale and purchase directions. PDF receipts.
- **Customer CRM** — Tags (wholesale, retail, friend, vendor, VIP), purchase history, preferred currency, contact preferences.
- **CSV Import** — Bulk inventory loading from vendor receipts. Day-one essential for any new operator.

### 2. Sourcing (the supply chain)

Adrian's 20-year vendor relationships become a sourcing network that operators can access.

- **Wholesale catalog** — Tea and teaware available to network operators at wholesale pricing. Trust-gated access (deepens as the relationship develops).
- **Sourcing-to-shelf pipeline** — Operator places wholesale order → receives tea → imports into their local inventory → serves or sells to their customers.
- **Compass network intelligence** — Tea masters and sourcing professionals using Compass generate supply-chain intelligence (pricing, availability, quality) that benefits the whole network. Anonymized and aggregated, never exposing individual vendor relationships.

### 3. Education (the knowledge)

The content that no individual tea house could create alone. Adrian's editorial voice plus contributions from tea masters and community members across the network.

- **Magazine (Read)** — Editorial content: stories, interviews, origin reports, cultural essays. Community contributors write through a structured process with editorial approval to maintain quality and prevent commercial selling. The goal is an offering to the tea community, not a marketing channel.
- **Learn Hub** — Structured curricula (Foundation, Brewing, Flavor, Mindfulness, Vessels, Community). Free at the foundational level. Deeper training may carry a fee or require in-person workshops.
- **Brewing Guides** — Portable. Printable cards, QR-linked pages. Information that goes OFF the screen and onto the tea table, into the package.
- **Glossary & Reference** — Shared knowledge base available to all.

Education content is the most undervalued asset. It's what makes a Teajia-connected tea house more than just a shop — it gives every operator a library of depth to offer their customers.

### 4. Scaffolding (the public face)

The customer-facing layer that each operator presents to their community.

- **Shop** — Product catalog with rich storytelling (lore, tasting notes, mood, terroir). WhatsApp/email inquiry checkout is correct — every order is a relationship, not a transaction. Don't apologize for it; frame it as personal service.
- **Event pages** — Public-facing event discovery and RSVP. Currently shared via direct links. Future: discoverable across the network.
- **Location identity** — Each operator's public presence. Simpler than Adrian's magazine-forward homepage. An operator needs: upcoming events, featured teas, who we are, how to find us.

### 5. Community (the network)

The people and places connected by a shared tradition.

- **Network directory** — Discover Teajia-connected tea spaces and sessions worldwide. Not a store locator. A lineage map. A guest who loved tea in Taipei can find a trusted table in Melbourne.
- **Guest portability** — One account, welcomed at any table. Tasting history, event attendance, favorites follow you across locations.
- **Magazine contributors** — Tea masters, educators, and practitioners contributing their knowledge. Editorial standards maintained. Approval process ensures quality.
- **Verification & trust** — Hosts can earn badges/verification after proving themselves to a certain level. The platform can't control consistency — each session is the host's expression — but it can signal trustworthiness.
- **Foundation (future)** — Land preservation, giving back to tea-producing regions. Announced when articulated. The driving force behind the mission, not yet the public message.

## Design Principles

### 1. Function First, Magic Second

The events system should make it easy to invite friends over for tea. If someone also wants to specify that it's a silent meditation, they can. If they want approval workflows and briefing cards, those are there. But the default is: how many seats, what kind of gathering, which friends are coming.

Advanced features should feel like a bonus when discovered, not a requirement. Nothing should feel lacking if the advanced features aren't used.

### 2. Suggest Without Pushing

After a session, a guest sees the teas that were served and can buy them. The app doesn't say "BUY NOW." It says "here's what you tasted" and makes purchasing effortless if they want to. The path from guest to practitioner to host to operator should be natural, never pressured.

### 3. The App Disappears During Practice

No live tasting modes. No phone-out-at-the-table features. The app prepares (sourcing, inventory, invitations, brew guides) and remembers (post-session recap, ratings, purchase links). Between those moments: presence.

### 4. Human Curation Over Algorithms

At 139 products and a trust-based community, Adrian's voice IS the recommendation engine. "If you like this, try this" should be hand-curated, not computed. Community tasting aggregation is noise at this scale. Algorithmic recommendations undermine the trust model.

As the network grows, this may evolve. But start with human curation and only add computation when the humans can't keep up.

### 5. The Brand Is the Tea

The logo is clean and minimal. On a bag of tea, it's a quality mark. To use the tools, you don't have to advertise the brand. Some operators will feature the name prominently. Others will have it on the bag and nowhere else. Both are fine.

### 6. Quality Over Quantity

The magazine templates need to reach the level of quality that Adrian expects. 150+ layout variants mean nothing if they don't feel like a real magazine. The editorial standard applies to everything: product descriptions, event pages, the Learn Hub, the shop. Every touchpoint should feel intentionally crafted.

## Current State (April 2026)

### What exists and works
- Full inventory management with inline editing, multi-currency, vendor tracking
- Tea Compass sourcing tool (user-scoped, field capture, vendor comparison, draft promotion)
- Events system with approval-based RSVP, progressive location disclosure, post-session archive
- Shop with AlcoveCards, rich product data, WhatsApp checkout
- Magazine with 150+ layout variants (quality needs improvement)
- Learn Hub with 6 modules and 3 learning tracks
- Consulting page with tiered services
- Invoice/order pipeline with stock deduction
- Customer CRM with tags and history
- CSV import for bulk inventory
- Multi-currency support throughout
- Three user roles (owner/admin/user) with admin request workflow
- JWT auth, Cloudflare Workers + D1 backend

### What doesn't exist yet
- Multi-account data isolation (no `account_id` on tables)
- Wholesale catalog and ordering
- Location/operator onboarding
- Network directory
- Guest account portability
- Simplified event creation for casual hosts
- Education content embedding/portability
- Operator-facing public page template
- Trust/verification badges
- Contributor workflow for magazine content

### What needs fixing (from existing audits)
- Dead share button on AlcoveCard
- Newsletter signup stores locally, never reaches backend
- JWT expires silently with no user notification
- Checkout should be framed as "Send Inquiry" not "Checkout"
- Magazine template quality below Adrian's standard
- No product URLs (modals don't update URL, can't share links to specific products)
- No offline/network error indicator
- 328 instances of hardcoded rgba() violating color system
- 8 Google Fonts blocking render

## What This Document Replaces

This vision document supersedes the assumptions in the previous audit documents (AUDIT.md, VISION_AUDIT_0 through 7, FUNCTIONAL_AUDIT.md, UI_UX_AUDIT.md, DESIGN_SYSTEM_AUDIT.md, WEBSITE_TEARDOWN.md). Those audits contain valuable technical detail and should be referenced for implementation specifics, but their strategic framing assumed a single-owner consumer product. The recommendations in those audits should be evaluated against this vision:

**Keep from those audits:**
- All bug fixes and functional improvements (dead buttons, broken forms, JWT handling)
- UX friction reduction (product URLs, checkout framing, search improvements)
- Design system cleanup (color token violations, font loading)
- Admin workflow improvements that make Adrian's daily work better

**Deprioritize from those audits:**
- Knowledge graph / AI contextual surfacing (hand-curation is correct at this scale)
- Tasting note similarity engine (curate manually)
- Community tasting aggregation (Adrian's voice is the review)
- Adaptive learning paths (curate 3 paths, link to practitioner stage)
- Subscription / auto-replenish (artisan model, not commodity)
- Streak trackers / gamification (presence, not performance)
- Live tasting mode (phones stay in pockets during sessions)
- Full analytics pipeline (Adrian packs every order — he knows what's selling)

**Reframe from those audits:**
- WhatsApp checkout is a feature, not a bug — frame it as personal service
- The events system isn't too heavy — it's right for curated sessions, needs a simpler mode for casual hosts
- The Compass isn't a mindfulness journal — it's supply-chain intelligence for sourcing professionals
- "Building hallways between rooms" is correct, but the hallways should suggest, not push
