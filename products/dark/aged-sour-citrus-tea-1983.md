A 1983 sour citrus tea (酸柑仔茶), a traditional Hakka preparation over forty years old.

Suan gan zi cha is a Hakka folk tea made by stuffing tea leaves (usually oolong or green tea) into hollowed-out sour citrus fruits, then repeatedly steaming and sun-drying over many cycles. The result is a dense, dark, citrus-infused tea ball that the Hakka traditionally used as a home remedy. At forty-plus years, the citrus oils and tea have completely merged.

## Terroir

Taiwan, likely Hakka regions of Hsinchu, Miaoli, or Taoyuan. Suan gan zi cha is a distinctly Taiwanese Hakka product — the sour citrus (Citrus sunki or similar) grows in the hills of northwestern Taiwan, and the tradition of stuffing them with tea is passed through families and communities.

## Processing

Hollowed sour citrus fruit stuffed with tea leaves, then subjected to nine cycles (traditionally) of steaming and sun-drying. Each cycle further integrates the tea and citrus while reducing moisture. The finished product is rock-hard and shelf-stable. Four decades of additional aging have continued the integration at a molecular level.

## Mood

Hakka medicine

## Experience

Break off a piece and brew it — the liquid is dark and complex, neither purely tea nor purely citrus but something third. The aged citrus peel contributes a bitter-sweet herbal quality while the tea provides body and depth. In Hakka tradition, this is medicine first and beverage second. At forty years, it is both.
