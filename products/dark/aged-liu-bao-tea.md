An aged loose liu bao from Guangxi, come to us from the Guangzhou tea trade. Dark, settled, and full in the body. A good first step into the world of Chinese dark tea for anyone new to the category.

## Terroir

Liu bao is the dark tea of Guangxi, in southern China. It grows in the humid hills west of the Xi River, a landscape of low mountains and warm mist.

## Processing

A post-fermented dark tea, not a puerh. The leaves are withered, rolled, lightly heaped so warmth and moisture begin the fermentation, then dried and aged loose. Time in storage turns the leaf dark.

## Lore

The tea takes its name from Liubao, a town in Wuzhou prefecture. It travelled south for generations on the old water routes into Southeast Asia, where it became the daily cup of Chinese labourers in Malaysia and Singapore.
