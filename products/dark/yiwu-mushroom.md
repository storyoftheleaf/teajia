Yiwu mushroom-shaped pu-erh (蘑菇形) is one of the more unusual compression formats in pu-erh production.

Shaped like a mushroom or toadstool with a rounded cap and narrow stem, this format was historically used for ceremonial or offering teas in Tibetan Buddhist practice. The Yiwu origin adds prestige to an already distinctive piece.

## Terroir

Yiwu township, Mengla county, Xishuangbanna, Yunnan. Yiwu is one of the most famous pu-erh origins. The mushroom format suggests either Tibetan trade heritage or a deliberate nod to that tradition.

## Processing

Pu-erh from the Yiwu region compressed into the traditional mushroom shape (also called jincha or tight tea). The unusual shape creates complex aging dynamics, with the dense stem aging very differently from the broader cap. The format has roots in the tea-horse trade to Tibet.

## Mood

quiet ceremony

## Experience

The shape matters here. Breaking tea from a mushroom-format cake feels different from prying apart a flat disk. The Yiwu sweetness is present, aged and smooth, with a camphor note that rises through steepings. A tea with a form that invites curiosity and a flavor that rewards it.
