Bai Liang (hundred liang) tea is one of the great monumental teas of Anhua, Hunan province.

Traditionally wrapped in bamboo bark and pressed into massive cylindrical columns weighing over three kilograms, these teas were designed for the tea-horse trade routes. The 花卷 (hua juan) name refers to the patterned bamboo wrapping that marks them.

## Terroir

Anhua county in Hunan province is the heartland of Chinese dark tea. The region's unique fungal environment and the specific strains of Eurotium cristatum (golden flowers) that colonize the tea during processing cannot be replicated elsewhere. Cool mountain streams and dense forest cover define the landscape.

## Processing

Anhua dark tea processing: the leaf is kill-greened, rolled, piled for microbial fermentation, then steamed and packed into bamboo-wrapped columns under extreme pressure. The bamboo casing creates a micro-environment for continued slow transformation.

## Experience

Dark and full-bodied with a woodsy, mushroom-like depth. It has a substantial mouthfeel that coats the throat. Not delicate. This is a tea built for sustenance, for cold days and long evenings.
