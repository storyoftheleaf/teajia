A 1985 Liu An basket tea (六安籃茶), forty years old.

Liu An is one of the most distinctive dark teas in China — packed into small bamboo baskets lined with bamboo leaves, creating a unique micro-aging environment. Originally produced in Qimen county (better known for its red tea, Keemun), Liu An was historically exported to Southeast Asia as both a daily drinking tea and a medicinal preparation. The bamboo basket format is not just packaging — it is part of the processing.

## Terroir

Qimen county, Anhui province, in the Huangshan mountain region of southern Anhui. The same terroir that produces Keemun red tea — foggy mountains, acidic red soil, and a temperate climate with distinct seasons. The tea plants here produce leaf with clean, mineral character that ages with clarity.

## Processing

Liu An processing involves a light roast, then packing into bamboo baskets lined with bamboo leaves and sealing for long-term aging. The bamboo lining creates a micro-environment that flavors the tea over decades — a gentle, clean influence distinct from the cedar or camphor storage used for pu'erh. Forty years of basket aging have fully integrated the bamboo character.

## Experience

The bamboo note is the signature — clean, green, and sweet, woven through the aged plum and mineral. Liu An has a lightness that other dark teas lack. It does not weigh you down. The warmth is gentle, the sweetness is clean, and the finish leaves the mouth feeling fresh. Best in the evening when you want calm without heaviness.
