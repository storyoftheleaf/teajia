Liu Bao tea from 1980 represents a style of Guangxi dark tea that was once commonplace and is now increasingly rare.

Traditionally consumed by tin miners in Southeast Asia for its cooling properties, liu bao was a working tea before it became a collector's item. Four decades of aging have elevated this one beyond its humble origins.

## Terroir

Cangwu county in Guangxi province, along the Xijiang river system. The specific microbial environment of this region, combined with limestone-rich soil and tropical humidity, gives liu bao its signature cooling sensation that distinguishes it from all other dark teas.

## Processing

Post-fermented Guangxi dark tea. Liu Bao undergoes a unique dual fermentation: first a light wo dui, then extended basket aging in Guangxi's subtropical climate. The cooling quality (often described as 'cha qi') develops over decades.

## Experience

The cooling sensation is real. It moves through the throat and settles in the chest. The flavor is clean, aged, almost mineral. There is nothing harsh about it. A tea that feels like it is doing something to you rather than just tasting like something.
