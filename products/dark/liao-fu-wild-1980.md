Wild-harvested (野生) Liao Fu dark tea from 1980.

The wild designation means this was made from uncultivated tea trees, likely growing in semi-wild conditions in Guangxi's forests. Wild material produces a broader, less predictable flavor profile than cultivated garden tea.

## Terroir

Guangxi province. Wild tea trees in Guangxi grow in forest conditions without cultivation, their character shaped by the surrounding ecosystem rather than agricultural management. The resulting leaf carries a broader, more untamed profile.

## Processing

Wild-harvested Guangxi dark tea from 1980, processed in the Liao Fu tradition. Wild tree material is more variable than cultivated leaf, producing a wider range of flavors. Forty-plus years of aging have integrated the wild character into something cohesive.

## Mood

forest floor

## Experience

Wilder and less refined than its cultivated counterparts, and more interesting for it. There is a herbaceous breadth and a forest quality that garden-grown Liao Fu does not have. The cooling sensation is strong. A tea that still tastes like the place it grew.
