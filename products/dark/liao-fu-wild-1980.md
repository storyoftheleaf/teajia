An aged 1980 loose-leaf pu-erh (san cha) carrying the Liao Fu name — a Hong Kong tea house that began importing border-region tea from the Yunnan/northern Vietnam line in the 1960s. The wild (野生) designation reflects leaf from uncultivated tea trees.

## Terroir

Wild tea trees in the north of Vietnam, on the border with Yunnan. This is the large-leaf assamica family (Shan Tuyet) that grows at high altitude in northern Vietnam's Ha Giang, Lao Cai, Yen Bai, and Son La provinces, largely untended. Wild trees grow without cultivation, their leaf shaped by the forest ecosystem rather than agricultural management, and the material carries a broader, less predictable character.

## Processing

Loose-leaf (san cha) wild-tree puer from 1980, in the raw/sheng tradition, held in dry storage. Wild material is more variable than cultivated leaf. Four decades of aging in storage have integrated the wild character into a cohesive, aged profile.
