A 1960 liu bao, sixty-five years old and among the oldest in the collection.

Liu bao of this age is exceedingly rare. By 1960, the Guangxi liu bao tradition was still centered on village production and basket aging before the Wuzhou Tea Factory standardized the process. This predates the factory era and likely represents artisanal, small-batch production from the old methods. The cooling qi that liu bao is prized for reaches its deepest expression at this age.

## Terroir

Guangxi province, southern China. The Xijiang river system and the surrounding limestone karst mountains define liu bao terroir. Village-produced liu bao from 1960 came from old trees along the river valleys, processed by families who had been making this tea for generations. The specific microbial environment of Guangxi — warm, humid, mineral-rich — is irreplaceable.

## Processing

Traditional village liu bao: light initial fermentation, then packed into bamboo baskets for extended aging. Sixty-five years of this process have produced a tea where the original leaf character is entirely gone, replaced by pure aged depth. The cooling sensation characteristic of old liu bao is at its most powerful in material of this age.

## Experience

The cooling qi moves through the body before you register the flavor. Then comes the betel nut, the earth, the herbs, and finally a sweetness so deep it feels geological. This is the oldest liu bao expression in the collection — a tea that has become something more than tea. It cools, it grounds, it settles.
