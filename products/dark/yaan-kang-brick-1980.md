A 1980 Kang brick (康磚) from Ya'an, Sichuan, forty-five years old.

Kang zhuan is a traditional Tibetan border tea (藏茶) produced in Ya'an for centuries. The name literally means "Kang brick" — Kang referring to the Kangding region on the ancient Tea Horse Road between Sichuan and Tibet. This was survival tea, compressed into bricks for transport by yak caravan across the Tibetan plateau. The Tibetans mixed it with yak butter and salt. At forty-five years, it has become something the original buyers would not recognize.

## Terroir

Ya'an city, Sichuan province. Ya'an sits at the gateway between the Sichuan basin and the Tibetan plateau, in a region of heavy rainfall and fog. The tea plants here are robust, coarse-leaf varieties bred for volume and durability rather than delicacy. The terroir produces a tea with body and substance — qualities essential for its historical role as a dietary staple.

## Processing

Kang brick processing involves heavy fermentation, pressing into dense brick form, and extended drying. The leaf material is mature — large leaves and stems — selected for body rather than finesse. The compression is extreme, creating a brick that can survive months of transport by yak. Forty-five years of aging have refined what was originally a utilitarian product into something unexpectedly complex.

## Experience

Dense and earthy with a tobacco-like quality and a warming body sensation. The sweetness is dark and muted, more felt than tasted. This is not a delicate tea. It has the weight and presence of something that was built to sustain people across mountain passes. At forty-five years, it has earned a complexity that its makers never intended.
