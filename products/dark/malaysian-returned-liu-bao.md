A liu bao (六堡) dark tea from Guangxi that went abroad, aged in Southeast Asia, and came back to Guangzhou.

## Lore

Liu bao takes its name from Liu Bao village in Cangwu County, meaning 'six stockades.' From the mid-1800s onward, Hakka migrants carried it down the Tea Ship Route through Guangzhou and Hong Kong to the tin mines and rubber plantations of British Malaya, where workers called it life-saving tea and Ipoh became known as its second hometown. This lot is a returned (huiliu) liu bao: received in Guangzhou in the 1990s, sent out to age for years in the humid warehouses of Malaysia and Singapore, then bought back into the Guangzhou trade.

## Terroir

Liu bao is a protected origin tea from Guangxi in southern China. Only leaves grown around Cangwu and Wuzhou, along the Xijiang river basin, may carry the name. The area is subtropical and warm year round, with red acidic soil and steady humidity that the tea plants have settled into over centuries.

## Processing

Liu bao is a hei cha, or dark tea, from the same post-fermented family as shou puerh and Fu brick. After the leaves are killed and rolled, they go through wet piling (wo dui), where they are heaped damp so microbes ferment them, turning the leaf dark and the liquor smooth. It is traditionally packed loose into large bamboo baskets to keep aging. Aged liu bao drinks dark and mellow with a gentle sweetness, and the binlang or areca nut aroma is the mark that time in storage has done its work.
