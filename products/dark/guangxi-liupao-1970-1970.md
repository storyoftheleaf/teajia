A 1970 liu bao, fifty-plus years old.

Liu bao of this age develops a quality that younger versions only hint at: the cooling sensation deepens, the earthiness refines, and something almost medicinal emerges. This is among the oldest liu bao in the collection.

## Terroir

Guangxi province, southern China. Village-produced liu bao from this era typically came from old-growth tea trees along the Xijiang river system, where the combination of limestone karst geology and subtropical climate creates conditions unique to this region.

## Processing

Traditional village-processed liu bao, likely basket-aged in Guangxi before reaching Taiwan. Five decades of natural aging have transformed it completely. The original processing character is no longer detectable, replaced by pure aged depth.

## Mood

deep grounding

## Experience

The oldest liu bao in the collection. The cooling sensation is profound. It moves through the body in a way that younger teas cannot replicate. The flavor is secondary to the feeling. This is a tea you sit with, not just drink.
