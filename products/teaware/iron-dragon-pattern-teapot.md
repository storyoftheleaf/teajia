Cast iron teapots (tetsubin in Japanese, tie hu in Chinese) retain heat longer than any other material.

The dragon pattern connects this piece to a long tradition of decorative iron casting in East Asian tea culture.

## Terroir

Cast iron teapot traditions span China and Japan. Japanese tetsubin are the most famous, but Chinese iron casting has its own distinct heritage.

## Processing

Cast iron body with enamel interior lining. The iron retains heat exceptionally well, keeping water hot far longer than ceramic or glass. The enamel prevents rust and iron leaching.

## Mood

enduring heat

## Experience

Holds temperature like nothing else. Heavy in the hand, slow to cool. Best for teas that need sustained heat: aged pu-erh, dark teas, heavy roast oolongs.
