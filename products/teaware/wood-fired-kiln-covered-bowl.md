Wood-fired (柴烧) ceramics are fired in traditional kilns fueled by wood rather than gas or electricity.

The ash from the burning wood settles on the pieces during firing, creating natural, unrepeatable glaze patterns. Each piece is unique because the fire itself decides the surface.

## Terroir

Wood-fired kiln traditions exist across China, Japan, and Taiwan. Each kiln and each firing produces different results based on wood type, kiln temperature, and placement.

## Processing

Wood-fired ceramic. The kiln atmosphere and ash deposits create surfaces that cannot be replicated by modern firing methods. The clay body interacts with the ash to produce colors and textures unique to each piece's position in the kiln.

fire-touched

## Experience

A gaiwan with a surface the fire chose. The slight texture and natural glaze add a warmth that factory gaiwans lack. Functionally identical, aesthetically alive.
