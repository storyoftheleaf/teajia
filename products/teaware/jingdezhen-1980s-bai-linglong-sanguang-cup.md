Bai Linglong (白玲珑) is a Jingdezhen porcelain style featuring delicate rice-grain perforations filled with translucent glaze. When held to light, the perforations glow.

Sanguang (三光) means 'three lights,' referring to the luminous effect. These are 1980s production, a period when Jingdezhen factories still maintained high craft standards.

## Terroir

Jingdezhen, Jiangxi province. The porcelain capital of China for over a thousand years. 1980s factory production represents the tail end of state-managed craft before privatization changed the industry.

## Processing

Jingdezhen porcelain with rice-grain perforation technique (玲珑). Small holes are cut into the unfired body and filled with transparent glaze that fuses during firing, creating windows of light in the porcelain wall. This technique dates to the Ming Dynasty.

## Experience

Hold one to the light. The rice-grain windows glow. These are pieces of Jingdezhen history, made when the factories still employed masters trained in centuries-old techniques. Functional and beautiful.
