A shou pu-erh cake from the 1990s with a loose compression.

The term 'loose cake' (散饼) describes a pressing style where the leaves are not tightly compressed, allowing more air contact and faster aging. These were common in the 1990s as everyday drinking teas.

## Terroir

Yunnan province. Loose-pressed shou from this era represents the everyday end of pu-erh production, teas made to be drunk rather than stored for decades.

## Processing

Wo dui fermented shou pu-erh with light compression. The loose pressing accelerates aging by allowing greater airflow through the cake. By the 1990s, this style was standard for teas intended for near-term consumption rather than long storage.

## Mood

easy comfort

## Experience

Soft and approachable with the rounded sweetness that loose-pressed shou develops faster than its tightly compressed cousins. The longan note adds a quiet fruitiness. A tea that asks nothing and gives enough.
