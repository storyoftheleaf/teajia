A ripe (shou) puerh made from Northern Vietnamese highland leaf, come to us as aged stock from the Guangzhou tea trade. Dark, thick, and mineral in the mouth, with the weight of big mountain leaf.

## Terroir

The highlands of Ha Giang and Lao Cai sit on the same mountain system as southern Yunnan, and the tea trees are the same family: large-leaf, old-growth Camellia sinensis var. assamica, standing wild between roughly 700 and 2,800 metres. Locally these are called Shan Tuyet, snow tea, for the pale down that covers the young buds.

## Processing

It is made the classic shou way: leaves heaped in a warm damp pile so heat and moisture build and microbes slowly work through them, then aged loose. What sets it apart is the leaf it starts from, not the process. Big-leaf highland tea carries more mineral and wood than the plantation leaf most ripe puerh is pressed from.

## Lore

The trade here runs the other way. For generations Hong Kong and Guangzhou houses bought wild Vietnamese border leaf precisely because it was old, big, and a little wild, and piled it into puerh-style tea. This lot is that lineage: a shou puerh from across the border.
