A 1997 shou iron cake (tie bing) with a yellow label. Iron cakes are compressed with hydraulic presses rather than stone molds, producing a much harder, denser cake.

This density slows aging and preserves the tea differently than looser pressings. The yellow label (huang biao) often marks a specific factory batch or grade designation.

## Terroir

Yunnan province. Iron cake production was common across multiple factories in the 1990s. The specific origin varies but the format was popular for its durability and the distinctive aging profile that extreme compression produces.

## Processing

Hydraulic-pressed shou pu-erh. The extreme compression of iron cake format means the interior leaf ages much more slowly than the exterior, creating different flavor zones within a single cake. Nearly thirty years have begun to unify these layers.

## Mood

compressed depth

## Experience

Denser and more concentrated than loose-pressed shou of similar age. The chocolate and wood notes are vivid and the sweetness is deep rather than light. Breaking apart an iron cake releases aged aromas trapped inside for nearly three decades.
