A ripe puerh (shou) from the Yiwu tea area, pressed from old-tree leaf that dates to 2008.

## Lore

Yiwu (易武) is a name that can mean a township, a market centre, or a much wider tea area, so a Yiwu label is a regional claim rather than one village. It grew into a collection and distribution point for tea from the old Six Famous Tea Mountains, linking local leaf with wider merchant networks.

## Terroir

The Yiwu tea area sits in eastern Xishuangbanna, Yunnan, China. Gushu means old-tree: leaf picked from trees far older than the bushes of a plantation. The material in this cake dates to 2008 and had been aging before it was pressed.

## Processing

This is a ripe puerh, called shou in Chinese, and is not a raw tea. Ripe puerh is Yunnan's dark, wet-piled style: leaf is heaped damp in a method called wo dui, or wet piling, so heat and microbes ferment it. The technique was developed in Yunnan in the early 1970s. Wo dui turns the leaf dark and makes it drinkable young, where without piling the same leaf would need years of aging. Because the leaf was already aging before the cake was pressed, this one begins further along than a ripe puerh pressed from fresh leaf. In the cup it pours a dark liquor and drinks full-bodied and earthy, with pile character settled by time.
