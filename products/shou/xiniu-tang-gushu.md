A ripe puerh (shou) from ancient trees at Xiniu Tang, in the Yiwu tea country of Yunnan.

## Lore

Xiniu Tang (犀牛塘), meaning 'Rhinoceros Pond', is a named origin site inside Yiwu, one of the Qing dynasty's Six Famous Tea Mountains. The older Dai name for the wider region translates as 'place where beautiful snakes live', a nod to how deep the forest still runs here.

## Terroir

Yiwu sits in southern Yunnan along the Laos border, with elevations from roughly 910 to 1700 meters and forest cover near 88%. Ancient tea trees, called gushu, grow inside that protected canopy alongside the rest of the forest rather than in cleared rows.

## Processing

A ripe (shou) puerh pressed into a cake from gushu leaf. Ripe puerh is made by wo dui, a wet-piling step developed in the early 1970s where damp leaf is heaped under cloth for weeks so microbes ferment it, turning the tea dark, smooth, and thick in the cup.
