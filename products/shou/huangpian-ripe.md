Huangpian are the older, more mature leaves that get set aside when puerh leaf is sorted. Once treated as lower grade and discarded, the trade came around to them: the same coarse leaves brew a rounder, sweeter, softer body than young leaf. This one is the ripe style, called shou, wet-piled so it is dark and smooth and ready now, not in decades. It comes from Yunnan, the historical home of puerh. This 1990s lot was expertly stored in Guangzhou.

## Lore

Puerh comes from Yunnan in southwest China, and huangpian are the more mature leaves pulled out during sorting. For a long time they were treated as lower grade and even discarded; the trade eventually came around to them for their rounder, sweeter body. The ripe style, shou, was developed in the early 1970s to bring aging forward in time.

## Terroir

Yunnan is the historical heart of puerh, a large mountainous province in southern China. Its highlands have long produced the broad-leaf tea puerh is made from.

## Processing

This is a wet-piled ripe puerh made from huangpian leaf. The wet-piling step builds heat and moisture and lets microbes transform the leaf, turning it dark and smooth so it is ready now rather than in decades.
