Qi Zi Bing Cha (七子饼茶) means 'Seven Sons Cake Tea,' referring to the traditional packaging of seven 357-gram cakes per bamboo tong.

This is the standard format for Yunnan pu-erh and has been for over a century. A 2001 shou cake in this format represents reliable, well-made production-grade ripe pu-erh.

## Terroir

Yunnan province. Qi Zi Bing production draws from tea gardens across the pu-erh growing regions, typically blending leaf from multiple sources to achieve a consistent, balanced profile.

## Processing

Wo dui (wet pile) fermented shou pu-erh compressed into standard 357-gram cake form. Twenty-plus years of post-fermentation aging have mellowed and refined the production character into a smoother, more integrated profile.

## Mood

simple comfort

## Experience

Honest and dependable. It brews dark, tastes clean, and has no rough edges after twenty years. The dried date sweetness is pleasant and the body is smooth. A tea you reach for when you want something reliable.
