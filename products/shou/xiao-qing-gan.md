Xiao Qing Gan (小青柑) is ripe pu-erh stuffed inside small, hollowed-out green mandarin oranges from Xinhui, Guangdong.

The combination of citrus and shou pu-erh is a traditional Cantonese preparation with roots in Chinese herbal medicine. Each piece is a single serving: drop the entire mandarin into your vessel and brew.

## Terroir

The mandarin oranges come from Xinhui district in Jiangmen, Guangdong, the only origin that produces the specific citrus variety prized for this preparation. The pu-erh comes from Yunnan. The two regions collaborate across a thousand kilometers to create this tea.

## Processing

Shou pu-erh is stuffed into hollowed Xinhui mandarin oranges and dried together. The citrus oils permeate the tea during drying and storage, creating a unified flavor. The green (qing) mandarin is harvested young, before it ripens, producing a more vibrant, sharper citrus character than mature fruit.

## Mood

bright warmth

## Experience

Drop one in and watch the water turn dark. The citrus hits first, clean and bright, then the shou earthiness rises to meet it. It is cheerful and warming and utterly approachable. A tea that makes people smile, especially those who think they do not like pu-erh.
