A 2005 wild tea cake from Xiaguan Tea Factory.

Xiaguan is one of Yunnan's oldest and most important tea factories, based in Dali. Known primarily for their tuo cha and iron cakes, Xiaguan's wild tea productions from the mid-2000s sourced leaf from the forests around the Dali-Lincang corridor. The Xiaguan house style favors a tighter compression and a smoke-tinged, assertive character.

## Terroir

The forests of western Yunnan, likely sourced from wild trees in the mountains between Dali and Lincang. Xiaguan's supply chain in this era drew from a wide network of village collectors. The wild designation indicates uncultivated forest trees rather than managed gardens.

## Processing

Xiaguan factory processing — sha qing, rolling, sun-drying, then pressed into cake with the characteristically firm Xiaguan compression. Xiaguan teas are known for starting aggressive and opening up with age. Twenty years have begun that transition but this tea still has substantial energy.

## Mood

factory discipline meets forest chaos

## Experience

More structured than the Da Xue Shan wild — Xiaguan's pressing and processing impose order on wild material. The bitterness is sharp and direct, the mineral note is strong, and the hui gan comes on like a wave. A tea with authority. It will continue to improve for decades.
