Over forty years of quiet storage have turned this raw pu-erh into something that no longer resembles the leaf it once was.

It belongs to a generation of teas that were pressed and forgotten, stored without fanfare in the years before aged sheng became precious. Its value was an accident of patience.

## Terroir

The exact garden origin is lost to time, as was common for teas pressed in this era. What remains is Yunnan's signature: broad-leaf varietal trees, red soil, and subtropical warmth written into every steep.

## Processing

Sun-dried raw pu-erh, stone-pressed into cake. Four decades of natural aging have fully transformed the leaf. The green astringency is long gone, replaced by deep, woody complexity.

## Mood

stillness that speaks

## Experience

It enters slowly. The first steep is quiet, almost shy, dark amber, a whisper of camphor. By the third steep it opens into something expansive: longan sweetness, old wood, a depth that sits in the chest. A tea that teaches you what forty years of silence sounds like.
