Purple-leaf sheng pu-erh from 2016.

Purple tea varieties contain elevated anthocyanins, the same antioxidant pigments found in blueberries and red wine. When processed as sheng pu-erh, these leaves create a tea with a distinctive bitterness and complexity that standard green-leaf varieties do not possess.

## Terroir

Yunnan province. Purple-leaf tea varieties are found scattered among standard populations in old tea forests, particularly in Xishuangbanna and Lincang. They are a natural genetic variant, not a separate species.

## Processing

Sun-dried purple-leaf maocha compressed into cake form. The anthocyanin content affects both flavor and aging trajectory. Ten years of aging have begun to mellow the distinctive bitterness that young purple-leaf sheng carries.

## Mood

wild edge

## Experience

There is a bitterness here that is not like regular sheng bitterness. It has a wild, almost berry-like quality that is unique to purple leaf material. Ten years have softened it but not erased it. A tea for adventurous drinkers.
