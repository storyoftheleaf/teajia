Yesheng (野生) means 'wild growing.

' This is tea from uncultivated trees growing in natural forest conditions without human management. Wild tea carries a different character than garden-grown material: broader, less predictable, shaped by its ecosystem rather than by a farmer's hand.

## Terroir

Yunnan's wild tea forests. Yesheng trees grow in natural forest conditions, their character determined by soil, elevation, surrounding vegetation, and the absence of human intervention. No two wild trees produce identical tea.

## Processing

Minimal processing of wild-harvested leaf. The yesheng designation means these trees were not planted, pruned, or managed. The resulting leaf is irregular and the flavor profile is wider-ranging than cultivated tea.

## Mood

untamed

## Experience

There is a rawness here that cultivated tea does not have. The bitterness is green and direct, the sweetness follows at its own pace, and there is an herbaceous quality that tastes like the forest rather than the garden. A tea for drinkers who want to taste the wild.
