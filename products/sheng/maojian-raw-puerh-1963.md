A 1963 maojian (毛尖, "fur tip") grade raw pu'erh, over sixty years old.

Maojian refers to a picking standard — the tender, downy tips of the tea plant. In pu'erh production, maojian grade represents the finest picking from spring harvests, traditionally reserved for the highest-quality productions. A 1963 maojian sheng predates the Cultural Revolution and comes from an era when Yunnan's tea infrastructure was still relatively undisturbed.

## Terroir

Yunnan province. The specific garden or mountain is unrecorded, but 1963 places this in the pre-Cultural Revolution period when many of Yunnan's old-growth tea gardens were still intact and productive. The maojian grade suggests careful spring picking from quality trees.

## Processing

Sun-dried sheng pu'erh maocha of the finest picking grade, stored loose. Over sixty years of aging have dissolved any varietal distinction into pure aged character. The original tenderness of the maojian tips has been replaced by the deep, woody sweetness that only time produces.

## Mood

pre-revolution quiet

## Experience

Sixty-plus years have smoothed everything into a single, unified expression. There is no bitterness, no astringency — those left decades ago. What remains is old wood, dried herbs, and a sweetness that seems to come from the tea's core rather than its surface. The hui gan is gentle but persistent. A tea from a world that no longer exists.
