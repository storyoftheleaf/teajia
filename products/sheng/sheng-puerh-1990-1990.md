A 1990 raw pu-erh that arrived without provenance or fanfare. The name came later, after the tea proved itself over years of quiet storage.

Thirty-five years have converted what was once anonymous leaf into something worth naming. Some teas earn their identity through patience rather than pedigree.

## Terroir

Yunnan province. The exact garden origin is unrecorded, as was common for teas pressed in this era before single-origin sourcing became standard. What remains is the unmistakable signature of Yunnan large-leaf varietal: thick body, complex sweetness, and a depth that only comes from old material and time.

## Processing

Sun-dried Yunnan maocha, stone-pressed into cake form. Thirty-five years of natural aging have fully transformed the original green astringency. The conversion is complete: what was once bitter and sharp is now sweet and deep.

## Mood

revealed worth

## Experience

It opens slowly and keeps opening. The first steep is camphor and aged wood, quiet and restrained. By the third steep the sweetness arrives, longan and honey, and it stays through a dozen more infusions. A tea that was hidden for a reason and found for a better one.
