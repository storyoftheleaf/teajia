A 2008 wild tea cake from Yiwu, the most storied region in pu'erh history.

Yiwu was the center of the ancient tea horse road and the origin of many of the most celebrated antique pu'erh cakes. Wild tea from Yiwu is gentler than wild material from Lincang or Bulang — the Yiwu character is silk rather than muscle, sweetness rather than bitterness. Even the wild trees here produce a more approachable tea.

## Terroir

Yiwu Township, Mengla County, Xishuangbanna, Yunnan. The forests of Yiwu sit at moderate elevation (1,200-1,600m) with deep, well-drained soil and consistent rainfall. The terroir produces tea with a distinctive softness and honey sweetness that has made Yiwu the most desired origin for many pu'erh collectors.

## Processing

Wild-picked leaf from uncultivated forest trees in the Yiwu area, processed as sheng pu'erh and pressed into cake. Seventeen years of aging have brought this into an approachable middle stage — the raw edge is gone but the tea has decades of transformation ahead.

## Mood

forest gentleness

## Experience

Yiwu softness with a wild edge. The honey sweetness is the foundation, layered with forest floor and a floral bitterness that resolves quickly into lingering sweetness. The body is silky — Yiwu's signature. Compared to the Xiaguan and Da Xue Shan wild cakes, this is the most approachable of the three.
