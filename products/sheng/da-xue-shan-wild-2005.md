A 2005 wild tea cake from Da Xue Shan (Great Snow Mountain) in Lincang.

Da Xue Shan is home to some of the oldest known tea trees on earth, including the famous 2,700-year-old wild tea tree discovered in 1997. Wild tea (野生茶) from this mountain comes from Camellia taliensis or wild Camellia sinensis var. assamica — trees that were never cultivated, growing in the forest canopy at elevations above 2,000 meters. The flavor profile is unlike cultivated pu'erh.

## Terroir

Da Xue Shan, Shuangjiang County, Lincang Prefecture, Yunnan. Elevation 2,000-2,800 meters in dense subtropical forest. The wild trees grow in deep forest shade with no human intervention — no pruning, no fertilizing, no managed picking paths. The soil is ancient forest floor, rich in organic matter from millennia of leaf fall.

## Processing

Wild-picked leaf processed as sheng pu'erh and compressed into standard cake form. Wild tea requires more careful handling than cultivated material — the leaves are often larger and tougher, the flavor more aggressive. Twenty years of aging have begun to tame the wild edge.

## Mood

mountain wilderness

## Experience

This does not taste like garden pu'erh. The bitterness is structural, the floral notes are wild and unrefined, and the body has a muscular quality that cultivated tea rarely achieves. Twenty years have softened it but not domesticated it. The hui gan is powerful and long.
