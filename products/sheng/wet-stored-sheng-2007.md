A 2007 sheng pu-erh that has been intentionally wet-stored (shi cang). Wet storage is a deliberate aging technique where cakes are kept in high-humidity environments to accelerate transformation.

It is controversial among purists but has produced some of the most beloved aged pu-erh in Hong Kong and Southeast Asian tea culture. The results depend entirely on the skill of the storage.

## Terroir

Yunnan province for production. The wet storage environment is as important as the tea origin. Controlled humidity, consistent temperature, and careful monitoring define the difference between excellent wet storage and damage.

## Processing

Sun-dried maocha compressed into cake, then aged in high-humidity conditions. Wet storage accelerates the microbial transformation that normally takes decades in dry conditions. When done well, it produces a smooth, dark, deeply transformed tea in a fraction of the time. When done poorly, it produces mold.

## Mood

earned depth

## Experience

Dark and smooth with an earthiness that dry-stored sheng of the same age cannot match. The mushroom and wet stone notes are signatures of good wet storage. There is no mustiness, no off-flavors, just depth that would normally require another decade of dry aging.
