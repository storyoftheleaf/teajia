A 1980 Red Seal (紅印) pu'erh with camphor storage character.

Red Seal (Hongyin) is one of the most revered designations in pu'erh history, originally referring to cakes produced by the China National Tea Corporation with a red seal on the wrapper. By 1980 the original Red Seal era had passed, but the name persisted for high-grade productions. The camphor character (樟香) indicates storage in proximity to camphor wood — either camphor-lined warehouses or natural camphor trees — a traditional Chinese storage method for valuable pu'erh.

## Terroir

Yunnan province. The specific mountain or factory is unrecorded, but the Red Seal designation was associated with quality factory productions from the state era. The camphor note is a storage characteristic rather than a terroir expression, but forty-five years have made it inseparable from the tea's identity.

## Processing

Sheng pu'erh compressed into standard cake form under state-era factory conditions. The defining secondary processing is the camphor storage — decades of aging in a camphor-influenced environment has imprinted an aromatic, cooling quality into the tea that no amount of re-storage can replicate or remove.

## Mood

aromatic history

## Experience

The camphor announces itself immediately — cool, aromatic, clean. Beneath it, the sheng pu'erh has aged into dark sweetness and plum. The hui gan is deep and sustained. Camphor-stored pu'erh is a specific taste that divides collectors, but for those who love it, nothing else compares. This is a tea that smells like a Chinese antique shop in the best possible way.
