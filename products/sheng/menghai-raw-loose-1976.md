A 1976 raw loose-leaf pu'erh from Menghai, produced during the Cultural Revolution.

Menghai Tea Factory was operating under state control in 1976, and loose-leaf sheng from this era was a byproduct of cake production — the maocha that didn't make it into pressed forms. Fifty years later, loose storage has allowed this tea to age differently from its compressed counterparts: more air contact, faster initial transformation, then a long, slow deepening.

## Terroir

Menghai county, Xishuangbanna, Yunnan. The original terroir of pu'erh production. In 1976, Menghai county's tea gardens were a mix of old arbor trees and newer plantation material, all processed through the state factory system. The tropical latitude, high altitude, and ancient soil produce leaf with a density and complexity that other regions struggle to match.

## Processing

Sun-dried maocha (raw pu'erh material) left in loose form rather than compressed into cakes. State-era processing was functional rather than artisanal. The leaf was picked, withered, sha qing (kill-green), rolled, and sun-dried. What five decades of loose storage have done to it was never part of the plan but is now the entire point.

## Mood

revolutionary quiet

## Experience

The camphor is the first thing you notice — cool and clean, cutting through the aged sweetness. Then the plum, the earth, the old wood. The hui gan (returning sweetness) is long and steady. Loose-stored sheng of this age has a transparency that compressed cakes do not — less concentrated, more open, like reading the same story told more slowly.
