Ruicaokui (瑞草魁) is a rare green tea from Anhui province, one of China's most important green tea regions.

The name translates roughly to 'chief of auspicious grasses,' a designation that speaks to its historical prestige. It is far less known internationally than Anhui's other famous greens like Huangshan Maofeng or Tai Ping Hou Kui, but within Chinese tea culture it carries respect.

## Terroir

Anhui province, eastern China. Anhui's mountainous terrain, with its fog, mineral-rich soil, and moderate climate, produces some of China's most celebrated green teas. The specific terroir that produces Ruicaokui is less documented than its famous neighbors but shares the same essential geography.

## Processing

Traditional Chinese green tea processing: pan-fired (sha qing) to stop oxidation, then shaped and dried. Ruicaokui production follows Anhui green tea conventions with careful temperature control during firing to preserve the delicate, nutty sweetness.

## Experience

Clean and bright with a chestnut sweetness that distinguishes it from grassier Chinese greens. The body is light and the finish is crisp. A morning tea that wakes you up without overwhelming you.
