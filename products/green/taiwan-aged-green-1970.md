A 1970 aged green tea from Taiwan.

Aged green tea is unusual because green tea is typically consumed fresh, and most loses its appeal within a year or two. A green tea that has survived and improved over fifty years is genuinely rare and suggests either deliberate storage or exceptional raw material that proved resilient enough to transform.

## Terroir

Taiwan. Green tea production in Taiwan was more widespread in the mid-20th century than it is today, before the market shifted overwhelmingly toward oolong. The specific origin region is unrecorded.

## Processing

Taiwanese green tea processing from the 1970s, likely following Japanese-influenced methods that were common in Taiwan at the time. Fifty years of aging have completely transformed the original green character into something closer to aged oolong.

## Mood

transformed patience

## Experience

This does not taste like green tea. Fifty years have turned it into something entirely different: warm, sweet, grain-like, with a depth that young green tea cannot imagine. A lesson in what time does to tea when you let it.
