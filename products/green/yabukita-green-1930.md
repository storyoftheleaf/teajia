A 1930 Japanese Yabukita green tea, nearly a century old.

Yabukita is today the dominant cultivar in Japan, covering over 75% of all tea acreage. But in 1930, Sugiyama Hikosaburo's cultivar was still new — registered only in 1953, though selected and propagated from the 1900s onward. A green tea from this era, whether Yabukita or an ancestor cultivar, represents Japanese tea production before mechanization transformed the industry.

## Terroir

Japan. The specific prefecture is unrecorded. Pre-war Japanese green tea was produced across many regions, with Shizuoka, Uji, and Kagoshima already established as major growing areas. The mineral profile and umami depth suggest careful cultivation and shaded or semi-shaded growing conditions.

## Processing

Traditional Japanese green tea processing from the pre-war era. Steam-fixed (mushisei) and hand-rolled, likely in small batches. Ninety-five years have transformed the bright vegetal character of fresh sencha into something dark, sweet, and profoundly umami. The catechins have long since broken down.

## Mood

ancestral stillness

## Experience

Nothing about this resembles fresh green tea. The color has darkened, the vegetal notes have vanished, and what remains is a deep, dark umami sweetness with grain and seaweed notes. It drinks more like an aged oolong than anything recognizably green. A window into a Japan that no longer exists.
