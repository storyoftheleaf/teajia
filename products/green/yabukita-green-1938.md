A 1938 Japanese Yabukita green tea, produced on the eve of the Pacific War.

Eight years younger than the 1930 version, this tea was made in the last full year of peacetime Japanese tea production before wartime austerity transformed the industry. The difference between the two vintages is subtle — both have crossed the threshold where age dominates origin — but the 1938 retains slightly more structure.

## Terroir

Japan. The specific growing region is unrecorded. By 1938, Japanese tea production was well-established but still largely artisanal, with hand-processing common outside the largest factories. The terroir signature has been largely overwritten by nearly nine decades of transformation.

## Processing

Pre-war Japanese steam-fixed green tea. The processing methods of this era were more varied than today's standardized approaches. Nearly ninety years of aging have converted the original character into something entirely new — dark, sweet, and deeply umami.

## Mood

pre-war calm

## Experience

Similar territory to the 1930 but with a touch more presence. The umami is pronounced, the sweetness is dark and grainy, and there is a warmth that green tea is not supposed to have. These pre-war Japanese greens are among the rarest aged teas in any collection.
