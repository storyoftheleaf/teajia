Taiwanese Tie Guan Yin from 1972, predating the island's modern tea boom by decades.

When this tea was made, Taiwanese oolong production was still firmly rooted in traditional methods brought from Fujian. The Muzha district of Taipei was the center of Taiwan's Tie Guan Yin production, and this style carries a heavier roast than its Anxi ancestor.

## Terroir

Muzha district in southern Taipei was established as a tea-growing area by Fujian immigrants who brought Tie Guan Yin cuttings to Taiwan. The local microclimate and slightly different soil composition gave the Taiwan version its own identity, distinct from but honoring its mainland roots.

## Processing

Heavy charcoal roasting in the Muzha tradition. Taiwanese Tie Guan Yin is typically roasted darker than Anxi versions, and this 1972 example has had over fifty years for the roast to mellow into deep, integrated sweetness.

## Experience

Over fifty years have turned this into a meditation tea. The roast is no longer a flavor but a texture, smooth and dark. The sweetness comes late and stays. This is not a tea that announces itself. It waits.
