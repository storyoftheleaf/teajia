Taiwan Raw Tea (台湾生茶) is an older designation for lightly processed Taiwanese oolong.

The 'raw' (sheng/生) descriptor was used before modern marketing terminology took over. A 1980 raw Taiwanese oolong has had over four decades to develop in ways the original maker likely never anticipated.

## Terroir

Taiwan. The 'raw tea' designation was common across multiple growing regions. Without specific origin data, the tea's regional character is expressed through its aged profile rather than identifiable terroir markers.

## Processing

Lightly oxidized Taiwanese oolong from 1980, processed in a style that predates modern gaoshan production methods. Over four decades of aging have transformed what was once a fresh, green-leaning oolong into something much deeper and warmer.

## Experience

It has traveled a long way from whatever it was in 1980. The freshness is gone, replaced by a warm, grain-like sweetness and a woody depth. Not what anyone expected from a 'raw tea.' A reminder that time transforms everything.
