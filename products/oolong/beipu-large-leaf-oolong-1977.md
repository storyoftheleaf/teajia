A 1977 large-leaf oolong from Beipu, Hsinchu County.

Beipu is Hakka tea country. The large-leaf (大葉) designation distinguishes this from the small-leaf cultivars more common in high-mountain Taiwanese oolong. Large-leaf cultivars in Beipu produce a fuller, more robust tea — heavier body, deeper oxidation, and a character that lends itself to aging.

## Terroir

Beipu Township, Hsinchu County, Taiwan. A Hakka settlement at moderate elevation in northwestern Taiwan. The warm, humid conditions and rolling hills produce teas with more body and less of the high-altitude floral character found in central mountain oolongs. Beipu is better known for Oriental Beauty, but its standard oolongs are substantial in their own right.

## Processing

Taiwanese large-leaf oolong with medium to heavy oxidation, likely with a traditional charcoal roast. The 大葉 cultivar produces a bigger, bolder leaf than the qingxin or jinxuan varieties used for gaoshan oolong. Nearly fifty years of aging have deepened and smoothed everything.

## Mood

Hakka warmth

## Experience

Full-bodied and roasted, with a dried fruit sweetness that only decades of aging produce. The large leaf gives it more substance than the delicate high-mountain oolongs. A Hakka tea through and through — unpretentious, substantial, and deeply satisfying.
