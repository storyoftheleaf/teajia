A 1970 dancong from Phoenix Mountain, over fifty years old.

Aged dancong is rare because the style was historically consumed fresh, and few thought to store it long-term. What survives from this era has a quality that modern dancong production cannot recreate: the combination of old-tree leaf, traditional processing, and a half-century of natural transformation.

## Terroir

Phoenix Mountain (Fenghuang Shan), Chaozhou, Guangdong. The old tea trees on this mountain are among the most ancient cultivated tea plants in the world. Some specimens are over 600 years old, and even younger trees benefit from the deep root systems and mineral-rich granite soil.

## Processing

Dancong processing from an era before single-bush naming became standard. The 1970 vintage suggests traditional methods: sun withering, careful oxidation, and charcoal drying. Fifty years of storage have fundamentally altered the original aromatic profile.

## Mood

aged elegance

## Experience

The floral aromatics of young dancong have receded into something deeper and more integrated. What remains is a woody sweetness with a dried fruit quality and a mineral backbone. The fragrance still fills the gaiwan but it whispers instead of sings.
