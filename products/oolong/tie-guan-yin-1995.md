Tie Guan Yin from 1988 is a relic.

Modern production trends toward green and floral, but this tea remembers an older tradition: heavier oxidation, deeper roast, and a patience that rewards decades of aging. Thirty-five years have turned it into something closer to memory than tea.

## Terroir

Anxi county in Fujian province is the ancestral home of Tie Guan Yin. The iron-rich red soil and mountain mist of this region give the cultivar its characteristic mineral backbone and the floral complexity that has made it one of China's most revered oolongs.

## Processing

Traditional heavy-roast Tie Guan Yin, oxidized well beyond today's standards and roasted over charcoal. Decades of aging have mellowed the roast into a smooth, integrated sweetness with no remaining harshness.

## Mood

deep remembrance

## Experience

Dark amber, almost still in the cup. The first sip is all roasted warmth, then a quiet sweetness rises through the chest. By the fifth infusion it has softened into something barely there, like the last note of a song you almost forgot.
