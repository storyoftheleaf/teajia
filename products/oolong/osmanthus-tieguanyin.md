Osmanthus-scented Tie Guan Yin is a traditional Chinese blend where dried gui hua (osmanthus) flowers are layered with oolong during processing.

The practice of scenting tea with flowers has deep roots in Chinese tea culture, and osmanthus brings a particular apricot-peach sweetness that pairs naturally with the roasted character of Tie Guan Yin.

## Terroir

Anxi county, Fujian province for the tea base. Osmanthus flowers are harvested in autumn when their fragrance peaks. The combination is a marriage of two seasonal harvests.

## Processing

Traditional Tie Guan Yin processing with osmanthus flower scenting. The flowers are layered with the finished tea in cycles, allowing the leaf to absorb the fragrance naturally. Excess flowers are typically removed before packaging.

## Mood

sweet nostalgia

## Experience

The osmanthus is gentle, not perfumed. It weaves through the roasted oolong base like a thread of sweetness. The effect is warming and slightly nostalgic. A tea for cooler days.
