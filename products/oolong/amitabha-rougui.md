A Wuyi rougui with a Buddhist name.

Amitabha is the Buddha of Infinite Light, and the naming practice of connecting tea to Buddhist concepts is common in Wuyi, where many tea gardens sit near or within temple grounds. The centuries-long relationship between Wuyi's monasteries and its tea production is one of the deepest in Chinese culture.

## Terroir

Wuyi Mountains, Fujian. Many of Wuyi's finest tea gardens have historical connections to Buddhist monasteries. The monks who tended these gardens for centuries are credited with developing many of the processing techniques that define yancha today.

## Processing

Traditional Wuyi yancha processing with heavy charcoal roast. The Amitabha designation likely reflects the specific garden origin or the maker's intention for the tea's character: a rougui with contemplative depth rather than pure intensity.

## Mood

contemplative fire

## Experience

Warmer and more inward than typical rougui. The cinnamon is present but tempered, with a honeyed sweetness that softens the spice. It has a settling quality unusual for a cultivar known for boldness. A rougui for sitting still.
