A 1990 Meizhan processed as a Wuyi rock tea (yancha).

Rock oolongs are defined by their terroir: the mineral-rich soil between Wuyi's sandstone cliffs creates a flavor profile called 'yan yun' (rock rhyme) that is the most prized quality in Chinese oolong. Over thirty years of aging have amplified this quality.

## Terroir

Wuyi Mountains, Fujian province. The narrow valleys between sandstone and granite cliffs create a unique micro-terroir where mineral content in the soil is extraordinarily high. Tea grown in these 'zhengyan' (true rock) areas carries a depth that cannot be replicated elsewhere.

## Processing

Heavy charcoal roast in the traditional yancha style. The strip-rolled leaf was processed to maximize mineral extraction and then aged for decades. The roast has been fully absorbed into the leaf over thirty years.

## Mood

stone and fire

## Experience

The yan yun is real. There is a mineral quality that sits underneath everything, like drinking from a stone cup. The roast is integrated, the plum note is dark and aged, and the finish has a sweetness that comes from the rock itself.
