A 1983 roasted oolong from Nantou County, four years younger than the 1979 version in the collection.

Nantou is the heart of Taiwanese oolong — Lugu, Dong Ding, and the central mountain ranges all fall within the county. A roasted oolong from 1983 represents the peak of traditional Taiwanese oolong culture, before the market shift toward lighter, unroasted gaoshan styles in the 1990s. This is the old school.

## Terroir

Nantou County, central Taiwan. The broad county encompasses elevations from 200m to over 3,000m. Without a more specific origin, this likely comes from the traditional oolong areas — Lugu township or the Dong Ding mountain region, where roasted oolong was the standard style.

## Processing

Traditional Taiwanese ball-rolled oolong with medium to heavy charcoal roasting. The roasting culture of 1980s Nantou was at its height — skilled roasters working with charcoal over extended sessions to build layers of flavor. Over forty years of aging have mellowed the roast and developed the characteristic dried fruit and caramel notes of aged Taiwanese oolong.

## Mood

old school warmth

## Experience

The roast is still there but has softened into caramel and toasted grain. The dried fruit sweetness is pronounced. Compared to the 1979 version, this is four years younger but follows the same trajectory — traditional Nantou roasted oolong aging gracefully into something warm, sweet, and deeply comforting.
