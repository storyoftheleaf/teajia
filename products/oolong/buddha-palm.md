Fo Shou (Buddha Palm) oolong takes its name from the broad, hand-shaped leaves of the cultivar.

Originally from Yongchun county in Fujian, this variety found a second home in Taiwan where it is processed with a slightly higher oxidation than mainland versions.

## Terroir

The Fo Shou cultivar thrives in subtropical highland conditions. Whether grown in Fujian or Taiwan, it favors humid, mineral-rich soil and moderate elevation where the large leaves develop their characteristic citrus-floral aroma.

## Processing

Medium-oxidized oolong with a moderate roast. The large leaves are rolled tight and open slowly across multiple infusions, releasing new layers with each steep.

## Mood

unhurried afternoon

## Experience

A warm, comforting cup with none of the sharp edges of lighter oolongs. The citrus and grain notes settle in gently and the body is smooth enough to drink all day.
