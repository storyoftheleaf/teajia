Sun-dried red tea from ancient wild trees in Yunnan.

Sun-drying (shai hong) instead of oven-drying is significant: it preserves enzymes in the leaf that allow continued transformation over time, similar to raw pu-erh. This makes sun-dried red tea one of the few red tea styles that can meaningfully age and improve.

## Terroir

Yunnan province, from wild-growing ancient tea trees. These trees grow in mixed forest environments at moderate to high elevation, their roots drawing from deep, mineral-rich soil. Wild trees produce lower yields but more concentrated, complex leaf.

## Processing

Full oxidation followed by sun-drying rather than the standard oven-finish. This preserves active enzymes and gives the tea aging potential unusual for red tea. The wild tree source provides a broader, more complex leaf structure.

## Mood

open horizon

## Experience

Unlike most red teas, this one changes. The first steeping is bright and honeyed, the later steepings develop woody depth. The sun-drying gives it a quality that is hard to name but easy to feel: something alive in the cup.
