Taiwanese high-mountain black tea with a honey fragrance.

This style has emerged as one of Taiwan's most distinctive contributions to red tea, using small-leaf oolong cultivars at elevation to produce a black tea with none of the heaviness typical of lowland production. The honey note comes from careful oxidation and the altitude itself.

## Terroir

High-mountain tea country in Taiwan, likely above 1,000 meters. The same conditions that produce exceptional gaoshan oolong create a distinctive red tea: cool nights, misty mornings, and slow leaf growth that concentrates sweetness and aroma.

## Processing

Full oxidation of high-altitude Taiwanese tea cultivar, processed as red tea. The lighter, more aromatic profile compared to typical black tea comes from using oolong cultivars and high-altitude processing conditions. Light withering preserves delicate aromatics.

## Mood

sunlit morning

## Experience

Golden-red liquor with a honey fragrance that is sweet without being heavy. The body is lighter than most red teas, more like an oolong in texture. Bright, clean, and best in the morning when you want something warm but not weighty.
