A 1977 Assam-cultivar red tea from Yuchi Township, home of Sun Moon Lake.

Taiwan's Assam tea story begins with Japanese colonial-era imports of Indian Assam cultivars to the Yuchi area in the 1920s-30s. The plants adapted to the subtropical lake-basin microclimate and produced a tea distinct from both Indian Assam and traditional Chinese red tea. By 1977, Yuchi's Assam production was mature and well-established.

## Terroir

Yuchi Township, Nantou County, in the basin surrounding Sun Moon Lake (日月潭). The lake moderates temperature extremes while the surrounding mountains trap moisture, creating a consistently warm and humid microclimate. The volcanic-origin soil is rich and well-drained. This specific terroir is what distinguishes Taiwanese Assam from its Indian ancestor.

## Processing

Full-oxidation red tea processing using the Assam (大葉種) cultivar. Taiwanese Assam production follows orthodox methods — withering, rolling, full oxidation, and firing. Nearly fifty years of aging have mellowed the characteristic Assam maltiness into something smoother and more complex.

## Experience

The Assam malt is still there but softened, wrapped in dark honey and dried fruit. The body is full but not heavy. Age has taken the edges off without taking the substance. A red tea that sits comfortably between its Indian heritage and its Taiwanese home.
