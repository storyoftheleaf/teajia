Ripe Puerh Brick (record: ripe pu'er brick, 1990, Yunnan; note: kept in Guangzhou's tea trade)

A brick of [ripe pu’er](shou:definition) pressed in Yunnan in 1990, which spent the decades since in Guangzhou’s tea trade. That puts it in the style’s first two decades: the [wet-pile method](shou:wet-piling) only reached production in 1973.

At thirty-six years the [smell of the pile](shou:age-young) is long gone, and the tea has settled into what the trade counts as [aged ripe pu’er](shou:age-aged). Storage shapes it as much as the years do.



Naka Gushu (record: ripe pu'er cake, Mengsong, Menghai; no year; no notice allowed)

Naka is a Lahu village in the north of [Mengsong](menghai-county:mengsong-naka), close to Huazhu Liangzi, the highest peak in Xishuangbanna. Mengsong lies in the east of Menghai County, across the Liusha River from Nannuo, and Naka is the tea most tied to its name.

This leaf was made into [ripe pu’er](shou:definition): heaped warm and damp in a [fermenting pile](shou:wet-piling), then pressed into a cake, so it drinks dark and soft now rather than after years of waiting.
