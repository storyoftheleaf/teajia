## Who the maker is {#who}

Wuzhou Tea Factory is a state-run Liu Bao factory in the city of Wuzhou, Guangxi, opened in 1953 as a tea works under the national tea company. It has stayed under state or cooperative ownership since, and was reorganised as a company in the 2020s. It is the best known single maker of Liu Bao, and it sells its tea under the San He brand, Three Cranes in English. Its own page covers the factory, not Liu Bao as a whole: farms in Cangwu and other makers in Wuzhou also make Liu Bao.

## A factory made for export {#export-role}

The factory grew up serving buyers abroad. For decades most of its Liu Bao went to Hong Kong, Macau and Southeast Asia, the places where the tea had been drunk since the mining era. As late as 2002 only two firms in Wuzhou made Liu Bao in any quantity, and this factory was one of them, mostly for export. When the first official Liu Bao standard was written in 2003, it was drawn from the practice of these makers, so the factory's way of working is part of how the tea is defined today.

## Reading it on a label {#on-a-label}

A tea marked Wuzhou Tea Factory or San He names the maker. It does not name an age, a leaf source or a grade. It tells a buyer the tea was made in a factory, piled under control and aged in the maker's own warehouses, which is a different route from the farmhouse way. How a given cake or basket tastes still depends on its year and on where it was stored, so the maker is where reading a label starts, not where it ends.
