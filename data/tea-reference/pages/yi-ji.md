## What Yi Ji means {#definition}

Yi Ji means first grade. In Anhua dark tea the raw leaf is sorted by how tender and mature it was when picked, and the sorted lots are numbered. Yi Ji is the first of those numbers, the most tender leaf of the numbered grades, and a higher number means older, coarser leaf.

## What a grade does and does not tell you {#grade-and-quality}

A grade describes the leaf that went in, not how well the tea was made or how it drinks. It says nothing about which hill or garden the leaf came from. Where each grade line falls can differ from one maker to the next, so Yi Ji from two producers is not guaranteed to match.

## A plain word, used in more than one place {#other-meanings}

Yi Ji is only the Chinese for first grade, and the same words turn up on other teas. Each scheme has its own rules, so Yi Ji on a label for another kind of tea does not mean the Anhua raw leaf grade described here. When a tea is sold as Anhua Yi Ji, the Anhua meaning is the one that applies.
