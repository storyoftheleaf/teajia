## A tea rooted in Yunnan {#identity}

Pu’er belongs to Yunnan’s long tea history. The name now gathers a family of teas whose identity is tied not only to manufacture, but also to material, place, and the traditions that developed around them. [cite:ctma-puer-history-2021]

## Trade and continuity {#history}

Tea moved from Yunnan toward Tibet and farther markets along trading networks that are often grouped under the Tea Horse Road. That commerce supported mountain production, market towns, and enduring links between tea-growing communities and distant drinkers. [cite:ctma-puer-history-2021]

## The modern family {#modern-family}

Modern Pu’er includes sheng and shou. Their processing paths differ, but both sit within a twentieth-century industry that restored tea gardens, expanded production, and developed distinct forms for domestic and export markets. [cite:ctma-puer-history-2021]
