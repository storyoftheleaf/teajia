## Who Tongqinghao is {#who}

Tongqinghao is the name of an old pu’er house from Yiwu, in the south of Yunnan, and the trade counts it among the best known names from that mountain. Sellers usually place its start in the Qing dynasty and give 1736 as the year, but that date comes from the brand’s own telling and is not settled. Its name is also written Tong Qing Hao, and Tong Qing in some shops.

## The old house and the name today {#old-and-new}

The house of the Qing and Republic years and the brand sold now are not the same thing. The old house is described as having stopped trading around the middle of the twentieth century, and the name came back in the 2000s as a modern Xishuangbanna company. A tea sold today under the name is a modern product of that revived brand, not a tea of the old house, and its leaf and maker have to be read from its own label.

## Antique cakes and the price of the name {#antique-cakes}

Cakes that survive from the old house, wrapped in its own paper from the early twentieth century, are collector pieces and have sold at auction for some of the highest sums ever paid for pu’er. That price belongs to those few antique cakes. It says nothing about the quality or the value of a modern tea that carries the same name.

## Who holds the name {#who-holds-the-name}

More than one company in Yunnan has used the Tongqinghao name since the planned economy ended, and at least one detailed account says two of them have gone to court over the trademark with rulings that did not agree. This page does not decide who the rightful heir is. When a tea carries the name, the seller or the label is the place to find out which company made it.
