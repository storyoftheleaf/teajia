## What Zhen Pin means {#definition}

Zhen Pin means "precious item" or "treasure". On a Tieguanyin it is a word a seller puts on a tea to say it is a cut above the ordinary, much as English labels say "premium" or "reserve". It is also written Zhenpin and sold in English as Precious or Treasure Grade.

## How Tieguanyin is graded {#how-graded}

Anxi Tieguanyin has a national standard that sorts the tea by how the dry leaf looks and how the liquor drinks: how full and heavy the rolled balls are, how clean the colour, how long the fragrance lasts, and how clear and sweet the taste is. The standard grades the light-fragrance and strong-fragrance styles separately. It names its grades special grade (tejí), then first, second and third grade, with a fourth grade for the strong-fragrance style. Zhen Pin is not one of the grade names in it.

## Where Zhen Pin sits {#where-it-sits}

Where Zhen Pin falls between special grade and first grade is not established. It is a seller's word, so it is not fixed by the standard, and no firm ranking of it against the standard grades could be found. Read it as the seller's signal of a better lot, and compare the style, the harvest and the price rather than the word.

## Grade names vary by seller {#names-vary}

Most Tieguanyin on sale carries grade words that the seller chose: special grade, first grade, premium, superior, or a name such as Zhen Pin. Two brands can print the same word on teas of different quality, and one brand's top tea can be named differently from another's. A grade word on a pack is a guide to what that seller considers its better lots, not a measure shared across the trade.
