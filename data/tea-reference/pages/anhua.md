## Where Anhua is {#where-it-is}

Anhua is a mountain county in north-central Hunan, in the city of Yiyang. It lies on the middle stretch of the Zi river, at the northern foot of the Xuefeng mountains, and is the southernmost and westernmost county of Yiyang. Most of the land is hills and steep valleys, with streams running down to the river. It is a place where rice is hard to grow and tea does well.

## Why people value Anhua {#why-people-value-it}

People value Anhua first as the home of Chinese dark tea, and then for the ground the tea grows in. The county is warm and very wet, with four clear seasons, a short cold spell, and cloud and mist on the high mountains such as Yuntai. The slopes on both sides of the river are deep, loose, slightly acidic soil weathered from shale, which drains well. A large share of the county is forest, and the tea sits among it, not on cleared plains. The local large-leaf tea, best known from Yuntai Mountain, has thick, soft leaf that suits dark tea making. In Anhua the same wet, warm air that helps the tea grow is also the air the tea is piled, pressed and aged in, which is why the trade ties the character of the finished tea to the place and not only to the method.

## How Anhua became a tea county {#history}

Anhua was set up as a county in the Northern Song, after the Meishan hill country was opened to the outside world, and tea has been recorded in the area since the Tang. The trade grew under the Ming and Qing, when Anhua tea went north and north-west by road and river to the north-west and on to Russia. Old stone paths and merchant towns such as Tangjiaguan, a stop on the long tea road, still show where that trade passed. For the tea itself, how it was made and what it became, see the Anhua dark tea page.

## Tea in the county today {#tea-today}

Tea is the main industry of Anhua. Tea gardens cover a large part of the hills, more than two hundred firms process the leaf, and hundreds of thousands of people in and around the county make a living from tea in some way. Visitors come to the tea museum, the old tea roads and the tea farms on Yuntai Mountain, so the place is also a destination in its own right.
