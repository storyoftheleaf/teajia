## A name at several scales {#scope}

Yiwu can mean a township, a market center, or a much broader tea area in eastern Xishuangbanna. In trade, the name often reaches beyond the town itself, so a Yiwu label should be read as a regional claim whose scale still needs context. [cite:teadb-greater-yiwu]

## Shared reputation, varied tea {#common-character}

Greater Yiwu is often associated with a softer opening and a persistent sweet return when compared with the more forceful profiles linked to western Xishuangbanna. The area contains many growing places, however, and their material can vary substantially; this is a regional tendency, not a tasting promise for an individual tea. [cite:teadb-greater-yiwu]

## Trade history {#history}

Yiwu became an important collection and distribution point for tea from the old Six Famous Tea Mountains. Its trade connected local leaf, merchant networks, tribute routes, and later some of the best-known early twentieth-century tea firms. [cite:teadb-greater-yiwu]

## Where Yiwu is {#where-it-is}

Yiwu is in the north of Mengla County, the eastern county of Xishuangbanna in southern Yunnan, on the east side of the Lancang River (the upper Mekong). It is one of the old Six Famous Tea Mountains, and the town of Yiwu has long been where tea from the surrounding villages is gathered and processed. Mahei, Mansa and Luoshuidong are among the villages around it where old trees still grow.

## What Yiwu is known for {#known-for}

Yiwu is known for raw pu’er that drinks soft and sweet rather than forceful. The trade describes a smooth, thick liquor with little bitterness, a floral and honeyed fragrance, and a sweet aftertaste that lasts through many infusions. People like it because it is easy to drink young and is said to hold up well with age. It is the usual contrast to the heavier teas of Menghai and Bulang. This is a regional tendency. The area is large, and the tea from one village can taste quite different from another.

## Yiwu on a label {#on-a-label}

Yiwu on a label is a market name, and it is not proof of where the leaf grew. The word is used for the town, for the mountain and for a wider area, and sellers also use it for tea made with Yiwu leaf or in a Yiwu style. Some cakes sold under the name blend in leaf from elsewhere. The clearest labels name the village or plot as well as Yiwu, and a seller who can tie a tea to a village, a harvest and a maker is giving you something you can check.
