## Where Guangxi is {#where-it-is}

Guangxi is a province-level region in the south of China, officially the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, and it borders Guangdong on its eastern side. Its tea is not one thing. Two very different teas carry its name: Liu Bao, an aged dark tea from the east, and jasmine tea from Hengzhou.

## How Wuzhou and Cangwu sit inside it {#wuzhou-and-cangwu}

Wuzhou is the city in eastern Guangxi that anchors the Liu Bao trade, and Cangwu is a county under it. Liubao town, which gave the tea its name, lies in Cangwu. When a label says Wuzhou it names the wider tea area and the city's factories, and when it says Cangwu or Liubao it points to the original home of the tea. Making has since spread to other counties around Wuzhou, so the three names are nested rather than interchangeable.

## What the land gives the tea {#land-and-climate}

Wuzhou sits near the Tropic of Cancer, with a warm, wet, subtropical monsoon climate, plenty of rain, and hills and forest all around. People in the trade link this damp, steady warmth to the way Liu Bao ages slowly in cool storehouses, because the microbes that shape a dark tea like it. The soils are acidic, and the trade sees the setting as part of why Liu Bao is made here and not simply anywhere.

## What Guangxi is known for in dark tea {#known-for-liu-bao}

Guangxi is known above all for Liu Bao, a dark tea the trade values for how it ages. It matters to buyers as the one famous dark tea of the south, made for the hot, humid life of Chinese workers in Southeast Asia, and Wuzhou has grown it into a large industry with its own protected name. The style, its making and its aged stock are on the Liu Bao page, so this page does not repeat them.

## Hengzhou jasmine tea {#hengzhou-jasmine}

The other tea Guangxi is known for is jasmine tea from Hengzhou, a county-level city. Hengzhou is described as the largest jasmine growing area in China, and a very large part of the country's jasmine flowers are picked there, which is why so much scented tea starts from its flowers. This is a scented tea of a different kind from Liu Bao, and the two share nothing but the province.

## Guangxi on a label {#on-a-label}

Guangxi on a label names a region, not a style. A Guangxi dark tea is most often Liu Bao, but a tea from Guangxi can also be jasmine, and a tea made like Liu Bao elsewhere is not from Guangxi. The finer names, Wuzhou, Cangwu and Liubao, tell you more than the province does.
