## Where Alishan is {#where-it-is}

Alishan is a mountain range and tea area in Chiayi County, in the southwest of central Taiwan. It is one of the three best known high mountain tea areas on the island, with Lishan and Shanlinxi. Its gardens sit on the western and southern slopes, mostly at about 1,000 to 1,600 metres, though places quote the range differently, from about 700 up to 1,800 metres. On a tea record, Alishan names the mountain area. The county is Chiayi, and some records file Alishan under Nantou, which is not where it is.

## Why people value Alishan tea {#why-people-value-it}

Alishan matters to a buyer for its air and its approachability. Cloud and fog cover the gardens for much of the day, the sun is filtered, and nights are cool, so the leaf grows slowly and comes out thick and soft with little bitterness. The trade explains the cup this way: sweet, smooth and light in astringency, with a floral aroma that lasts through many infusions. Most Alishan tea is lightly oxidised, ball-rolled oolong with little or no roast. It is often the first high mountain oolong people try, and the style most buyers picture when they say high mountain tea.

## The cultivars grown {#cultivars}

Qingxin oolong is the main cultivar, and it is the one people most often mean by classic Alishan, with orchid-like or osmanthus-like fragrance and lasting sweetness. Jin Xuan, the milky cultivar also sold as TTES No. 12, is grown too, mostly on the lower and middle ground. Which cultivar a tea is, and how high it grew, changes the cup more than the name Alishan does.

## Townships and sub-areas {#sub-areas}

The tea area is wider than Alishan Township. Tea grows in three townships. Meishan, on the northern edge, has lower and middle gardens in villages such as Taiping, Ruili and Taixing. Zhuqi is the centre, with Shizhuo and Fenqihu. Alishan Township itself holds the higher gardens along the mountain road. Named sub-areas also carry weight with sellers: Shizhuo, Zhangshuhu and Ruifeng are among those most often named, and garden height varies a lot even within one village.

## Alishan on a label {#on-a-label}

Alishan on a label is a market name that covers a lot of ground, and it does not say which township, garden or height the tea came from. Tea from outside Taiwan, including high-altitude oolong from other countries, is sometimes sold as Alishan, so a bare name with no garden, height or cultivar is the weaker claim. Fenqihu is a related case: it is a tourist town with only a few tea gardens, and much tea sold under its name comes from nearby areas such as Shizhuo. A named village, a stated height and a cultivar make the fuller claim.
