## What the company is {#what-it-is}

China National Tea Corporation, known in pu’er circles as Zhong Cha, CNNP or China Tea, began in 1949 as the state’s national tea trading company, set up soon after the People’s Republic was founded. It organised tea production, export and sales across the country, and in Yunnan it stood above the big state factories. The name has changed with the state’s trade bodies over the years, and the sources do not agree on every step, so this page does not try to give one clean chain of names.

## The Zhong Cha mark {#zhong-cha-mark}

The Zhong Cha trademark is a green character for tea, cha, ringed by eight red characters for zhong, which is why the trade also calls it the “eight zhong” mark. It dates from the early 1950s. Sources give 1951 for the trademark and 1952 for the finished look of the logo, so the exact year is not settled. It is one of the oldest tea marks in China and is the sign most people mean when they say a pu’er is “Zhong Cha”.

## Many factories, one mark {#many-factories}

Through the state era, pu’er from several Yunnan factories reached the market under the Zhong Cha mark, chiefly Kunming, Menghai and Xiaguan. The company was the trader and brand owner, and the factories made the tea. After the factories moved to their own brands in the 1980s and 1990s, Zhong Cha was also put on tea from smaller and newer makers. For that reason the mark belongs to the company, not to one factory or one style of tea.

## Reading the mark on a tea {#mark-and-tea}

A Zhong Cha label says who sold the tea. It does not say which factory made it, which leaf went in, or how good it is, and teas under the mark vary a great deal from one era and one maker to the next. A factory’s history is not a fact about every tea under this name. What a given cake is, and what its own designation such as Red Seal or Blue Seal Green Label means, belongs on that tea’s own page, not here.

## Where the company stands now {#today}

The company is now part of COFCO, the state grain and food group, and it still sells tea, pu’er among it, under the Zhong Cha name. The sources differ on when it became a COFCO subsidiary, with dates in the mid 2000s given, and one account says the brand was briefly set aside and then brought back. That brief gap is reported by only a few places, so it is not stated as settled.
