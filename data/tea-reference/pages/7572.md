## The recipe ripe pu’er is measured against {#definition}

7572 is a recipe mark, a four-digit number that names one blend of ripe pu’er first made by Menghai Tea Factory in the mid 1970s. It is the best known ripe recipe in the trade, and other ripe cakes are often described by how they compare with it. Buyers look for it because it stands for a plain, reliable idea of what ripe pu’er should be.

## How to read the four digits {#reading-the-number}

The digits are a code. The first two, 75, stand for 1975, the year the recipe was first made. The third, 7, is the leaf grade that makes up most of the blend, on a scale where 1 is the finest bud and 9 the coarsest leaf. The last, 2, is the factory code for Menghai. The same layout is used on other numbered recipes, which is why a ripe cake and its sheng companion can share the first two digits.

## What the number does not tell you {#what-it-does-not-say}

The 75 is the year of the recipe, not the year of the cake. A 7572 pressed in 2010 and one pressed in 2023 carry the same number, so the year of pressing has to come from the wrapper, the batch record or the seller. The number also does not say how old the tea is, how it was stored, or whether it is a good example of the recipe. Young cakes can still taste of piling, while well stored older ones have settled and softened, so two cakes with the same number can drink quite differently.

## Recipe, not grade of quality {#recipe-not-quality}

Although the number carries a leaf grade, 7572 is not a quality grade like Gong Ting. It names a whole blend, and the 7 describes the main body of the leaf, not how fine or valuable the tea is. A finer ripe tea is not a better 7572, and a cake sold as 7572 shows the factory’s recipe, not a mark of rank.
