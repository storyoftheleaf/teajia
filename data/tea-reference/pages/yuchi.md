## Where Yuchi is {#where-it-is}

Yuchi is a township in the middle of Nantou County, in central Taiwan, and it holds Sun Moon Lake. It is a basin of gentle hills around the lake, and its tea is grown mostly on slopes at roughly 600 to 800 metres. Tea from here is often sold under the name Sun Moon Lake, which is the better known name for the same ground.

## Why red tea grew here {#why-red-tea}

Taiwan is better known for oolong, so red tea in Yuchi needs a reason, and the reason is the land. The basin is warm and wet, with a lot of mist and cloud, deep reddish soil, and a lake that keeps the weather mild and steady. The trade, and the Japanese tea men who first trialled it, compared this to the Assam country of India, where red tea is made from the large leaf variety. That is why Yuchi became Taiwan's red tea place and not only a place that happens to make some.

## The Assam planting {#assam-planting}

Under Japanese rule, the colonial government wanted red tea for export and brought in large leaf Assam tea seeds from India. Trial plantings went in at several sites, and Yuchi was the one that did well. Sources give different years for the seeds and the first plantings, so no single date is safe to quote. The Assam type is the root of Yuchi red tea, and plantings of that type are still called Assam red tea here.

## The research station {#research-station}

A tea research station was set up at Yuchi to work on red tea, and the station still stands, now part of Taiwan's national tea research service (the Tea Research and Extension Station). Its work after the war gave Yuchi its named cultivars, among them the Assam type Taicha 8 and the later Taicha 18, called Ruby (Hong Yu), a cross of a Taiwanese wild tea with a Burmese large leaf red tea. The cultivar is a separate fact from the place: Yuchi makes red tea from more than one of them.

## Decline and return {#decline-and-return}

Red tea from Yuchi was an export crop and it faded when it could no longer compete. From the 1970s rising wages made hand picked leaf expensive against red tea from other countries, and many growers moved to oolong or to other crops. The 1999 earthquake hit the township hard, and rebuilding turned attention back to local red tea. Since then a younger generation has returned to grow and make it, and Yuchi is again the first place most people name for Taiwanese red tea.
