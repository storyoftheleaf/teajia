## Where the Wuyi Mountains are {#where-it-is}

The Wuyi Mountains run along the border of northern Fujian and Jiangxi in south-east China. The tea town is Wuyishan City, in Fujian. The range is a UNESCO World Heritage site, listed in 1999, and much of it is protected forest and nature reserve. Tea grows in several different parts of it, from the river valleys to the high northern edge, and the parts make different teas.

## Why tea grows well here {#nature}

Wuyi is steep, wet and misty. The mountains hold in warm air from the sea, so rain is heavy and fog is common through the year. In the eastern part, red sandstone peaks rise out of the Nine-Bend River gorge as cliffs with flat tops. The west is mostly volcanic rock and granite. Tea bushes grow in the thin, stony soil of the ravines, on ledges and between rocks, where the ground drains fast and mist settles on the slopes. This is the reason people give for the mineral, stony character they look for in Wuyi tea, and for the slow growth of the bushes.

## The core, the edge and the river banks {#zones}

The trade sorts Wuyi tea by how stony the ground is and how close it is to the scenic core. Zheng yan, true rock, is the core of about 70 square kilometres, with steep rocky ground. Its best-known part is the three pits and two streams, among them Niulan Keng and Huiyuan Keng. Ban yan, half rock, is the ring around the core, with less rock in the soil. Zhou cha is tea from the flat, sandy ground along the river. Wai shan, outer mountain, is tea from farther away still. Most buyers and growers say the stony character fades with distance from the core, though the zones are a trade habit and not a guarantee of any one cup. The shop's page on Wuyi rock tea covers how these teas are made and judged.

## Two kinds of tea, two parts of the mountain {#two-teas}

Wuyi is known for two very different teas, and they come from different ground. Rock oolong, such as Da Hong Pao, Rougui and Shui Xian, comes from the rocky valleys and cliffs lower down. Lapsang Souchong, the original smoked black tea, comes from the high northern edge of the range around Tongmu village, close to the Jiangxi border, in forested country that is also protected. Tongmu is widely called the birthplace of Chinese black tea. Both are sold as Wuyi tea, so a Wuyi label does not tell you which kind you have. The pages on rock tea and Lapsang cover each.

## Wuyi on a label {#on-a-label}

Wuyi is a famous name, and it is used on a wide range of tea. The strict meaning of Wuyi rock tea is tea grown inside the Wuyi scenic area from the local cultivars and made by the Wuyi method. Tea from the outer mountain, the river banks or other parts of Fujian can still be sold with a Wuyi style name, and the name Da Hong Pao in particular is used loosely. A label that says only Wuyi or Wuyi Mountains, without naming a zone or a pit, does not say how close to the core the tea grew. Ask for the zone and the cultivar when you want to know.
