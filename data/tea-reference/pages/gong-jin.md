## A selling name, not a grade {#definition}

Gong Jin is a name that appears on ripe pu’er bricks, not a leaf grade. The trade grades ripe tea by leaf size and bud content, with names such as Gong Ting and special grade, and Gong Jin is not one of those steps. The word reads as “tribute gold”, and its job is to make a brick sound fine, not to say what is in it.

## Where the word turns up {#where-seen}

It is found in product names on pressed ripe tea from Menghai makers, written Gong Jin, Ruigong or, on some packs, as part of a longer name such as “gold brick”. The Nanqiao Ruigong Brick is one example. Each time it sits in the title beside the maker or the year, and nothing on the listing explains it as a grade or sets out what leaf it stands for.

## Do not read it as Gong Ting {#not-gong-ting}

Gong Jin and Gong Ting look alike but are different words. Gong Ting is the real grade name for fine, bud-rich ripe leaf. Gong Jin carries no promise about the leaf. The word “gong” (tribute) is borrowed for its old imperial ring, and the tea has no known link to any court.

## What is not established {#not-established}

No agreed meaning, start date or maker for the name has been found. Whether any one maker uses it for a particular blend or leaf grade is not established, so a Gong Jin brick has to be judged on its recorded leaf, year and keeping, not on the name. The same characters are also used as a name on some unrelated dark teas, which are a different tea.
