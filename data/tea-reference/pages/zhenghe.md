## Where Zhenghe is {#where-it-is}

Zhenghe is a county in the city of Nanping, in the north of Fujian. It is an inland mountain county, set back from the coast and covered in forest, with mist, cooler air and wider swings between day and night than the coastal tea hills. The trade points to that mountain setting when it explains why Zhenghe tea tastes the way it does. Altitude is quoted differently from place to place, so no single figure is given here. Tieshan is the township most often named in its tea story.

## What Zhenghe is known for {#known-for}

Zhenghe is one of the two main homes of white tea, with Fuding. It grows its own bush, Zhenghe Da Bai, a large-leaf variety with plump, downy buds. It buds later than the Fuding bushes and is usually described as the darker, taller-growing one. It is used for Silver Needle and White Peony, and it also makes black tea and a scented green tea. The bush is tied to Tieshan, where it is said to have been found in the late nineteenth century, and it is now planted well beyond the county.

## Zhenghe and Fuding {#zhenghe-and-fuding}

The trade sets Zhenghe against Fuding. Fuding is called the lighter, fresher, more floral style, and Zhenghe the fuller, rounder and mellower one, with a thicker liquor. Because Zhenghe leaf comes later in a rainy season, much of it is withered indoors, and some makers pile it longer, which adds to the deeper cup. This is the trade's comparison of two places and not a rule. Gardens, picking, processing and storage change a cup more than the county name does, and a few sellers reject the contrast altogether.

## Zhenghe Gongfu {#zhenghe-gongfu}

Zhenghe also makes black tea. Zhenghe Gongfu is a Fujian gongfu black tea named for the county, and it is made from the same Da Bai bush as the white tea. It is counted among the classic Fujian black teas, alongside Bailin and Tanyang. It is described as a full, smooth, mellow cup with a sweet finish, and some sellers call it winey. The date it began and how widely it was exported are told differently from place to place and are not settled here.

## How Zhenghe's tea story is told {#history}

Zhenghe is an old tea county, and its name is part of the story. The usual telling says the emperor of the Song period renamed it after his reign title because he liked its tea. The tale is widely repeated, but it is copied from one page to the next and not checked against a record, so it is a story and not a fact on this page. What is better supported is the later one: the Da Bai bush was found and spread from Tieshan in the late nineteenth century, and Zhenghe white tea and black tea were both made for sale from about then.
