## Where Wuzhou is {#where-it-is}

Wuzhou is a city in the east of Guangxi, right on the border with Guangdong. It stands where the Xi and Gui rivers meet, and the stretch of river below the meeting is the road down to Guangzhou. The city has been a river town for more than two thousand years, and that position, not its own tea fields, is the reason it matters in tea.

## The river port {#river-port}

Wuzhou is the gate between Guangxi and the coast. Goods from the hills of eastern Guangxi came down the rivers to its port and went on by water to Guangzhou, Hong Kong and the sea. It was opened to foreign trade as a treaty port in 1897, and it is still described as the main river port of Guangxi. For tea this meant a simple thing: leaf made in the hills had to reach Wuzhou to reach the world.

## Wuzhou and Liu Bao {#liu-bao-trade}

Liu Bao is named for the town of Liubao, which lies in Cangwu County under Wuzhou, so the tea is grown and first made in the countryside around the city and not in the city itself. Wuzhou was where it was gathered, finished and sent on. Tea came down from the hills by small boat and raft to the river, then by larger boat to Guangzhou, and from there by sea to the Chinese communities of Malaya and Southeast Asia. The trade still calls this the old tea-boat road. This is why Liu Bao is commonly called a Wuzhou tea even though Liubao town is the source of its name.

## How the trade fell and came back {#history}

Liu Bao sales were strong in the 1930s, then fell sharply after war reached the south in 1937 and the sea trade was cut. Later the planned economy and farmers leaving tea for other crops pushed output very low, and by the end of the 1980s very little was being made. Since the 1990s, and above all in the last fifteen years, Wuzhou has rebuilt the tea as a large local industry, with the city government backing it, new plantings, and a regional brand. Figures for output differ from one report to the next, so the size of the industry today is not settled here.

## Wuzhou on a label {#on-a-label}

Wuzhou on a label names a city and its tea area, not a single maker. A Liu Bao sold as Wuzhou can come from a farm in Cangwu, from a factory in the city, or from one of the other counties around it, and the word alone does not say which. Liubao or Cangwu is the narrower claim and points to the original home of the tea. The Wuzhou Tea Factory is one maker in the city and has its own page, so its history is not the history of every Wuzhou tea.
