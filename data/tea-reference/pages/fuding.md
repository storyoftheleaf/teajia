## Where Fuding is {#where-it-is}

Fuding is a county-level city in the north-east corner of Fujian, on the coast and against the Zhejiang border. Mountains close it in on three sides and the East China Sea lies to the east. It is hilly almost everywhere, and Taimu Mountain, a scenic and geopark area, is its best known peak.

## Why people value Fuding {#why-it-matters}

Fuding is where mountain and sea meet, and that is the reason most often given for its tea. Hills and mountains cover most of the land and forest covers well over half of it, so the gardens sit on terraced mid-slopes with forest above them. The climate is mild, humid and often misty, with sea air moving up the valleys, and the soil is acidic red and yellow earth. The trade ties this to the plump, downy buds that white tea needs, and to a tea that is usually described as fresh, sweet and floral. Many gardens are also grown with other plants and animals around the bushes, which the region now presents as a farming heritage in its own right.

## What Fuding is known for {#known-for}

Fuding is known as the home of white tea. Fuding, Zhenghe and Jianyang are the three places in Fujian that make it, and Fuding is the one most often named as its origin. Its way of making white tea, a long natural wither without any frying or rolling, is listed as national cultural heritage in China. The leaf comes mostly from two local bushes, Fuding Da Bai and Fuding Da Hao, which together make up nearly all the tea grown there. What the types and grades of white tea are, and how Fuding and Zhenghe compare, is covered on the white tea pages.

## Taimu, Bailin and the other districts {#inside-fuding}

Taimu Mountain and Bailin are the two places inside Fuding that the trade names most. Taimu is tied to the first famous bud tea and to the mother tree of the Fuding bush. Bailin is tied to an early bud tea of its own, known as white down tea. Diantou, Panxi and Guanyang are also named as main growing districts. Sellers say tea from higher and cooler ground is lighter and cleaner, and that each district differs in scent, body and sweetness, but too few places agree on how to tell them apart to treat any of that as settled. The year and the maker change the cup as much as the district does.

## Fuding on a label {#on-a-label}

Fuding white tea is a protected origin name in China, recognised since 2009. On a label it means that the leaf grew and was made inside Fuding, which is a claim about place only. It does not say which grade the tea is, which year it was picked or how long it has aged. Taimu and Bailin are narrower names inside it, and a label rarely proves which of them is meant.
