## Where Ya'an is {#where-it-is}

Ya'an is a prefecture-level city in western Sichuan, on the western edge of the Sichuan Basin where the land climbs towards the Tibetan plateau. Mountains close it in, and four rivers run through it. It sits on the old road from the basin to Kangding and Tibet, which is why its tea went west. Mengding Shan, the best known tea mountain in Sichuan, is in Mingshan, in the east of the city's area.

## Why tea grows well in Ya'an {#climate}

Ya'an is nicknamed the Rain City. Rain falls on most days of the year, the air stays damp and mist hangs on the hills, and the winters are mild because the mountains to the north shield it from cold air. Tea likes that: steady moisture, cloud cover and no hard frost. The hills around Mengding Shan are also heavily forested, and the soils are acidic yellow and red earths, which suit the tea bush.

## The two teas Ya'an is known for {#known-for}

Ya'an is known for two quite different kinds of tea. One is the green tea of Mengding Shan, made from young spring leaf and sold as a fine tea. The other is dark tea for Tibet, made from mature leaf and pressed into bricks. The city's tea trade today carries both names, Mengding Shan tea and Ya'an Tibetan tea. They share a place but not a market, a leaf grade or a way of drinking.

## Mengding Shan {#mengding-shan}

Mengding Shan is the mountain behind most of the fine tea of Ya'an. It rises to about 1,450 metres, and the tea gardens climb its slopes. Its best known teas are green: Mengding Ganlu, a small curled leaf, and Mengding Mao Feng and Shihua. It also makes a famous yellow tea, Mengding Huangya. The mountain's name is protected as a geographical indication, so Mengding Shan on a label should mean tea from this area. Tea tradition ties it to Wu Lizhen, said to have planted the first tea bushes here more than two thousand years ago. That is a story the trade tells with pride, and it is not proven history.

## Ya'an and the road to Tibet {#tibet-trade}

For about a thousand years Ya'an was where tea for Tibet began its journey. Porters carried the bricks on foot from Ya'an to Kangding, and from there the tea went on to Lhasa and other Tibetan towns. In return Tibet sent horses. This is the reason Ya'an is a dark tea place as well as a green tea place. The making of that tea, the bricks and how they are drunk are on the page for Sichuan border tea.
