## Why the tea is called “returned” {#returned}

Huiliu means “returned.” On a Malaysian Returned Liu Bao label, Malaysia names a stop in the life of the tea. Liu Bao made in Guangxi travelled overseas, and older tea later found buyers back in China. [cite:teajia-malaysian-returned] [cite:cns-malaysia-liubao-trade]

## The river route to Malaya {#river-trade}

A trade account published in 1951 follows Liu Bao downstream. At Libu the tea changed to a larger wooden boat for Fengkai, then to a powered vessel for Guangzhou. The account names Ipoh and Kuala Lumpur among its final markets. [cite:guangxi-archives-liubao]

## How older tea found its way back {#tea-came-back}

On a visit to Wuzhou in 2024, Malaysian trade representative Lin Hengyi saw a 1973 import order linking a Malaysian firm and a Wuzhou tea company. His family still kept tea from the 1970s. During the visit, merchants asked him about bringing older Liu Bao back to the Chinese market. [cite:cns-malaysia-liubao-trade]
