## Names in the tea market {#market-names}

In Yunnan pu’er, the name of the mountain or village is a large part of what you pay for. Buyers treat a place like Banzhang, Bingdao or Mansong as a promise of old trees and a recognisable character, and because real supply from these places is small, their leaf sells at many times the price of tea from the wider region. In spring 2026 an industry event published a purchase-price index covering 108 named origins, and it put names such as Bohetang, Banzhang, Bingdao, Mansong, Fenghuangwo, Xigui, Jingmai, Yibang and Manzhuan on the list. [cite:ctma-yunnan-108-mountains-2026]

## What a list can tell us {#reading-the-list}

A famous mountain name on a price list or a cake wrapper shows where the market’s attention is. It does not prove the leaf grew there: the same names are also put on blends and plantation tea, so ask who sourced the leaf and how. Even inside one real mountain, prices differ from village to village, and again by garden, tree age and how strictly the leaf was picked. Read a mountain name as a starting point for questions, not as a guarantee.
