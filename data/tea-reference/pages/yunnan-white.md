## What Yunnan white is {#definition}

Yunnan white is white tea made from Yunnan’s large-leaf tea plants, rather than from the small and medium-leaf bush cultivars of Fujian. It is mostly picked in spring, as buds or a bud with one to three leaves, and its buds are typically large and silver-haired. Unlike Fuding white tea it is mostly not sold by grade.

## How Yunnan white is made {#making}

Yunnan white follows the white tea way: the leaf is withered and then dried, never heated to stop its change and never rolled. The withering is done indoors or in shade rather than in hot midday sun, and the leaf is then dried by air, without the final low bake that Fujian white tea usually gets. A longer or darker wither gives a darker, redder cup. It is sold loose and pressed, in the cake, coin and brick shapes of pu’er.

## Moonlight White {#moonlight-white}

Moonlight White, yue guang bai, is the best-known Yunnan white. Its home is Jinggu County in Pu’er City, and the Jinggu tea is made from the Jinggu Da Bai Cha variety, whose mother tree grows in Yangta village; other Yunnan makers use the name too. Two explanations are given for the name: the look of the leaf, dark on top and silver beneath, like the moon in a dark sky, and a story that it was picked or withered by moonlight.

## Yabao {#yabao}

Yabao is made from the buds of wild-growing or old trees in south-west Yunnan, picked in late winter or early spring before they open and sun-dried with little else done to them. It is mostly sold as white tea. Which plant the buds come from is not settled: some are said to be from a wild tea species, not the cultivated tea plant.

## How Yunnan white began {#history}

Yunnan white is a recent tea in the market. The name Moonlight White and the trade in Yunnan white tea have grown popular only in recent years, though local practice may be older.

## Keeping Yunnan white {#ageing}

Yunnan white is pressed and kept like Fujian white, and some keep it for years, but there is no agreed view of how long it should be kept or how it compares with aged Fujian white; some say it is best within a few years.

## Brewing Yunnan white {#brewing}

Yunnan white is brewed gently, as a white tea is, in a gaiwan or small pot, and it gives many infusions; the leaf holds up well.

## What people often notice {#common-impressions}

A honeyed, sweet cup, often richer and fuller than Fujian white, with little bitterness, sometimes fruit like apricot or melon and a hay or herbal note. Yabao is sweet and silky, often with pine or herbal notes.
