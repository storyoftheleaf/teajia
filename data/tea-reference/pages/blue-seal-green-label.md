## What the name means {#definition}

Blue Seal Green Label is a collector's name for raw pu’er cakes sold under the China National Tea Corporation mark in the mid twentieth century. It belongs to the family of colour-named designations, Red Seal, Blue Seal, Green Seal and Yellow Seal, which the trade uses to tell old Zhong Cha cakes apart. The colour comes from the print on the wrapper, not from the tea. The exact wording Blue Seal Green Label is not used the same way everywhere, and some sellers treat Blue Seal and Green Seal as one thing and others as two.

## A name for a wrapper, not a grade {#not-a-grade}

Despite the page type, this is not a leaf grade in the sense of Gong Ting or special grade. It does not say how fine the leaf is. The Blue Seal wrappers are marked with a class, usually written as grade A and grade B, but that class belongs to the wrapper and the batch, and the trade does not read it as a standard that holds across years. The colour tells a collector which wrapper is in hand, and nothing more.

## What the label does not prove {#what-it-does-not-prove}

The mark belongs to China National Tea Corporation, which was the trader and brand owner, so the label says who sold the tea and not which factory made it or which leaf went in. Teas under old colour names vary a great deal from one era and one maker to the next, and the name carries no promise of age, origin or quality. Wrappers are also copied and reused, so the same words on a cake are not proof that it is old. Age and authenticity come from the cake itself and from its record, never from the name alone.
