## Where Li Shan is {#where-it-is}

Li Shan, which means Pear Mountain, is a high mountain area in the middle of Taiwan, on the Central Cross-Island Highway. It is a tea area and also a town, with orchards of pears, peaches and apples around it. The tea gardens are the highest in Taiwan, at roughly 1,800 to 2,500 metres, though places quote the range differently and some start as low as about 1,450. On a tea record the name covers the whole area, and a garden or sub-area inside it, such as Hua Gang, is the finer place.

## Why people value Li Shan tea {#why-people-value-it}

Li Shan is valued for cold. At this height the nights are cool, mist and cloud sit on the slopes, there is little direct sun, and frost or snow can reach the gardens in winter. Leaves grow slowly, come out thick and soft, and are said to carry more sweetness and less bitterness. The usual tea is a lightly oxidised, ball-rolled high mountain oolong, most often from the Qing Xin cultivar. Sellers describe it as sweet, thick in the mouth and clean, with a fresh green finish. The cold also limits how often it can be picked: spring and autumn crops are usual, and the coldest gardens may give only two or three harvests a year or skip winter. That, with the cost of working steep ground at this height, is why it is priced high.

## From orchards to tea {#history}

Fruit came to Li Shan before tea. After the Central Cross-Island Highway opened, the high ground was settled and planted with temperate fruit, and Li Shan became known for its pears. Tea was planted among the orchards later, in the last decades of the twentieth century, and it spread because the leaf sold well. By the 1990s Li Shan counted as one of Taiwan's main high mountain tea names. Places do not agree on who planted the first tea or in which year, so no start date is given here.

## Taichung or Nantou {#taichung-or-nantou}

Most places put Li Shan in Heping District, Taichung City, not in Nantou. A few seller pages file it under Nantou, and it sits close to the Nantou border, near Ren'ai Township, which is probably why the two are mixed. A record that says Lishan, Nantou follows that looser habit. Treat Taichung as the usual answer for the place, and treat the county on a seller's label as a convenience.

## Li Shan on a label {#on-a-label}

Li Shan on a label is a trade place name, and its edges are not fixed. Neighbouring high ground, such as Fu Shou Shan farm and the gardens near Da Yu Ling, is sometimes sold as Li Shan and sometimes under its own name. Some sellers say a tea sold as Li Shan should come from above 2,000 metres, but this is not a common rule. A label that says Li Shan but gives no garden, height or harvest season does not say much beyond the general area.
