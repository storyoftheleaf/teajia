## Where Cangwu is {#where-it-is}

Cangwu is a county of Wuzhou city, in eastern Guangxi, close to the border with Guangdong. Its northern part is hill and mountain country, and that is where the tea is. The town of Liubao lies there, in the north of the county, and Liu Bao tea takes its name from the town. Cangwu is the original home of Liu Bao, the place the style comes from.

## Why the mountains matter to the tea {#land-and-climate}

Liubao town sits among forested mountains, north of the Tropic of Cancer, in a warm, wet, subtropical monsoon climate. Mist and cloud are frequent, so the tea bushes grow in a good deal of shade, and the soils are acidic red earth. People in the trade point to this soft, damp, shaded setting as the reason the local leaf suits Liu Bao. It is the reason buyers who care about origin look to Cangwu first and to the rest of Wuzhou second.

## The home of Liu Bao {#known-for}

Cangwu is known for one tea, Liu Bao, and for being where it began. The town's own villages are the ones the trade names for the best leaf, and Heishi is the village most often mentioned beside Liubao itself. The county gazetteer of 1697 is the earliest record usually cited for the tea, and many accounts give the style a very long history, although that older date is not settled. What the style is, how it is made and how it ages are on the Liu Bao page, so this page does not repeat them.

## The tea boat road {#tea-boat-road}

Tea left the mountains by water. Merchants gathered it at the river landing of Hekou, below Liubao, and boats carried it down the Xijiang river through Wuzhou to Guangzhou, then on to Hong Kong, Macau and Southeast Asia. The trade calls this the tea boat road. It explains why Liu Bao was long drunk more in Malaysia and Singapore than in China, and why its history belongs to the overseas Chinese communities as much as to Guangxi.

## Decline and return {#decline-and-return}

The river trade broke down in the war years, and demand from Southeast Asia fell again later. By the early 1990s very few tea gardens were still worked in the county, and some were cut down for other crops. Interest returned in the 2000s, helped by the wider rise of aged dark tea, and Liu Bao gained protected-origin status in 2011. Today Cangwu is one part of a larger industry centred on Wuzhou, not the whole of it.

## Cangwu on a label {#on-a-label}

Cangwu on a label names a county, and Liubao names a town inside it. They tell a buyer more than Wuzhou or Guangxi do, because the protected Liu Bao name covers the wider Wuzhou area and the leaf there does not all come from Cangwu. A label that says Cangwu is still not proof of leaf from the Liubao mountains, so a named village or a named maker backs it up better than the county word alone.
