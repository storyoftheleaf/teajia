## Where Dali is {#where-it-is}

Dali is a prefecture in western Yunnan, north of Lincang and well north of the pu’er mountains of Xishuangbanna. Its best known tea place is the town of Xiaguan, which sits between the Cang mountains and Erhai lake. Dali is a place in tea mostly because of what was pressed and traded there, and much less because of what grows there.

## The tuocha trade at Xiaguan {#tuocha-trade}

Xiaguan stood on the old caravan routes that carried Yunnan tea north to Sichuan and Tibet, and from the early twentieth century it was a gathering and pressing town for compressed tea. The bowl shaped tuocha is tied to Xiaguan: trading houses there are credited with developing it, and it is pressed there still. Sources give different years for when the shape first appeared, so no date is settled here. The trade at Xiaguan is the reason the name Dali shows up on pu’er at all.

## Pressed in Dali, grown elsewhere {#on-a-label}

Dali on a tuocha or cake says where the tea was pressed, not where the leaf grew. The Xiaguan trade has long blended leaf brought in from other parts of Yunnan, with Lincang counties such as Fengqing often named among them. Reading a Xiaguan tuocha as Dali leaf is a mistake. It is better read as a Dali pressing of Yunnan leaf, and the maker is a separate question from the place.

## Tea grown in Dali {#grown-in-dali}

Dali makes only a small amount of tea itself. Gardens lie mainly on the Cang mountains and on the Wuliang range in Nanjian County, and what is sold locally is often green tea. Dali sits north of the zone where Yunnan’s large leaf tea trees are most concentrated, in a belt where large and smaller leaf plants grow side by side. The finest pu’er material is not usually said to come from here.
