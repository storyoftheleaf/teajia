## Where Muzha is {#where-it-is}

Muzha is a hill district in the southeast of Taipei, in Wenshan District, on the edge of the city. The tea slopes are low hills of a few hundred metres, and Maokong, the best known tea hillside, is part of the same tea country. The area is wet, with a large gap between day and night temperatures. Growers and sellers say this resembles Anxi in Fujian, where Tieguanyin comes from.

## How Tieguanyin came to Muzha {#history}

Tieguanyin reached Muzha from Anxi in Fujian in the late nineteenth century, carried by Fujian settlers. The trade most often names the tea master Zhang Naimiao, who brought plants over in 1895 and planted them in the Zhanghu part of Muzha. Many tea families in the hills still carry the Zhang name. Later shipments of plants are reported, but the years and numbers differ from place to place, so they are not settled here.

## The tea Muzha is known for {#known-for}

Muzha is known for Tieguanyin made in the traditional Taiwanese way: a ball-rolled oolong, oxidised part way and then roasted over charcoal. It is far darker and heavier than the green, lightly finished Tieguanyin many people know from Fujian. The trade describes roasted and ripe-fruit notes, nuts and honey, an amber cup with a full body, and a sweet, lingering return. The slow roasting, repeated over many days, is a large part of why the tea costs more.

## Visiting the tea hills {#visiting}

Tea here is also a place people go to. From the later twentieth century the city encouraged tea farms to open to visitors, and the hills now hold tea houses and farms that sell their own leaf. Most of those farms grow Tieguanyin. Buying at the source is common, which makes the farm and the year worth asking about.

## Muzha on a label {#on-a-label}

Muzha Tieguanyin on a label names a place and a style of making. It is not proof of the plant. True Tieguanyin trees, often called zhengcong, give little leaf and need careful hands, and some growers in Muzha now use other cultivars such as Qingxin oolong for the same style of tea. Tieguanyin is also a name used for a way of making, so leaf sold as Tieguanyin may come from other plants. Place, cultivar and roast are three separate facts, and a label rarely gives all three. How the name sits with the wider tea is on the [Tieguanyin](tieguanyin) page.
