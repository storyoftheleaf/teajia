## Where Lugu is {#where-it-is}

Lugu is a mountain township in the south west of Nantou County, in central Taiwan. It sits in the foothills of the Central Mountain Range and is a township of valleys, bamboo forest and tea gardens. Xitou, Shan Lin Xi and Dong Ding mountain are all known place names in or beside it. On a tea record, Lugu names a township, which is a finer place than the county Nantou and a larger one than any single mountain or garden inside it.

## Why people value Lugu tea {#known-for}

Lugu is known as the home of Dong Ding oolong, and for most buyers that is the whole reason the name matters. The place is cool and often under cloud and mist, with sunny mornings and foggy afternoons, and the trade ties the character of its tea to that weather and to the mountain setting. Tea shares the township with bamboo, and the bamboo shoots and bamboo goods of Lugu are as well known in Taiwan as its tea. This page covers the place only. For what Dong Ding oolong is and how it is made, see the Taiwan oolong page.

## How tea came to Lugu {#history}

The story told locally is that tea bushes were brought to Lugu from the Wuyi Mountains in Fujian in the middle of the nineteenth century. In the most repeated version, a local man named Lin Fengchi returned from the mainland with a small number of seedlings, said to be 36, and some were planted on the slopes of Dong Ding. Places differ on the year, giving 1851 in some and 1855 in others, and on who planted what, so the details are told, not settled. What is agreed is that the bushes came from Fujian, that tea has been grown here since the nineteenth century, and that it grew into the township's main cash crop.

## Dong Ding and the higher gardens {#dong-ding-and-dalunshan}

Dong Ding is a mountain in Lugu, and gardens on and around it are where the tea took its name. Dong Ding mountain itself is not high, with one local source putting it at about 750 metres. Altitude for Dong Ding tea is quoted very differently, from about 600 metres up past 1,000, because the name covers more than the one mountain. Some growers have moved to higher ground in the township, such as Dalunshan, where gardens are described at about 1,250 to 1,500 metres. One account gives declining soil fertility on the old slopes as the reason. A tea from those gardens is still a Lugu tea, but it grows higher and cooler than Dong Ding mountain itself.

## Lugu on a label {#on-a-label}

Lugu on a label or a record says the tea was grown in that township. It does not say the tea is Dong Ding oolong, and it does not say how the tea was made, whether roasted or not. Local grade marks that appear on Lugu tea are a separate matter from the place. The name Dong Ding is also used for the style when it is made outside Lugu, so Lugu is the stronger place claim of the two.
