## A factory's mark, not a style or a place {#definition}

Three Cranes, also written San He, is the trademark of Wuzhou Tea Factory in Guangxi, the largest maker of Liu Bao. It is a brand of one factory. It is not a type of tea, a grade of leaf or a village, and other makers of Liu Bao do not use it. The story of the name, and of the factory behind it, is on the San He Liu Bao page.

## What the mark tells a buyer, and what it does not {#reading-the-label}

The mark tells a buyer who made the tea: Wuzhou Tea Factory, working in its own warehouses. Sellers also treat it as a sign of consistent factory making, which is a reputation and not a measure of any one tea. It does not say how old the tea is, which leaf it holds or how it is stored. Those come from the rest of the label: the harvest year and the year of packing, which are often printed as a pair, and a grade number such as third grade. The four or five digit codes that follow the brand name on many labels are recipe or batch numbers. What each digit stands for is not established here, so the number is best read as a lookup against the seller's own description.
