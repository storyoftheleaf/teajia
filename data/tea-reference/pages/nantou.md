## Where Nantou is {#where-it-is}

Nantou is a county in the middle of Taiwan, and the only one that does not touch the sea. About four fifths of it is hills and mountains, rising to the Central Mountain Range in the east and to Yushan, the island's highest peak, in the south. Sun Moon Lake, the island's largest lake, lies in the middle. On a tea record, Nantou usually names the county as a whole, and a township or mountain inside it, such as Lugu or Shan Lin Xi, is the finer place.

## Why people value Nantou tea {#why-people-value-it}

Nantou is Taiwan's biggest tea county, with more than half of the island's tea plantings and output. What makes it matter to a buyer is range. In one county the land climbs from low hills to cold mountain gardens, so it makes very different teas side by side: roasted oolong from the lower slopes, lightly oxidised high mountain oolong from the cool heights, and black tea near the lake. A tea sold as Nantou says which county it came from, and the township, mountain or garden says which of those teas it is.

## Low, middle and high Nantou {#altitude-bands}

The trade splits Nantou's tea country by height. Low hills such as Mingjian grow tea for volume. The middle belt, around Zhushan, Sun Moon Lake and Dong Ding, is where roasted oolong is at home. Above about a thousand metres, around Shan Lin Xi and the high ground of Ren'ai Township, the gardens are cool and misty and make the light, sweet high mountain style. Which style a Nantou oolong follows depends on where in the county it grew, so the county name alone does not tell you whether it will be roasted or green and floral.

## Lugu and Dong Ding {#lugu-dong-ding}

Lugu Township, in the south west of Nantou, is where Dong Ding oolong comes from, and it is the best known tea place in the county. Lugu has grown tea for sale since the nineteenth century, and Dong Ding mountain is covered in gardens. For what Dong Ding oolong is and how it is made, see the Taiwan oolong page. What this page adds is its place: Lugu sits in the middle belt of Nantou, below the high mountain gardens, so it is a county of roasted teas as well as light ones.

## Shan Lin Xi {#shan-lin-xi}

Shan Lin Xi is a high mountain tea area in Zhushan Township, in Nantou, and it is described as lying near Dong Ding. Its gardens are among the county's highest and are known for bamboo forest, mist and steep slopes. Places quote its altitude differently, from the low hundreds of metres up to about 1,950 metres in one account and 1,600 to 1,800 metres in another, so no single range is safe to state.

## Ren'ai Township and Lishan {#ren-ai-and-lishan}

Ren'ai Township, in the east of Nantou, grows high mountain tea at roughly 1,100 to 2,000 metres. Lishan is next door but is not in Nantou. Sources place it in Taichung, and sellers sell it as its own name for tea grown above about 1,800 metres. Teas from the high ground between the two, such as Dayuling, are placed in different counties by different sources, so a seller's county for them is not safe to assume. Alishan, often named with these, is in Chiayi County.

## Sun Moon Lake and Yuchi {#sun-moon-lake}

Yuchi Township, around Sun Moon Lake, is Nantou's black tea country. It grows Assam type tea, introduced in Japanese times, and its Sun Moon Lake black tea became better known after the 1999 earthquake, when growers there turned to it. It is not oolong, so it matters here only as the other half of Nantou's tea picture.
