## Where Beipu is {#where-it-is}

Beipu is a township in the south-west of Hsinchu County, in north-western Taiwan. It sits in low hill country between the central mountains and the coastal plain, well below the altitudes of the high-mountain oolong areas. It is one of the most Hakka townships on the island, and the tea grown here is Hakka farmers' tea.

## Oriental Beauty, the tea Beipu is known for {#known-for}

Beipu is best known for Oriental Beauty, called Dongfang Meiren in Mandarin. It is a heavily oxidised oolong, close to black tea in how it is made, with a sweet, honeyed and fruity cup and little bitterness. Beipu is counted among the core places where it is made, together with Emei and Zhudong in the same county. The tea is sold under other names too, including Pengfeng tea and white-tip oolong.

## Why a leafhopper makes the tea {#leafhopper}

Oriental Beauty is made from young shoots that a small green leafhopper has bitten. The plant answers the bite by producing aroma compounds, and the leaf curls and takes on white, green, red and brown tones as it is made. This is why Beipu growers keep sprays off the gardens, and why the tea is picked only in the heat of early summer, when the insects are active. Because the leaf cannot be made to order, the harvest is small and the tea costs more than most oolong. Qingxin Dapang is the cultivar most often named as the best suited, with other cultivars also used.

## Beipu and the rest of Hsinchu {#in-hsinchu}

Beipu is one place among several that make Oriental Beauty. Emei and Zhudong in Hsinchu, the Toufen area in Miaoli and parts of Taoyuan and New Taipei all grow it. A label that says Beipu names a township. A label that says Oriental Beauty names a style, which is not limited to Beipu.
