## Where Bulang Mountain is {#where-it-is}

Bulang Mountain lies in the south of Menghai County in Xishuangbanna, Yunnan, next to the border with Myanmar. It covers roughly one thousand square kilometres and is one of the larger tea areas in the county. It is a mountain area with many villages, not a single village. Banzhang, Lao Man’e and Manxinlong are among its named tea villages.

## Who lives there {#people}

The mountain takes its name from the Bulang people, who have grown and drunk tea here for a very long time. Lao Man’e is often described as one of their oldest villages, at about fourteen hundred years. Hani villages such as the Banzhang settlements sit in the same mountains.

## The tea it is known for {#known-for}

Bulang Mountain is known for raw pu’er from old tea gardens. The trade describes the tea as forceful: a strong, bitter start that turns sweet, thick liquor and a strong feel in the throat. It is often set against the softer, sweeter style of Yiwu. Villages differ a good deal. Lao Man’e is known for bitterness, and some other villages make sweeter tea.

## A newer famous mountain {#history}

Bulang is a pu’er area whose fame is recent. Its reputation grew with the rise of Banzhang, whose price pulled attention to the whole mountain.

## Bulang Mountain on a label {#on-a-label}

Bulang Mountain on a label is a broad market name. Leaf from smaller or less famous villages is often sold in bulk, blended, and offered as Bulang tea. A cake called Bulang Mountain rarely names the village, and some makers do not state where the leaf came from. It points to a mountain style more than to one place.

## Bulang in ripe pu’er {#in-ripe-pu-er}

Bulang leaf is also used for ripe pu’er, and ripe cakes and loose tea sold under the name Bulang Mountain are common. The raw tea is strong, so ripe tea made from it is described by sellers as thick and full. Sellers disagree on whether it stays bitter after piling, so ripe Bulang tea is better judged cup by cup than from the name.
