## What sheng means {#identity}

Sheng is the undarkened branch of Pu’er, often called raw Pu’er in English. The label distinguishes it from shou, whose accelerated post-fermentation creates a markedly different tea from the outset. [cite:teaguardian-raw-cooked-puer]

## From maocha to finished tea {#processing}

Maocha names an intermediate state in tea production, while sheng describes the tea as it reaches the drinker. Sheng material may be sorted, dried, left loose, or steamed and compressed; the two terms overlap, but they describe the tea from different viewpoints. [cite:teaguardian-raw-cooked-puer]

## Change through storage {#aging}

Sheng is not given shou’s rapid fermentation. Its character instead changes gradually during storage, with leaf, liquor, aroma, and texture developing over years. Storage conditions therefore belong to the tea’s history, not as a guarantee of quality but as part of how any particular cake has evolved. [cite:marshaln-young-puer-2013]
