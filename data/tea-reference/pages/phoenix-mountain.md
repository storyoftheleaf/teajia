## Where Phoenix Mountain is {#where-it-is}

Phoenix Mountain, Fenghuang Shan, is a mountain range in Fenghuang town, in Chao'an district of Chaozhou, in eastern Guangdong. It is the high ground of the Chaoshan region, with its top peak rising to about 1,500 metres. Tea grows on its slopes from low foothills up to roughly 1,100 metres, and the name on a tea label usually means this range as a whole rather than one village.

## Mist, cool air and slow leaf {#mist-and-climate}

People value the mountain for the weather the tea grows in. Cloud and mist sit on the upper slopes for much of the year, the light is soft, and the nights are cool. The bushes grow slowly in these conditions, and the trade links that slow growth to the thick leaf and the lasting scent prized in this tea. The higher the garden, the cooler it is, and the more a tea is expected to show what the trade calls mountain rhyme.

## Wudong and the other gardens {#wudong-and-villages}

Wudong, the high ridge around the top of the range, is the part named most often as the heart of the mountain, and its high gardens are the ones the trade prices highest. Wudong village sits high on the ridge, with gardens near and above 900 metres. Da'an, lower on gentler slopes in Fengxi, is known for the largest old tea garden on the mountain. Other villages such as Shiguping, Lizipping and Guantou are also named, and each is said to give its own character, though little of that is written down plainly. A village name on a tea tells you more about where it grew than the mountain name does.

## The old trees {#old-trees}

The mountain is known for tea trees that stand where they have grown for hundreds of years, left unpruned so that they grow tall and send roots deep into the granite. A few are said to be 600 or 700 years old, and are tied by tradition to the Song dynasty, though no age is agreed or proven. Their leaf is made on its own and sold at very high prices, because each gives only a few kilograms a year. Most of what is sold today comes from bushes grown from these trees, not from the trees themselves.

## The people of the mountain {#people}

Phoenix Mountain is widely named as the homeland of the She people, who are tied to the tea of the mountain by local tradition. The mountain is also the home of Chaozhou gongfu tea, the small-cup way of brewing the region is known for. Tea has been made here for centuries, but no start date is agreed: sources give 700, 900 and more than 1,000 years.
