## What Gongjian means {#definition}

Gongjian is the middle one of the three traditional loose Anhua teas, known together as the three tips. The name is usually rendered "tribute tip". It is a loose, lightly pressed tea made from Anhua raw dark tea, and it sits between the finer Tianjian and the coarser Shengjian.

## Where it sits among the three tips {#among-three-tips}

The three tips are sorted by the leaf they are made from. Tianjian takes the most tender leaf, Shengjian the coarsest with the most stalk, and Gongjian the leaf in between, usually described as second grade. For a buyer that places it as the plainer, sturdier step up from Shengjian and the lighter, less tender step down from Tianjian. A grade says how the leaf was sorted, not how well the tea was made or kept, so a careful, well aged Gongjian can drink better than a careless Tianjian.

## What people often notice {#common-impressions}

People often notice pine smoke or pine wood from the wood-fire drying, a sweet taste and a thick, smooth feel, in a clear orange liquor. Aged Gongjian is said to turn mellower and sweeter with years in storage. These impressions are shared by Anhua dark tea in general and are not specific to this grade.

## Other names and other teas {#other-names}

Gongjian is also written Gong Jian and sold in English as Tribute Tip. Its three sisters are together called the three tips, and the same family is also named Xiangjian, the Hunan tip. Tianjian and Shengjian are separate grades with their own leaf. Why the tea is called "tribute" is not established here, so the name should not be read as proof that this grade was ever sent as tribute.
