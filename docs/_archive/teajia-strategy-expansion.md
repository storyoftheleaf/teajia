# TeajiA Strategy Expansion

## Sessions, Spaces, Pricing, Pipeline & Guest Strategy

**Companion to:** teajia-complete-strategy.md
**Purpose:** Locks in the decisions and frameworks discussed for the three studio spaces, client tiers, pricing philosophy, session structure, inquiry/intake process, and magazine guest strategy.

# 1. The Three Spaces

Each space serves a distinct purpose in the TeajiA ecosystem. Together they tell the full story of what you do: connect, transform, equip.

## Space 1: The Office / Podcast Studio

**Purpose:** Social connection and discovery.

**Character:** This is where conversations happen. Two people (or a small group) sit across a tea table, share tea, and talk. Ideas emerge. Relationships form. Content is created.

**Function in the Business:**
- Hosts the TeajiA podcast/conversation series. Guests sit over tea, share wisdom, and the conversation is recorded and produced.
- Primary discovery channel. People find TeajiA through the conversations published from this room.
- Natural meeting space for potential collaborators, clients, and community members.
- Where business relationships develop organically over tea.

**Atmosphere:** Warm, inviting, conversationally oriented. Good acoustics. Intimate but not overly formal. The kind of room where people relax into real talk.

**Capacity:** 2 to 4 people comfortably.

**Revenue Connection:** Content creation (podcast drives audience), relationship building (leads to all tiers of engagement), potential hireable experience ("Have a meaningful conversation over tea in a beautiful space, walk away with produced content").

---

## Space 2: The Living Room / Ceremonial Space

**Purpose:** Deep connection, ceremony, and transformation.

**Character:** This is the heart. Twelve people sit on the floor in a circle. Charcoal is burning. The room has beautiful art and a sound experience on the walls. The atmosphere invites people to drop in, slow down, and connect at a level they rarely access elsewhere.

**Function in the Business:**
- Hosts group ceremonial sessions and meditative tea gatherings.
- The space that sells the vision. When someone sits in this circle and feels what happens, they don't need a pitch. They understand what you're offering to build for others.
- Community gathering point. Regular or occasional group sessions build a local following.
- Can host private group bookings (retreats, special occasions, visiting groups).
- Filming location for course content, especially anything related to ceremony, presence, and group practice.

**Atmosphere:** Meditative, grounded, immersive. Floor seating, charcoal, art, sound. More about silence and presence than conversation. When people speak here, it means something different than in the office.

**Capacity:** Up to 12 people.

**Revenue Connection:** Group sessions (paid), private group bookings, course filming, and critically, the experiential proof that drives Tier 2 and Tier 3 space design clients. This room is your showroom for what a tea space can do to a community.

---

## Space 3: The Art Studio / Shop

**Purpose:** Tangible experience and accessible entry.

**Character:** A working studio that doubles as a shop. You can see the art being made, touch the teaware, hold the cups, taste tea, and walk out with something. It grounds the entire practice in real, physical objects.

**Function in the Business:**
- The casual entry point. Someone walks in, curious. They sit, they taste, they discover.
- Product showroom for everything in the TeajiA shop: tea, teaware, incense, oracle cards, art, tables.
- Where Tier 1 clients (personal practice) naturally emerge. They experience, they buy, they begin.
- Catalog/preview space for Tier 2 and 3 clients to see what curation looks like in practice.
- Working studio, so sometimes there's ambient noise. This is honest and part of the character.

**Atmosphere:** Creative, accessible, alive with making. Not a pristine gallery. A space where beautiful things are being created and can be touched, held, and taken home.

**Capacity:** Flexible. Individual visitors, small groups, or browsing while you work.

**Revenue Connection:** Direct product sales, Guided Practice Setup sessions, the most natural path from stranger to practitioner to client.

---

## How the Three Spaces Work Together

A visitor might first encounter TeajiA through the podcast (Space 1 content). They visit Bali and stop by the studio/shop (Space 3) out of curiosity. They sit, they taste, they buy a few things. They come back for a Guided Practice Setup. Eventually they attend a group session in the living room (Space 2). They feel something profound. They own a retreat center in another country. They ask: "Can you help me create something like this?"

The spaces are a funnel, but not a corporate one. They're a natural progression of deepening relationship with tea and with you.

# 2. Client Tiers

## Tier 1: Personal Practice

**Who:** Individuals discovering tea or deepening their practice. Visitors to Bali, locals, people who find you through content or word of mouth.

**What they want:** To experience real tea, understand what they're drinking, build confidence in their own practice, and go home with the right tools.

**Entry point:** Walking into the studio/shop, attending a group session, booking a Guided Practice Setup.

**Typical engagement:** One to three sessions. Product purchases. Ongoing relationship through content and occasional return visits or sourcing requests.

**Value range:** $100 to $1,000+ depending on session type and products purchased.

**Conversion path:** Many Tier 1 clients become long-term community members, magazine readers, and referral sources. Some eventually become Tier 2 or 3 clients when their life circumstances align.

---

## Tier 2: Space Integration

**Who:** People with existing spaces who want to bring tea culture into what they offer. Retreat center owners, wellness space operators, private residence owners with a room to transform, community leaders, boutique hospitality operators.

**What they want:** Design guidance for integrating tea into their space, the right teaware and tea selection, training for their team or themselves, and sometimes ongoing tea supply.

**Entry point:** Often a Tier 1 experience first (they sat with you and want this for their space). Also through the magazine (you featured their world or someone in it), through referral, or through the website portfolio.

**Typical engagement:** Starts with a Design Session (paid consultation). May lead to a partial or full implementation project. Scope varies widely: might be just curation and tea selection for a single room, or might include spatial recommendations, full teaware curation, tea program, and staff training.

**Value range:** $5,000 to $30,000 depending on scope.

**What's included at this tier (varies by project):**
- Design Session with written concept
- Space layout and atmosphere recommendations
- Teaware curation and sourcing
- Tea selection and program design
- Staff or personal training on brewing, serving, and holding space
- Printed training materials and reference guides
- Optional ongoing tea supply relationship

---

## Tier 3: Full Tea House Design

**Who:** Well-resourced individuals, organizations, or hospitality operators who want a complete tea space created from concept through opening. International reach. Private residences, retreat centers, boutique hotels, community spaces, showrooms.

**What they want:** The full vision. Design, curation, tea program, training, operations, launch support. They want someone who can see the whole picture and bring it to life.

**Entry point:** Referral from Tier 1 or 2 clients, the magazine and portfolio, direct inquiry through the website, personal connection through events or the Bali community.

**Typical engagement:** Design Session, then a custom-scoped project with multiple phases: concept, sourcing, installation, training, opening.

**Value range:** $50,000 to $100,000+ depending on scope.

**What's included at this tier:**
- Everything from Tier 2, plus:
- Full space design (layout, atmosphere, flow, materials guidance)
- Furniture selection or custom creation (tea tables, seating, display)
- Art selection or commissioning
- Complete teaware collection curated for the space and its clientele
- Comprehensive tea program (menu, sourcing, rotation, seasonal approach)
- Staff training program (brewing, ceremony, service, tea knowledge)
- Operations design (service flow, daily rhythm, inventory, sourcing schedule)
- Launch support and refinement
- Optional ongoing supply and advisory relationship

---

## Tier: Events

**Who:** Festival organizers, brand managers, private hosts, retreat leaders, corporate event planners.

**What they want:** A tea experience brought to their gathering. You arrive with everything needed and create an atmosphere and moment that elevates their event.

**Entry point:** Direct inquiry, referral, community connections in Bali, brands or organizations that discover TeajiA through content.

**Typical engagement:** Single event or short series. You bring tea, teaware, setup, and your presence.

**Value range:** $500 to $5,000+ depending on duration, scale, and whether travel is involved.

**What's included:**
- Pre-event consultation to understand the gathering and the desired atmosphere
- All tea, teaware, and setup materials
- Your presence and facilitation for the duration
- Teardown
- For larger or international events: travel and accommodation as additional costs

# 3. Session Offerings (Detailed)

## Open Sit

**What it is:** Someone visits the studio/shop space. You share tea. They experience the world of TeajiA. No appointment needed, no formal structure.

**Charge:** Free. Products purchased naturally.

**Why it's free:** This is your gift and your lead generator. It's the wine shop tasting bar. The generosity of this experience is what builds trust, word of mouth, and the relationships that lead to everything else.

**What people typically walk away with:** A few teas, sometimes a teapot or cup, a feeling they want to return to. Average spend varies but organic purchases often land in the $50 to $300 range.

---

## Guided Practice Setup

**What it is:** A dedicated session for someone who specifically wants to build their own tea practice. They're coming to you for your expertise, your curation, and your teaching. They leave fully equipped and confident.

**Duration:** 2 to 3 hours.

**Charge:** $250 to $300 session fee. Includes $100 to $150 in product credit toward teas and teaware purchased during the session.

**What's included:**

*During the session:*
- Your full attention and guidance
- Tasting multiple teas together, exploring what resonates with them
- Teaware selection tailored to their taste, space, and practice level
- Hands-on brewing instruction with the specific tools they're choosing
- Conversation about philosophy, approach, and how to integrate tea into their life
- Curation of their "starter collection" (teas, teaware, accessories)

*What they take home (beyond purchases):*

1. **Printed Brewing Guide** (single card, front and back, or small folded card)
   - Specific to the teas they purchased (not generic)
   - Water temperature, leaf amount, steep times, number of infusions for each tea
   - Vessel recommendations matched to what they bought
   - Brief storage notes

2. **Practice Philosophy Card**
   - A few lines from you. The kind of thing you'd naturally say during the session.
   - Example tone: "Let the first cup be for the tea. The second for yourself. The third for whoever you're sharing it with."
   - Not a lecture. A seed.

3. **TeajiA "What's Next" Card**
   - Clean, on-brand
   - Links to the magazine, courses (when available), and the shop
   - "When you're ready to go deeper" or "Your practice grows with you"
   - Contact information and how to reach you for sourcing, sessions, or guidance

4. **Follow-up Check-in** (1 to 2 weeks after the session)
   - A personal WhatsApp or email message
   - "How's the practice going? Any questions about the teas?"
   - Costs you five minutes. Cements the relationship.
   - Natural opening for repeat purchases, referrals, or deeper engagement.

**Optional addition (future):**

5. **TeajiA Practice Booklet** (12 to 16 pages, printed, designed)
   - One-time design investment, printed in batches
   - Covers the six tea types briefly
   - Basic ceremony principles
   - How to care for your teaware
   - What your tea journey might look like from here
   - Beautiful enough to keep on a tea shelf
   - Becomes part of the TeajiA brand experience
   - Every Guided Practice Setup client receives one

**Typical total spend:** Session fee ($250 to $300) plus product purchases beyond the credit ($200 to $500+). Total experience value for the client: $450 to $800+.

## Private or Group Experience (Hireable)

**What it is:** You are hired to bring a tea experience to a gathering, event, dinner, or occasion. You bring everything: tea, teaware, the setup, the atmosphere, and your knowledge and presence.

**Contexts:**
- Private dinner parties
- Retreat center sessions
- Brand activations and launches
- Festival appearances
- Corporate team experiences
- Wedding or celebration ceremonies
- Visiting group experiences (tour groups, wellness retreats stopping in Bali)

**Charge:**
- Half-day (up to 4 hours): Starting at $500

- Scale up for larger groups, premium contexts, or multi-day engagements
- Tea and supplies: included in base rate for standard sessions, quoted separately for large-scale events
- Travel (beyond local Bali): quoted as additional cost

**What's included:**
- Pre-event consultation (phone/WhatsApp) to understand the gathering
- All tea, teaware, and setup materials
- Setup and teardown
- Your presence, facilitation, and teaching throughout
- Flexibility to adjust the experience to the room (more social, more meditative, more educational depending on context)

**What could be added for premium events:**
- Custom tea selection for the occasion
- Printed menus or tasting cards for guests
- A small "take-home" package for each guest (tea sample, card, something tangible)
- Collaboration with the client's chef or event planner on food pairings
- Photography coordination if they have a photographer present

## Group Ceremonial Session (Living Room Space)

**What it is:** A group experience in your living room ceremonial space. Up to 12 people sit in a circle on the floor. Charcoal, sound, art, silence. This is the meditative, transformative offering.

**Contexts:**
- Regular community gatherings (weekly, monthly, or seasonal)

**Charge:**

**What's included:**

- Your guidance and presence
- The atmosphere of the space itself

**Why this matters for the business beyond session revenue:** Every person who sits in this circle and feels what happens becomes a potential ambassador, client, or collaborator. This is where retreat center owners realize they want this in their space. This is where word of mouth is born.

# 4. Pricing Philosophy

## Guiding Principles

**Generosity at the entry, professionalism at every level.** The casual sit is free. Everything else has a clear, fair price. People who value your expertise deserve a way to pay for it, and you deserve to be compensated for real work.

**The session fee is not a barrier. It's a signal.** It tells people this is a professional practice, not a hobby. It also filters for people who are serious about their journey. The product credit built into the Guided Practice Setup softens the fee and ensures they walk away with tangible value.

**Inquiry-based pricing for high-value work is correct.** Every Tier 2 and 3 project is different. Stating prices on the website would either scare people away or undervalue your work. The conversation determines the scope and the investment.

**The paid consultation protects your time and qualifies serious clients.** Anyone willing to pay $750 to $1,500 for a Design Session is a real prospect. The credit-toward-project structure means they're not losing anything by taking that step.

**Ongoing supply relationships turn one-time projects into recurring revenue.** "We don't just design your space. We keep it alive." This is built into Tier 2 and 3 offerings and represents a significant long-term revenue stream.

# 5. Inquiry and Intake Process

## The Flow

### Step 1: Inquiry Form

When someone clicks "Start a conversation" or "Inquire" anywhere on the site, they reach a short, elegant form. Not corporate. Feels like TeajiA.

**Fields:**
- Name
- Email
- WhatsApp (optional, for Bali-based inquiries)
- What brings you here? (checkboxes, select all that apply):
  - Personal session or practice guidance
  - Space design or tea integration
  - Event or group experience
  - Tea sourcing
  - Sourcing journey
  - Something else
- Tell me a bit about what you're envisioning (open text, 2-3 sentences is fine)
- How did you find TeajiA? (optional, open text)

**Design notes:** This form should be beautiful and minimal. No required fields beyond name, email, and the checkbox. Make it feel like writing a note, not filling out an application.

---

### Step 2: Personal Response (Within 24 to 48 Hours)

You respond personally. Not automated, but templated enough that you're not writing from scratch. Warm, specific to what they shared, and clear about the next step.

**For Session/Practice inquiries:**
- Acknowledge what they're looking for
- Suggest a session type (Guided Practice Setup, group session, etc.)
- Offer available times
- Share what to expect

**For Space Design inquiries:**
- Acknowledge their vision
- Ask one or two clarifying questions if needed
- Suggest a conversation (call, video, or in-person if they're in Bali)
- Mention the Design Session as the natural next step

**For Event inquiries:**
- Acknowledge the event context
- Ask about date, location, group size, and desired atmosphere
- Offer to discuss details by call or WhatsApp

**For Sourcing inquiries:**
- Acknowledge what they're looking for
- Ask about quantity, types, and intended use
- Suggest a conversation to understand their needs

---

### Step 3: The Conversation

For Tier 1 (sessions), this might just be confirming the booking. Simple.

For Tier 2 and 3 (space design), this is the free first conversation. The "first date." 30 to 60 minutes, ideally over tea if they're in Bali, or by video call if remote. You're both exploring fit.

**What you're learning:**
- What is their space? (photos, video, or description)
- Who is their community?
- What role do they want tea to play?
- What's their timeline and budget range?
- Have they experienced tea culture before, and at what depth?

**What they're learning:**
- Who you are and how you think
- That you understand their world
- That you have a clear process
- That this is a real, professional practice

---

### Step 4: The Vision Guide (For Space Design Inquiries)

After the initial conversation, if there's mutual interest in moving forward, you send the Vision Guide. This is a beautiful, short PDF (one to two pages, on-brand) that asks them to prepare for the Design Session.

**Vision Guide prompts:**
- Share 3 to 5 photos of your space (or the space you're considering)
- Describe your community. Who gathers here? Who do you serve?
- What do you hope tea brings into your space? (atmosphere, connection, offering, revenue, all valid)
- Are there spaces, images, or experiences that inspire your vision? (share links or photos)
- What's your timeline for this project?
- Is there anything else that would help me understand what you're building?

**Why this matters:**
- It makes them do real thinking, which qualifies them
- It gives you material to prepare for the Design Session
- It signals that you have a process and take this seriously
- The act of filling it out deepens their commitment
- It becomes part of the project archive

---

### Step 5: Design Session (Paid Consultation)

The second date. You've reviewed their Vision Guide responses. You come prepared with questions, ideas, and reference images. If in person, you sit over tea (of course). If remote, you send a tea package in advance so you're drinking together.

**During the session:**
- Review their space together (photos, video, or on-site)
- Explore atmosphere, flow, and possibilities
- Discuss teaware, tea, and material direction
- Brew teas that represent what their space could feel like
- Begin shaping the concept together

**Deliverable (within one week):**
- A written Design Concept (beautiful short PDF, 2 to 4 pages)
- Your recommendations for space, atmosphere, and flow
- Initial direction for teaware, tea, and materials
- Scope options (what a partial vs. full engagement would include)
- Investment range for the project
- Clear next step if they want to proceed

**Price:** $750 to $1,500 (depending on whether it requires travel/site visit). Credits toward the full project if they proceed.

---

### Step 6: Proposal (For Full Projects)

If the Design Session confirms the fit and they want to move forward, you send a proposal.

**Proposal format:** Clean, beautiful, 2 to 4 pages. Not a 30-page corporate document. On-brand.

**Contents:**
- Brief restatement of their vision and what you discussed
- Scope of work (what's included, what's not)
- Phases and timeline
- Investment (total and payment schedule)
- What you need from them (access, decisions, timing)
- Terms (travel, revisions, ongoing supply if applicable)

**Payment structure (example for a Tier 3 project):**
- 30% upon agreement to begin
- 30% at sourcing/procurement phase
- 30% at installation/training phase
- 10% at project completion/opening

---

### Step 7: Ongoing Relationship

After any engagement, the relationship continues through:
- Follow-up check-ins (for all tiers)
- Ongoing tea supply (for Tier 2 and 3)
- Magazine features (their space, their story)
- Invitations to community gatherings and events
- Referral opportunities

This is where one project becomes the seed for the next.

# 6. Magazine Guest Feature Strategy

## Philosophy

TeajiA's magazine is a community space. Featuring other voices doesn't create competition. It creates a hub. The person who brings everyone together holds a unique position that no single guest can replicate. You are the curator, the connector, the venue. Nobody competes with the venue.

## Guest Categories

### Audience Builders (2 to 3 of first 10)

People with their own following who will share the feature and bring new eyes to TeajiA.

**Who:** Tea influencers, wellness figures with tea practices, ceramicists with strong social presence, food/travel creators who touch the tea world.

**What they bring:** Their audience discovers TeajiA. Immediate readership boost. Social sharing.

**What they get:** Beautiful, thoughtful editorial treatment they rarely receive. Most content creators are producing their own material. Being featured by someone else, with care and depth, is a gift.

### Credibility Anchors (1 to 2 of first 10)

Respected figures whose presence signals that TeajiA is serious.

**Who:** Longtime tea masters, scholars of tea history, established ceremony practitioners, revered artisans. People the tea world already respects.

**What they bring:** Legitimacy. When a well-known Taiwanese tea master or a respected tea historian appears in your magazine, it tells the world that TeajiA operates at a certain level.

**What they get:** A platform that treats their knowledge with the depth it deserves. Many of these figures are not being documented well, if at all. You're preserving and amplifying their wisdom.

### Potential Clients and Collaborators (2 of first 10)

People who run spaces, retreats, or hospitality concepts where tea could be integrated.

**Who:** Retreat center founders, boutique hotel operators, wellness space owners, community builders. People whose spaces are beautiful and whose values align with yours.

**What they bring:** They introduce their audience to TeajiA, and the feature itself is a soft demonstration that you understand their world. Some of these features will naturally evolve into "could you help me with my space?" conversations.

**What they get:** Exposure, a beautiful story about their work, and a deeper connection to the tea world. They also get to experience your editorial eye and strategic thinking, which builds trust for potential future projects.

### Makers and Artisans (2 to 3 of first 10)

The people who create the objects of tea culture.

**Who:** Potters, woodworkers, metalworkers, textile artists, bamboo craftspeople. People whose teaware or tea-adjacent objects you carry, admire, or want to collaborate with.

**What they bring:** They connect your shop to living stories. A teapot in your shop becomes ten times more compelling when readers have met the person who made it. They also bring their own following, often passionate and design-oriented.

**What they get:** A platform that treats their craft with the seriousness it deserves. Many artisans struggle with visibility. A well-told story about their process and philosophy is deeply valuable to them.

### Cross-World Connectors (1 to 2 of first 10)

People who bring tea into unexpected or broader contexts.

**Who:** A musician who drinks tea before performing. An architect who designs around ceremony. A therapist who uses tea in practice. A chef who integrates tea into cuisine. A meditation teacher who uses tea as a gateway.

**What they bring:** They expand TeajiA's audience beyond the tea-specific world. They show that tea intersects with everything, which is core to your philosophy. They make the magazine surprising and alive.

**What they get:** A chance to articulate a part of their practice that doesn't usually get space. Most interviews with musicians are about music. Asking about their tea practice is unexpected and often deeply personal. The result is content they genuinely want to share.

## The Pitch

Keep it simple and genuine:

"I'm building TeajiA as a community space for tea culture, a magazine, a learning platform, and a gathering place for people who care about tea. I'd love to feature your story and your work. We'd have a conversation (in person or by call), I'd shape it into something beautiful, and it lives in the magazine for your community and mine. No cost, no catch. Just a desire to share your voice with people who would value it."

Most people in the tea world are not getting this kind of editorial attention. The pitch is easy because the offer is genuinely generous.

## Production Process

1. Outreach (your personal message, not a template blast)
2. Conversation (in person over tea when possible, by video otherwise). Record it.
3. AI structures the interview from the recording, with your editorial voice shaping the final piece.
4. Draft shared with guest for approval.
5. Photography (theirs, yours, or a combination).
6. Published with full attribution, links to their work, and their photo.
7. You share with your audience. They share with theirs.

## Guest Selection Criteria

Before inviting someone, ask:
- Does their work or practice genuinely contribute something to the tea world (broadly defined)?
- Would featuring them serve the TeajiA community?
- Is there a natural overlap between their audience and the people you want to reach?
- Would you want to sit with them over tea regardless of the magazine?

If yes to most of these, they're a good fit.

## Building the First 10 List

Start with people you already know and have relationships with. The first features should feel natural, not like cold pitches. Then expand outward. Each published feature opens doors to the next guest because you can show them what the magazine looks and feels like.

**Action item:** Draft a list of 15 to 20 potential guests across all five categories. Star the 10 you'd reach out to first. Prioritize people you can have the conversation with in person (Bali-based or visiting soon).

# 7. International Tea Sourcing (Strategic Note)

You mentioned you can ship certain teas directly from China and Taiwan internationally. This doesn't need to become an e-commerce operation on the website, but it opens a significant revenue channel through your space design work.

**For Tier 2 and 3 clients:** Ongoing tea supply can be built into the service package. You source and ship directly to their space on a recurring basis (quarterly, seasonally, or as needed). This turns a one-time design project into a recurring revenue relationship.

**Framing:** "We don't just design your space. We keep it alive."

**Structure:**
- Initial tea program designed as part of the project
- First supply included in the project scope
- Ongoing supply priced per delivery or as a retainer
- Seasonal rotations curated by you
- Reorder process simple (WhatsApp, email, or a standing order)

This is one of the most valuable long-term revenue streams in the model and should be positioned as a natural extension of the design work, not a separate product.

# 9. Brand Identity: Bali-Rooted, Internationally Reaching

TeajiA is based in Bali. The spaces are in Bali. The sessions happen in Bali. The studio and shop are in Bali. This is the root.

But the reach is international. The magazine speaks to the global tea community. The space design work serves clients anywhere. The sourcing journeys go to Taiwan, China, and beyond. The teas come from around the world.

**Positioning:** TeajiA is a Bali-rooted brand with international reach and relevance. You're not "the Bali tea guy." You're a global tea culture figure who happens to live and practice in one of the most beautiful places on earth, and who brings 20+ years of deep connection to tea origins across Asia.

**Implications for client targeting:**
- Local Bali community: Sessions, shop, community gatherings, events
- Bali hospitality and wellness ecosystem: Space integration, training, sourcing
- International private clients: Space design, sourcing journeys, online purchasing (teaware, incense, oracle cards)
- International hospitality and retreat operators: Full tea house design (flagship offering)

The website should feel global in its editorial voice and design quality, while making clear that the physical heart is in Bali and that in-person experiences happen there.
