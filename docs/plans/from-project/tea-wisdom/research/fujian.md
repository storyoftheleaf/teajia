## What is commonly agreed
1. Fujian makes oolong, white, black, green and scented tea; the best-known are Wuyi rock oolong, Anxi Tieguanyin, Fuding/Zhenghe white, Lapsang, Fuzhou jasmine. Agreement: 5 of 8 (Rishi, tea.travel, A Moment of Tea, Wanling, Sohu).
2. Fujian is named as the birthplace of black tea, Lapsang Souchong from Wuyi as the first. Agreement: 4 of 8 (Rishi, A Moment of Tea, Wikipedia Wuyi tea, Sohu partly). Dates not agreed.
3. Oolong is traced to Wuyi and spread to Anxi, Guangdong, Taiwan. Agreement: 3 of 8 (Rishi, Wikipedia Wuyi tea, Wanling for Wuyi/Anxi).
4. Mountains, mist and separated valleys explain the diversity; subtropical monsoon climate. Agreement: 4 of 8 (Rishi, A Moment of Tea, Wanling, Sohu).
5. English "tea" comes from southern Fujian (Amoy) pronunciation. Agreement: 3 of 8 (Wikipedia Etymology of tea, A Moment of Tea, Wikipedia Wuyi tea for Bohea).
6. Fuding and Zhenghe are the two main white tea areas; Fuding coastal, Zhenghe inland and colder in winter. Agreement: 4 of 8.
Applies to: Fujian as a province label.

## Where places disagree or are unclear
Output and value figures differ by year and source (4.61 million tonnes for 2019 in one English page is not credible against Chinese figures; 1581 billion yuan whole-chain in 2023 in another), so no figures are on the page. "Birthplace of white tea" and "1600 years of tea" are repeated but single-source or tied to place pride.

## Not established
First dates for black tea and oolong. Province rank by output (stated in Chinese news, not cross-checked). Whether Fujian has "the most" cultivars (336 in one page).
