# Northern Vietnam highlands            (entry-id: northern-vietnam-highlands)
Kind: growing area (major region)
Part of: none     Related: yunnan (not a page here)

## Chinese names
- 山雪茶 · shānxuě chá · Shan Tuyet (snow mountain) tea
- 熟茶 · shúchá · ripe tea

## What is commonly agreed
1. Old Shan Tuyet trees grow in the northern border provinces, chiefly Ha Giang and Lao Cai, with Yen Bai and Son La listed alongside; gardens mostly above about 1,000 m.
   Applies to: the region as a whole. Agreement: 6 of 9.
   Places: Path of Cha (blog, en); Han-Yi (blog, zh); Ha Giang news (govt press, vi); Lao Cai news (govt press, vi); Vietnam+ (govt press, zh); Noble Viet Tea (seller, en).
2. Shan Tuyet means snow mountain, from white down on buds; old trees, some several hundred years (estimates), tended by Hmong and Dao; some listed as heritage trees.
   Agreement: 6 of 9. Places: same six plus Goo Farm (seller, en).
3. The region is compared with Yunnan for climate, large-leaf trees and raw material.
   Agreement: 4 of 9. Places: Path of Cha, Siam Teas, Han-Yi, Moychay.
4. Ripe pu'er style tea is made there from old-tree leaf (Ha Giang mainly), medium fermentation, softer than Yunnan shou.
   Agreement: 3 of 9 for the style (Anshim, Moychay, Siam Teas, all sellers describing their own teas); Lao Cai news lists a pu'er style tea at Y Ty.
5. The pu'er name for Vietnamese tea is not agreed: Siam Teas and Moychay stress the difference from Yunnan pu'er and call it heicha-like; others use the name freely.
   Agreement: 3 of 9 that it is unsettled.

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- Altitude: 1,000 m and above; Ha Giang 1,200 to 2,200 m; Suoi Giang 1,300 to 1,400 m.
- Ripe aging years: one seller gives both 5 and 6 years for one tea.
- Tree age: "over 100", "over 200", "several hundred"; recorded, not measured.

## What people commonly notice when drinking it
Ripe tea: woody, earthy, nutty, sweet finish, softer than Yunnan shou (3 of 3 seller pages).

## Not established
- Border trade with Yunnan: only Han-Yi says old-tree tea went to China as raw material; one place only. History of Vietnamese pu'er-style making, founding dates, trade volumes.
- Whether the pu'er name is legally allowed.
- 2025 provincial mergers (Ha Giang into Tuyen Quang, Suoi Giang into Lao Cai): one place (Noble Viet Tea); left off the page.
