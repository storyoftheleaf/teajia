# Yi Ji   (entry-id: yi-ji)
Kind: leaf grade
Part of: anhua-dark     Related: tianjian, gongjian, shengjian (Anhua tips, no pages of their own yet)

## Chinese names
- 一级 · yijí · first grade

## What is commonly agreed
Nothing reached 3 independent places. Research was cut short: the shared WebFetch budget ran out after two reads (both on one site, so they count as one place).
Statements used on the page, with their real support:
1. Anhua raw leaf (heimaocha) is sorted into numbered grades by picking time and leaf maturity; grade one is the most tender of the numbered grades, with a special grade above it in at least one sorting.
   Applies to: Anhua heimaocha.
   Agreement: 1 of 2 read (same site), plus the approved Anhua dark tea page and the marks.csv record, which both say the leaf is sorted into numbered grades with the most tender going to the finest tips.
   Places: chawh.net (Chinese tea culture site, zh, http://m.chawh.net/heic/anhc/7796.html)
2. A grade describes the leaf, not the maker's skill or the place. Reasoning carried over from the approved Gong Ting page; no Anhua-specific source read.
3. Yi Ji as a general word for first grade is plain language meaning, not a researched claim.

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- The chawh page puts a special grade (teji) above grade one and divides the four grades into 16 levels; whether other makers use the same cut was not checked.

## Not established
- Which finished tea uses Yi Ji. One site (two pages) says Tianjian is mainly grade one with some grade two blended in, Gongjian mainly grade two, and gives picking windows (late April for grade one, one bud with three or four leaves). Only 1 place, so left off the page.
- Tasting notes for Yi Ji.
- Whether "Anhua Yi Ji" as a product name (2 teas in the shop) means a specific tip, a brick or a loose raw tea. Not established.
- Any national standard wording (GB/T 32719.2 or Hunan standards) for the numbered grades: not read.
