# Yuchi            (entry-id: yuchi)
Kind: growing area (township)
Part of: nantou     Related: taiwan-oolong (none for red tea yet)

## Chinese names
- 魚池 · Yúchí · fish pond (township name)
- 日月潭 · Rìyuètán · Sun Moon Lake
- 紅茶 · hóngchá · red tea; 阿薩姆 · Āsàmǔ · Assam

## What is commonly agreed
1. Yuchi is a township in central Nantou County and contains Sun Moon Lake; tea is grown on hills around about 600 to 800 m.
   Applies to: the place. Agreement: 5 of 8. Places: Wikipedia, thetea (Japanese), foodnext, teatrekker, umi.
2. Land and climate (warm, wet, misty, red soil, lake) resemble the Assam region, which is why red tea was tried here.
   Agreement: 5 of 8. Places: foodnext, moa, thetea, umi, ncnu.
3. Large leaf Assam seed was brought in under Japanese rule and Yuchi was where it succeeded.
   Agreement: 6 of 8. Places: tecompany, ncnu, teatrekker, thetea, umi, foodnext.
4. A research station at Yuchi works on red tea and cultivars (Taicha 8, Taicha 18 Ruby).
   Agreement: 6 of 8. Places: tecompany, ncnu, thetea, umi, foodnext, moa, teatrekker.
5. Export decline from the 1970s (wages, cost), revival after 1999 earthquake with local cultivars.
   Agreement: 4 of 8. Places: tecompany, ncnu, foodnext, umi/thetea (decline only).

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- Year of the Assam seed: c.1910 (umi), 1923 (tecompany), 1925 (thetea, teatrekker), 1936 (ncnu, trial in Nokoh area).
- Year of the station: 1936 (tecompany, thetea); others give none.
- Peak of exports: 1934 (foodnext), 1937 (tecompany), late 1950s to early 1960s (umi).
- Whether British tried it first (ncnu only).

## Not established
- Any founding date or "first planted" year; the "tribute to the Japanese emperor" claim (1 source); Arai Kokichiro as "father of Taiwanese red tea" (2 sources: thetea, foodnext); the 2011 origin certification (1 source).
