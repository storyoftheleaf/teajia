# Li Shan            (entry-id: li-shan)
Kind: growing area
Part of: taiwan     Related: nantou, taiwan-oolong

## Chinese names
- 梨山 · Líshān · Pear Mountain
- 和平區 · Hépíng Qū · Heping District (Taichung)
- 福壽山 · Fúshòushān · Fushoushan farm, neighbouring

## What is commonly agreed
1. Li Shan is a high mountain area in central Taiwan, in Heping District, Taichung, known first for temperate fruit (pear, peach, apple). Agreement: 5 of 9 (MOA page, Yam, Umi, Sumusen, aboxtik; Fushoushan source implies it). Places: MOA (government, zh), Yam (news, zh), Umi (seller blog, en), Aboxtik (seller, en), Sumusen (seller, en).
2. Tea gardens roughly 1,800 to 2,500 m (low end 1,450 in two). Agreement: 5 of 9. Altitude figures vary.
3. Cold nights, mist, little sun, frost or snow; slow, thick, sweet leaf; lightly oxidised Qing Xin oolong. Agreement: 6 of 9 (Sumusen x2, FoodNext, Yam, Umi, Aboxtik, Fushoushan source, Teforia).
4. Few harvests a year; cold gardens may skip winter. Agreement: 4 (Sumusen, FoodNext, Aboxtik, Fushoushan source).
5. Tea came after fruit, late twentieth century. Agreement: 3 (Sumusen, Fushoushan source, Umi/MOA on orchards).

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- County: 6 places say Taichung (MOA, Yam, Umi, Sumusen, Aboxtik, Fushoushan source); 1 seller page (Squarespace "Where is Nantou") files it under Nantou; the shop's record says Nantou. Existing nantou page already says Taichung.
- Altitude: 1,450 to 2,520 (Sumusen, Yam), 1,800 to 2,500 (FoodNext), about 2,100 (Umi), 1,600 to 2,600 (Teforia, typo in source), 1,800 to 2,200 (Nantou seller).
- Start of tea: early 1980s trial (Sumusen) vs about 1970 / mid-1970s at Fushoushan farm (Fushoushan source).

## Not established
- Who planted first tea and when; the 1960 highway date (MOA only); origin-certification mark rules (Yam only); the "above 2,000 m" rule (Fushoushan source only); counterfeit scale (one seller only).
