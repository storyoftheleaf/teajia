# Wuyi Mountains            (entry-id: wuyi-mountains)
Kind: growing area
Part of: none (no Fujian page exists)     Related: yancha, lapsang

## Chinese names
- 武夷山 · Wǔyíshān · Wuyi Mountains / Wuyishan
- 正岩 · zhèngyán · true rock; 半岩 · bànyán · half rock; 洲茶 · zhōuchá · river-bank tea; 外山茶 · wàishānchá · outer-mountain tea
- 三坑两涧 · sān kēng liǎng jiàn · three pits, two streams
- 桐木 · Tóngmù · Tongmu village; 正山小种 · zhèngshān xiǎozhǒng · Lapsang Souchong

## What is commonly agreed
1. Northern Fujian on the Jiangxi border; UNESCO World Heritage, listed 1999. Agreement: 5 of 12 (UNESCO, Wikipedia, New World Encyclopedia, Fujian government x2). Applies to: the range.
2. Steep, wet, misty; red sandstone cliffs in the east, volcanic or granite in the west; stony, well-drained soil and mist credited for the tea's mineral character. Agreement: 7 of 12 (UNESCO, Wikipedia, NWE, Rishi, Jing Tea Shop, TeaDB, People's Daily, Jiemian). Applies to: Wuyi generally; the causal link is the trade's own explanation.
3. Zone naming zheng yan / ban yan / zhou cha / wai shan; core about 70 sq km; three pits and two streams. Agreement: 6 of 12 (Jiemian, People's Daily, Path of Cha, TeaDB, Jing Tea Shop, iTeaWorld). Applies to: rock tea.
4. Wuyi makes both rock oolong and Lapsang/black tea; Lapsang from Tongmu, northern edge, near Jiangxi border; Tongmu called birthplace of Chinese black tea. Agreement: 6 of 12 (People's Daily x2, Seven Cups, Wikipedia (Lapsang), Fujian gov, Verdant search result, Wikipedia Mount Wuyi). Applies to: place, not cultivar.
5. Wuyi rock tea strictly means tea from the scenic area, local cultivars, Wuyi method (Jiemian national standard, People's Daily; yancha page already says it). Agreement: 3. Label caution built from this plus zone sources.

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- Date picking stopped on the Da Hong Pao mother trees: 2006 (People's Daily, existing yancha page) vs 2007 (TeaDB). Left out.
- Area of the UNESCO site: 99,975 ha (Wikipedia) vs 107,044 ha (UNESCO page, with buffer wording). Left out.
- Rainfall: 1,906 mm (Jiemian) vs 2,200 to 3,200 mm (Wikipedia). Left out.
- Tongmu altitude about 1,000 m (one place); not used. Origins.csv says 200-800 m for the whole record.
- Whether Tongmu lies inside the reserve: Wikipedia/UNESCO structure implies protected forest, no place states it directly for Tongmu. Page says "protected" loosely; flag for review.

## Not established
- Tongmu altitude; Wuyi tea economy figures (one source); founding dates of Wuyi tea; Danxia status of Wuyi sandstone (UNESCO page does not use the word; NWE separate Taining site). Page 'Ban yan quality' disagreement (TeaDB says weaker, others say better value).
