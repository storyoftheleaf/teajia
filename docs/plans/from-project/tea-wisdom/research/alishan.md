# Alishan            (entry-id: alishan)
Kind: growing area
Part of: taiwan     Related: nantou, taiwan-oolong

## Chinese names
- 阿里山 · Alishan · Ali Mountain
- 青心烏龍 · qingxin wulong · Qingxin oolong; 金萱 · jinxuan · Jin Xuan
- 梅山 / 竹崎 / 阿里山鄉 · Meishan / Zhuqi / Alishan Township

## What is commonly agreed
1. Alishan is a high mountain tea area in Chiayi County, Taiwan, gardens mostly around 1,000 to 1,600 m (sources range 700 to 1,800 m).
   Applies to: the area as a whole. Agreement: 6 of 8. Places: Valley of Tea, Steep Atlas, Teapark, Liao Changxing, Muyu, Nanshanju.
2. Cloud, fog, filtered sun and cool nights slow the leaf; sweet, smooth, low bitterness. Agreement: 5 of 8 (Valley of Tea, Steep Atlas, Teapark, Muyu, Nanshanju).
3. Qingxin oolong is the main cultivar, Jin Xuan also grown. Agreement: 6 of 8.
4. Tea grows in Meishan, Zhuqi and Alishan townships; the tea area is wider than Alishan Township. Agreement: 3 of 8 (Liao Changxing, Teapark, Muyu), Steep Atlas names Shizhuo and Fenqihu consistent with this.
5. Vague "Alishan" labels with no garden, height or cultivar are a warning; non-Taiwan oolong sometimes sold as Alishan. Agreement: 2 explicit (Valley of Tea, Steep Atlas) plus Teapark/Liao on sub-area detail; stated cautiously on the page.
6. Most is lightly oxidised, ball-rolled oolong. Agreement: 4 of 8.

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- Altitude range varies: 700-1,700, 800-1,600, 1,000-1,600, 1,000-1,800, 1,200-1,600.
- Fenqihu: Steep Atlas lists it as a production area; Liao Changxing says it has only one or two gardens and much tea sold under its name is from Shizhuo or Zhangshuhu.
- Seasons (spring, autumn/winter, "winter tea") are given by only 2 places: not used.
- The origins.csv record lists region as Nantou; all sources place Alishan in Chiayi County.

## Not established
- Founding or first planting dates. Soil (volcanic vs red-brown alluvial: two sources disagree). Prices. Rainfall.
