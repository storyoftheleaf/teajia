# Anhua (entry-id: anhua)
Kind: growing area (county)
Part of: none (no Hunan page exists)   Related: anhua-dark, dark

## Chinese names
- 安化 · Ānhuà · Anhua
- 益阳 · Yìyáng · Yiyang (city)
- 资江 · Zījiāng · Zi river
- 雪峰山 · Xuěfēng Shān · Xuefeng mountains
- 云台山 · Yúntái Shān · Yuntai Mountain (large-leaf tea source)

## What is commonly agreed
1. Anhua is a mountain county in north-central Hunan, under Yiyang, on the Zi river, at the northern foot of the Xuefeng mountains.
   Applies to: the county. Agreement: 5 of 8. Places: Wikipedia, China Daily EN, chinainsight, People's Daily CN, EU GI document.
2. Warm, humid subtropical climate, four seasons, heavy rain, short cold spell; deep shale-weathered soil by the river; forested mountains.
   Applies to: the county in general. Agreement: 5 of 8. Places: EU GI document, Wikipedia, helloteacup, chinainsight, China Daily EN.
3. Large-leaf Yuntai Mountain tea is the local cultivar. Agreement: 3 of 8 (EU GI, helloteacup, China Daily CN, plus Hunan gov scenic page).
4. County set up in the Northern Song (1072); tea recorded from the Tang; Ming and Qing trade north and north-west; stop on the tea road. Agreement: 6 of 8.
5. Tea is the county's main industry (since 2006 named the leading industry; about 210 firms; hundreds of thousands employed). Agreement: 4 of 8. Figures differ by source and year, so none are on the page.

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- County area, population and tea garden acreage differ by source and year. Left off the page.
- Tea road names vary (Tea Horse Road, Ten Thousand Li Tea Road).

## Not established
- Wild tea (Ancha) in Anhua: People's Daily CN says tea grows wild on cliffs and by water; chinainsight says wild tea grew there in the Song; one seller says old gardens near 1,000 m. Only loosely 3 places and none define a wild-tea grade, so no claim written.
- What "Anhua" on a label covers: the protected-origin area is reported (one document, EU GI register) to include townships in Anhua, Taojiang, Heshan and Ziyang, so wider than the county. One source, not written; worth checking.
- Yi Ji, Huangye, Bai Liang as places or makers: no source read ties them to a place within the county. Not established.
- Exact first-tea dates (Qin, Han, Tang tribute claims).
