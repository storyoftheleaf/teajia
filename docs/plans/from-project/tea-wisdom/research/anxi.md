## What is commonly agreed
1. Anxi is a mountainous county in southern Fujian (Quanzhou), north of Xiamen, in the Daiyun range. Applies to: location. Agreement: 5 of 10. Places: Wikipedia, FAO GIAHS, steepatlas, kingteamall, fjax Xiping page.
2. Climate is warm, humid, misty; soils are acidic granite-derived red and yellow earth. Agreement: 4 of 10 (kingteamall, steepatlas, jingteashop, fjax). Terraced gardens with forest above (FAO, Fujian gov, 2 places).
3. Tieguanyin is the dominant cultivar (about 64 percent of gardens per FAO). Other well-known cultivars: Huangdan/Huangjingui, Benshan, Maoxie, Meizhan. Agreement: 6 of 10.
4. Xiping is the origin place of Tieguanyin; Xianghua high and misty, strong taste; Gande newer, lower, lighter. Agreement: 5 of 10 (walker, umi, chazhidao, steepatlas, jingteashop, fjtea).
5. Origin stories (Wei, Wang) are folk accounts set in Xiping in the 1700s. Agreement: 4 (fjax, umi, kingteamall, fjtea Chinese page). Already on the Tieguanyin page.
6. Light/green style rose from the 1990s; traditional roasted was standard before. Agreement: 3 (umi, steepatlas, walker). Left to the Tieguanyin page.
7. Overseas trade: Quanzhou then Xiamen ports; Anxi families ran tea houses in Southeast Asia. Agreement: 3 (qq article, fjax Xiping, FAO/Fujian gov maritime route).
8. Protected geographical indication Anxi Tieguanyin; some tea sold as Tieguanyin is other cultivars or from elsewhere. Agreement: 3 (kingteamall, walker, Tieguanyin Wikipedia snippet via search).
9. Tea is the main income; Anxi was poor until the 1990s. Agreement: 2 to 3 (Fujian gov, umi). Phrased loosely.

## Where places disagree or are unclear
- Elevation and soil figures differ between sources (e.g. red soil band, "best" altitude 600 to 900 m vs inner Anxi 700 to 1200 m). Left out.
- Area under tea and output differ (6,700 ha vs 400,000 mu; 42,000 t). Left out.
- Dates of tea history differ (late Tang, Song, 1640 layering). Only "many centuries, local sources say late Tang" kept.
- Inner vs outer Anxi: where the line falls is not explained in 2 of 3 places that use it.
