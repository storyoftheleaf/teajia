# Teajia — Offer, Gaps, and Strategic Focus

*Business strategy brief. Cross-references PERSONAS.md and VISION.md.*

Last updated: April 2026

---

## Part 1: What You Currently Offer Each Persona

### Independent Tea Master
**What you offer:** Compass (field sourcing, vendor comparison, draft promotion), full inventory management, events with approval workflows and briefing cards, invoicing, CRM. The most complete offering of any persona.

**Gaps:** No way to be part of the network without taking on Teajia identity or running a space. No contributor profile unless they write. No peer connection to other masters — they're isolated users, not a community. No recognition of their expertise within the platform.

---

### Aspiring Operator (serious, no space yet)
**What you offer:** Magazine (content), Learn Hub (education), events (attending), shop (buying).

**Gaps:** No persistent personal identity — no collection tracking, no tasting history, no "my journey" layer. They read and attend but leave no trace and feel no belonging. This is the persona most likely to become an operator, and they currently have no pathway visible to them.

---

### Tea House Founder (building a dedicated space)
**What you offer:** The full operational suite — planned and partially built. Inventory, events, CRM, invoicing, wholesale access (coming). This is the persona the platform was designed for.

**Gaps:** Multi-account is Phase 1-3 and not shipped. There is no operator onboarding, no public location page, no guided setup. Anyone trying to use this today as an operator hits Adrian's single-account setup and gets lost. The promise exists but the door isn't open yet.

---

### Integrator (tea as secondary offering)
**What you offer:** Wholesale sourcing access (when it ships), some education content they could reference.

**Gaps:** Almost entirely unserved. There is no simple entry path for "I just want to add tea to my yoga studio." The full admin suite would overwhelm them. They need: a curated starter pack of teas, a simple way to order more, a brief education foundation, and maybe one event to start with. None of that is packaged for them. This persona needs a distinct, simpler product surface — not the full operator suite.

---

### Home Practitioner / Private Host
**What you offer:** Shop, magazine, education, events (attending). Simple event hosting exists in principle.

**Gaps:** Collection tracking is aspirational, not built. No journal. No simple "invite friends to tea" flow that feels like it's meant for them (vs. the full professional event system). No clear "this section of the app is for you" identity. They know they're not a tea house operator but they also don't feel like just a customer.

---

### Curious Reader / Online Learner
**What you offer:** Magazine, Learn Hub, free education content.

**Gaps:** The front door is broken. Magazine templates are not at quality. Newsletter signup stores email locally and does nothing. There is no "start here" path for someone new. No reason to return after a first visit beyond new content. This persona is your largest pipeline and currently your most neglected entry point.

---

### Tea Worker / Staff
**What you offer:** Scoped access to their location's tools (planned).

**Gaps:** Role expansion is Phase 1 and not shipped. Currently can't be served at all — they'd get full admin access or nothing.

---

### Corporate / Institutional Client
**What you offer:** Events (one-off sessions), consulting pathway.

**Gaps:** No dedicated B2B inquiry path. No "plan a tea event for our team" page. No package presentation. These clients exist and have budget, but they can't easily find the right door or understand what they'd receive. They need a landing path, not the general site.

---

### Collector
**What you offer:** Compass (sourcing intelligence), shop, collection tracking (aspirational).

**Gaps:** No collector-specific catalog treatment for aged or rare lots. No provenance storytelling at the level a collector expects. No way to note acquisition source, storage conditions, or drink-by estimation. Collection tracking doesn't exist yet. This persona would pay more per item than any other — they're underserved given their value.

---

### Gift Buyer
**What you offer:** Shop.

**Gaps:** No gift-framed presentation, no curated gift sets, no gifting flow (gift message, alternate shipping address). This is largely a seasonal persona but they represent discovery — the person who receives the gift may become a long-term customer.

## Part 2: Core Users — The Strong Tea Community

These are the people who will build Teajia from the inside out. They are not casual visitors. They are practitioners, teachers, masters, and serious enthusiasts for whom tea is a significant part of their life or work.

**The core community is:**

- **Tea masters and sourcing professionals** — People with field knowledge, vendor relationships, years of practice. They are the most respected voices in the tea world. Bringing them in — even just as contributors or Compass users — lends Teajia the authority it can't buy. You already know several. This is your most important network to activate.

- **Serious home practitioners** — The people attending events, building collections, going deep on tea culture. In major cities with specialty tea scenes (Melbourne, London, NYC, Taipei, Berlin, Tokyo), this community is real, connected, and hungry for infrastructure. They are not being served by any existing platform at this level of seriousness.

- **Tea educators and writers** — People producing quality tea content, teaching courses, writing books. They need a home for their voice. Teajia's magazine is that home — but only once it reaches the quality standard that would make a serious writer want their name on it.

- **First-generation operators** — The tea friends in Australia and elsewhere who are ready to use this. These early adopters are not just customers. They are proof points, feedback sources, and community anchors.

**The key insight about your core community:** They are already connected to each other through the traditional tea world — forums, events, sourcing trips, festivals. Teajia doesn't need to create the community. It needs to give the community infrastructure and a home. The network already exists. You are building the platform that makes it visible and functional.

## Part 3: How to Make People Excited About Participating

The gap between "interesting platform" and "community I want to be part of" is almost always one thing: **recognition**. People want to feel like they matter within it, that their contribution is seen, that being here is different from being anywhere else.

**For tea masters and professionals:**
Give them a profile. Let their expertise be attributed, not anonymized. A tea master contributing a sourcing note or article should have their name, background, and photo visible. Right now there's no mechanism for this. When a well-respected master in Yunnan has a profile on Teajia and their sourcing notes are visible to the community, other masters want that same recognition. This is how you pull serious people in without paying them.

**For aspiring operators and practitioners:**
Give them a tea journal that accumulates over time. Every session attended, every tea purchased, every note left — quietly building a personal archive. Not social. Not public by default. Just theirs. The moment someone has two years of their tea life recorded in one place, they don't leave. This is the retention mechanism the platform currently lacks entirely.

**For curious readers:**
Give them a reason to return and a reason to go deeper. The magazine must reach quality first — that's non-negotiable. But beyond that: a "start here" path that guides a newcomer from curious to engaged without overwhelming them. A well-designed newsletter that actually sends. Three article recommendations that assume you just arrived and want to know what tea is really about.

**For operators and integrators:**
Give them an easy first win. The first win for an operator is buying tea they're proud to serve. Not setting up their CRM. Not configuring their inventory. **Sourcing is the hook.** Every other tool becomes valuable once they have the tea. The onboarding flow should get an operator to their first wholesale order in under 20 minutes.

**For everyone:**
Make the network visible. The moment people can see that there are Teajia-connected tables in Tokyo, Melbourne, Lisbon, and New York — even if it's only 8 locations — the platform stops feeling like "Adrian's app" and starts feeling like a global community. A simple network map, even with 5 pins, changes the emotional weight of joining.

## Part 4: What to Open Up

These are aspects that currently exist in principle but are locked or invisible to most people.

**Open the magazine to contributors.** Right now it's a one-person editorial operation. Creating a clear, structured contribution pathway — with editorial standards and a defined process — gives tea masters and educators a home for their voice. You maintain quality control. They get attribution and reach. This is how serious tea people become invested in the platform.

**Open events to discovery.** Right now events are shared by direct link only. Even a simple event listing page — filterable by location and type — changes the character of the platform. It becomes a place where sessions are found, not just attended by people who already know Adrian.

**Open a lightweight path for home practitioners.** The personal journal, collection tracking, and simple event hosting (for a dinner party, not a tea house) should feel like they are for this person specifically. Give them their own entry point that isn't the full admin suite.

**Open a B2B inquiry path.** A dedicated page for corporate clients, hotels, cultural institutions — with a clear description of what a tea program looks like, what's involved, and a simple inquiry form. This doesn't need to be complex. It needs to exist.

**Open contributor profiles.** Even before a full editorial workflow exists, tea masters who contribute content should have a page that reflects their expertise and their body of work on the platform.

## Part 5: New Features to Develop

Ranked by impact relative to effort and current phase.

### High Impact, Low Effort (do first)

**1. Fix the newsletter.**
Storing emails locally and logging to console is the most quietly damaging bug in the platform. Every person who "subscribed" thinks they're connected to you. They're not. This is a direct pipeline to your most engaged visitors and it's broken. Fix immediately.

**2. Load real magazine content.**
Adrian's interviews and imagery exist. The templates need to reach quality first. But once they do, populating the magazine with real, deep content changes the public face of the platform entirely. Everything else builds on this.

**3. Post-session guest experience.**
After a tea session: teas served, their notes, purchase links. This is the moment when a guest becomes a customer. The component exists — making it guest-facing is the delta. This is the single highest-conversion feature available right now and it's sitting unfinished.

---

### High Impact, Medium Effort (build next)

**4. Personal Tea Journal.**
Tasting history, collection tracking, events attended — a quiet personal archive that persists across every interaction. Not social, not public by default. This is the feature that turns occasional visitors into people who cannot imagine leaving. Every persona benefits from this. Home practitioners, aspiring operators, serious guests, collectors — all of them.

**5. Contributor Profiles.**
A profile page for tea masters and writers who contribute to the magazine or use Compass. Name, background, attribution on their articles, link to their sourcing specialties if relevant. This is the recognition mechanism that pulls serious tea people into the platform. Even if only 10 people have profiles initially, those 10 people will tell others.

**6. Event Discovery Feed.**
A simple, non-algorithmic list of upcoming events across the network. Geographic filtering, gathering type. Not a social feed. A directory. This is the moment the platform becomes discoverable to people outside Adrian's direct network.

**7. Network Map.**
A minimal, beautiful map showing Teajia-connected spaces and sessions worldwide. Even 5-8 locations is enough to change the emotional weight of the platform. "This is bigger than one person" is the message. It requires multi-account to be partially built but even a manually-curated version at launch has enormous signal value.

---

### Medium Impact, Medium Effort (near-term roadmap)

**8. Simple B2B Inquiry Page.**
A dedicated landing path for corporate clients, hotels, cultural institutions. Clear description of what a tea program looks like. One inquiry form. Connects directly to consulting revenue. Does not require platform features — just a page and a form.

**9. Integrator Starter Pack.**
A bundled "Tea for Your Space" path — curated selection of 3-5 teas suited to hospitality or wellness contexts, a simple ordering flow, a one-page education guide, and a contact pathway. Serves yoga studios, boutique hotels, meditation centers without requiring them to navigate the full admin suite. High lifetime value if they return for restocking.

**10. Brewing Guide QR System.**
Printable cards and QR-linked pages that go off-screen and onto the tea table. Every tea in the catalog gets a brewing profile. These print on tea packages, get posted in tea spaces, and provide lasting utility that doesn't require the app. This deepens the relationship between the physical product and the platform.

**11. Gift Curation.**
Curated gift sets, gifting framing in the shop, simple gifting checkout flow (message, alternate address). Seasonal but high-discovery value — the gift recipient is a new customer. Low platform complexity, meaningful revenue.

---

### Longer Term (Phase 3-4)

**12. Collector Catalog Treatment.**
Aged lots, rare origins, and single-harvest material presented with provenance storytelling at a level that collectors expect. Acquisition date, storage notes, drinking window. This is a distinct catalog layer, not just product descriptions.

**13. Diaspora Community Features.**
For practitioners living abroad from tea-producing cultures — content in their language of origin, sourcing that connects them to home, events that honor their tradition. This is an underserved but deeply loyal audience.

**14. Training Pathway with Verification.**
Completing consulting or workshop training leads to a trusted host badge. Connects the consulting revenue to the platform trust system. Over time, this is how you scale quality control across the network without being present at every table.

## Part 6: What to Focus On

The platform is pre-launch. The instinct to build features is right, but the sequence matters enormously.

**The critical path is:**

1. **Magazine at quality + real content loaded** — The front door. Nothing else matters until this is right. It is also the single thing that brings serious tea people to the platform without any marketing.

2. **Newsletter actually works** — The most immediate trust failure. Fix this week.

3. **Post-session guest experience** — The first commercial conversion mechanism. A guest leaves an event and can buy the tea they tasted. This is the loop that sustains everything.

4. **Personal journal / collection tracking** — The retention mechanism. Once someone has a year of their tea life in the platform, they don't leave. Without it, you're building a catalog people browse and forget.

5. **First 5-10 trusted users onboarded** — The Australia contacts and tea friends who are ready. Real feedback from real users is worth more than any feature build right now. Get them in, watch what confuses them, and fix it.

6. **Contributor profiles + one real contributor** — Even a single well-respected tea master with a profile on Teajia sends a signal to the community. Who in your network would be proud to have their work published here? That is your first call.

**What to resist:** Building operator infrastructure (multi-account, wholesale catalog, onboarding flows) before the public-facing platform is worth joining. The business model is right, but operators sign up for the sourcing and stay for the tools. They only show up if the platform has credibility — and credibility comes from the magazine, the content, and the community, not the admin dashboard.

**The north star for the next six months:** Make Teajia the most authoritative, beautiful place to learn about tea online. Everything else — operators, wholesale, community — flows from that.

---

## Summary: The Three Moves

If you had to reduce everything above to three strategic moves:

**Move 1 — Earn authority.** Get the magazine to the quality standard. Load the real content. Fix the newsletter. Make Teajia the place serious tea people send curious friends to learn. This is the draw that brings every other persona in.

**Move 2 — Give people a home.** Build the personal journal. Launch contributor profiles. Make it possible for a tea master or a serious practitioner to have a presence in the platform that reflects who they are. Recognition creates loyalty.

**Move 3 — Make the network visible.** Launch the event discovery feed and the network map with whatever you have — even if it's 5 locations. The moment people can see the community, they want to be part of it. The network already exists in the real world. Give it a face.
