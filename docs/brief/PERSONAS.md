# Teajia — Audience Personas & Use Cases

*Business briefing document. For technical feature plans see `plan/`. For brand strategy see `VISION.md`.*

Last updated: April 2026

---

## Overview

Teajia serves a wide spectrum — from someone who just discovered gongfu tea on YouTube to a tea master with twenty years of sourcing relationships. These personas are not tiers or subscription levels. They are entry points. People move between them, often upward, and the platform should support every transition naturally without pushing.

---

## Persona 1 — The Independent Tea Master

**Who they are:** Deeply skilled practitioners who already have their own practice, their own sourcing contacts, and often their own clientele. They may run a small tea house, do private sessions, or teach. They are not beginners and they do not need to be educated about tea.

**What they want from Teajia:** Professional infrastructure that matches the quality of their practice — sourcing intelligence, inventory, event management, invoicing. They want tools, not branding. They may never publicly associate with Teajia at all.

**Example:** A practitioner in Taipei who hosts gongfu sessions in their apartment, buys directly from farmers, and needs better inventory and invoicing tooling.

**Key value:** Compass, inventory, events, CRM. Quietly powerful. No franchise feeling.

---

## Persona 2 — The Aspiring Operator (No Space Yet)

**Who they are:** Someone who is serious about tea and senses that it could become more than a hobby — but they don't yet have a space, a business, or a clear plan. They are building knowledge, building taste, building a vision.

**What they want from Teajia:** A home base. A place to learn, discover, track their collection, connect with a serious community. They want to feel like they are part of something real, not a wellness trend. When the time comes to take the next step, Teajia should be the natural scaffolding.

**Example:** A professional in their 30s who has spent two years going deep on tea, attends sessions when they can, and is beginning to think seriously about hosting their own.

**Key value:** Magazine, education, events, collection tracking, sourcing access. The beginning of an operator journey.

---

## Persona 3 — The Tea House Founder (Building a Dedicated Space)

**Who they are:** Someone actively planning or opening a dedicated tea house. Tea is the primary offering. They may be starting from scratch or converting an existing space.

**What they want from Teajia:** The full system — sourcing, inventory, event tools, customer-facing pages, education infrastructure for their guests. They want to open faster and operate better than they could alone. They may want to carry the Teajia name, or just the tools.

**Example:** Someone leasing a small space in a mid-size city who wants to open a serious gongfu tea room. They know tea but have never run a hospitality business.

**Key value:** Everything — sourcing, ops, education, customer CRM, events, public shop. Potentially consulting and in-person onboarding.

---

## Persona 4 — The Integrator (Tea as a Secondary Offering)

**Who they are:** A business owner in adjacent territory — a gallery, a yoga studio, a meditation center, a boutique hotel, a flower shop, a natural wine bar — who wants to bring tea into their space meaningfully. Not a vending machine and some teabags. A real tea offering that enhances what they already do.

**What they want from Teajia:** Sourcing access, enough education to serve confidently, operational tools that don't require them to become a tea expert overnight, and a supplier relationship they can trust. They want Teajia to do the hard part so they can focus on their main business.

**Example:** A boutique hotel that wants to offer a proper tea service in their lobby. A yoga studio that wants to offer post-class tea. A meditation retreat that wants to serve tea as a ritual.

**Key value:** Wholesale sourcing, education content, events (optional), Teajia's curation as the quality signal they can put in front of their clients.

---

## Persona 5 — The Home Practitioner / Private Host

**Who they are:** Someone who has no business ambitions but wants a serious tea practice at home. They entertain friends and family with tea. They care about the quality and ritual. They might host a small regular gathering.

**What they want from Teajia:** Beautiful tea they can actually get. Education that goes deeper than YouTube. A place to track what they've tried and loved. Possibly a simple way to host and invite people — not a business, just a gathering.

**Example:** A person who travels to Asia regularly, fell in love with oolong, came home and built a tea corner, and now hosts a monthly tea afternoon for close friends.

**Key value:** Shop, magazine, education, collection tracking, simple event hosting for private gatherings.

---

## Persona 6 — The Curious Reader / Online Learner

**Who they are:** Someone who found Teajia through an article, a social post, a friend's recommendation, or a search. They are not ready to buy or commit to anything. They are exploring. They read, they watch, they scroll.

**What they want from Teajia:** Good content. Real depth, not superficial listicles. The feeling that this is a place worth returning to. Trust built slowly.

**Example:** Someone who just had their first proper gongfu tea experience and wants to understand what they tasted. Someone researching puerh aging. Someone who is passionate about food culture and tea is the next thing.

**Key value:** Magazine, education library, free content that demonstrates authority without gatekeeping. This is the largest top-of-funnel group and the most important long-term pipeline.

**Note:** Most operators, masters, and practitioners started here. The path from reader to customer to operator begins with a good article.

---

## Persona 7 — The Tea Worker / Staff Member

**Who they are:** Someone employed at or helping with a Teajia-affiliated tea location. They are learning on the job. They need to set up events, manage stock, assist guests, and educate themselves without needing the full complexity of the platform.

**What they want from Teajia:** Scoped, simple access. Learn, prepare, serve, restock. No admin overwhelm.

**Key value:** Scoped inventory access, event tools, education content. Simple interfaces.

---

## Persona 8 — The Corporate or Institutional Client

**Who they are:** A company, cultural organization, or institution that wants tea programming — as a team experience, a client event, a cultural offering, or a wellness benefit. Budget exists. They are not hobbyists. They want quality and reliability.

**Example:** A tech company that wants a quarterly tea ceremony for their team. A Japanese cultural institute that wants to offer a regular tea program. A law firm that wants to gift clients a curated tea experience.

**Key value:** Events, sourcing, consulting. Credibility and polish. The ability to deliver a premium experience reliably.

---

## Persona 9 — The Collector

**Who they are:** Someone who approaches tea the way others approach wine or art. They are interested in aged puerh, single-origin rarities, antique teaware, provenance. The collection is its own thing — not just something to drink.

**What they want from Teajia:** Access to genuinely rare and vetted material. Sourcing intelligence. A way to track and catalog what they have. Content that treats the subject with appropriate seriousness.

**Key value:** Compass (sourcing), collection tracking, magazine (provenance stories), wholesale access to rare lots.

---

## Persona 10 — The Gift Buyer

**Who they are:** Someone purchasing tea as a gift for someone who loves tea. They may know very little about tea themselves. They need curation and confidence — they want to buy something genuinely good without becoming an expert.

**What they want from Teajia:** Clear recommendations, beautiful presentation, confidence that what they buy is actually excellent. Simple checkout.

**Key value:** Shop, curated collections, gifting packaging. Probably a seasonal or one-time visitor unless their recipient converts them.

---

## Gaps & Underserved Use Cases

These are not fully-served yet but represent real demand:

- **The Diaspora Practitioner** — Someone from a tea culture (Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Taiwanese) living abroad who wants to maintain their practice and connect with sourcing from home. Teajia may be their clearest link to quality and community outside their home country.

- **The Wellness Practitioner** — An acupuncturist, herbalist, or TCM practitioner for whom tea is medicine first. They need education grounded in traditional medicine frameworks, not just flavor.

- **The Event Planner** — Someone incorporating tea into weddings, ceremonies, or cultural events as a commissioned service. They need a reliable supply partner and possibly facilitation.

- **The Sommelier / Food Professional** — Someone with deep sensory training who approaches tea from a professional tasting perspective. They want technical content and sourcing access that meets their existing vocabulary.

- **The Travel-Oriented Enthusiast** — Someone planning sourcing trips, origin visits, or tea-region travel who wants expert guidance and potentially a curated journey. Pre-buying interest, post-trip ongoing purchase.
