Five quiet wrong prices

All of them live on your shelf. None of them looked like a fault, because every one of them produced a perfectly plausible number.

Each was the same shape: a default answering a question nobody had asked. The schema said dollars when nobody had said dollars, said zero when nobody had said a cost, said 2.5 when the shop charged three. A wrong price that looks wrong gets fixed in a day. These read as an expensive tea, or a cheap one.

What was wrong, worst first

About seven times too much

A cost with no currency was read as dollars

What it was doing

cost_currency defaults to USD, so a tea bought for ¥1,200 could sit in the database as $1,200 and nothing objects, because 1,200 is a fine number. The ×3 then triples the error. It surfaces as an expensive tea, never as a fault. That is exactly how the 1993 Y562 came to show a cost of $1,200 against notes saying 60 CNY a box.

What it is now

Every door that writes a cost has to say what it was paid in, the agent door included. The old rows cannot be repaired by reading, because a defaulted USD and a chosen USD are the same three letters, so instead they are now findable, and there are tools to answer them one vendor at a time.

Free

A tea added without a cost was stored as costing nothing

What it was doing

cost_amount defaults to 0, and zero is not “unknown”, it is free. Four doors each handled it differently. The worst was the agent door: it turned a missing cost into a zero before the currency check ran, so the check saw a zero, correctly decided a zero needs no currency, and waved it through. It was creating free teas in silence.

What it is now

The cost is required to add a tea, at the form, the bulk import and by voice alike. What is refused is absence, not zero: a gift really did cost nothing and typing 0 says so. The three paths that genuinely cannot know a cost now record “not known” instead of letting the default say “free”.

Your postage, at your expense

The Add Product form pinned every tea you added by hand

What it was doing

It pre-filled the shop's freight rate as a value. So every tea you added was stamped with that day's rate, stopped following it when you renegotiated, and wore the gold dot claiming you had chosen to pin it. Clearing the field saved a zero, meaning free shipping, so there was no way to say “just use the shop's”. And its placeholder still read 13.00, a rate this shop has never charged.

What it is now

The shop's live rate is the placeholder, which is what an untouched field will follow. Leaving it alone means the tea follows 85 ¥/kg and keeps following it. The price preview now charges a blank field at the shop rate instead of showing a price with no postage in it.

Two prices for one tea

A column on every product said 2.5 while your shelf charged three

What it was doing

The markup had four homes at once. Shop pricing multiplied by three, but a markup_multiplier column on every row said 2.5, and the curator listing path read that column. So the same tea carried two different prices depending on which surface asked.

What it is now

Three, in one place, read when it is used rather than copied onto rows. Migration 0013 cleared the rows still holding the old default rather than writing 3 into hundreds of them, because writing a policy number onto every row is the fault, not the fix. Curator listings moved to three; nothing on the shop shelf moved.

One tea, shipping free

The 1993 Y562 was carrying a freight rate of zero

What it was doing

Migration 0011 cleared it at 13:12. The bug where clearing that field saved a zero was not fixed until 13:25. Anything that touched the tea in those thirteen minutes wrote the zero back, and a zero there means you said it ships free.

What it is now

Cleared again by migration 0015, in two clauses so it lands whichever of the two stories is true. The full walkthrough of that one shows every row shape it touches and every one it leaves alone.
