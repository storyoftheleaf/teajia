## Verified classification (put lightly, teach people who don't know)
- **Liu bao = hei cha (黑茶, dark tea)**, China's post-fermented category (same
  family as shou puerh & Fu brick). NOT puerh, NOT from Yunnan — from GUANGXI.
- Name = Liu Bao village, Cangwu County, Wuzhou, Guangxi ("six stockades");
  named for the sixth, best-producing village. Protected Geographical
  Indication: only Wuzhou-grown, traditional-method tea may carry the name.
- Cultivar = Guangxi zhong ye (medium-leaf), larger than Fujian small-leaf,
  smaller than Yunnan da ye. Terroir: subtropical, red acidic soil, Xijiang.
- **Binlang xiang (槟榔香)** = areca/betel-nut aroma, the singular quality
  marker of aged liu bao. Gratings 1-6; historically exported grades were 3-6.
- **Processing:** wo dui (渥堆) pile ferments 45-60d/50-65°C (Aspergillus);
  then steamed & packed into bamboo baskets lined with bamboo leaves, 50g-50kg.
  Bamboo = micro-environment, gentle sweetness, long aging.
- **Sheng vs shou puerh (Yunnan):** sheng = raw (kill-green, roll, sun-dry,
  ages naturally, years; young brisk/bitter → mellow with age). shou = ripe
  (wo dui wet-pile, technique stabilized early 1970s; smooth, ready fast).
  80s sheng = four decades to become itself; 90s shou = mature wet-pile era.

## Verified history (sprinkle, no fabrication)
- **Malaysia link:** mid-19th→mid-20th c Hakka migrants to Malaya tin mines +
  rubber worked under heat, humidity, standing water; liu bao = daily necessity,
  called "life-saving tea." If a mine didn't provide it, it couldn't attract
  workers (Legend of Tea). Ipoh = the "second hometown of liu bao."
- **Tea Ship Route (茶船古道):** Wuzhou down Hejiang → Xijiang → Guangzhou →
  Hong Kong → SE Asia (Ipoh, KL, Singapore). 1951 China Tea News documents
  export from Wuzhou via Guangzhou to HK, most to Ipoh/KL. 1950s-90s exports
  exceeded 1,000 t/yr to SE Asia. Returned/回流 stock ages 10-30+ yr in humid
  Malaysian warehouses → darker, smoother, developed character; sought back
  in Guangzhou trade.
- **TCM / "good for":** cools + aids digestion; dispels dampness (祛湿),
  expels heat & humidity, regulates spleen/stomach, warming and cooling;
  post-ferment → probiotics → gut. Miners used it vs heatstroke, rheumatism.
- **HK-style liubao:** HK firms (incl. Shen Chang/Si Jin Qian) historically
  produced from Guangdong/Vietnam/Myanmar/Indonesia big-leaf for SE Asia,
  called da ye in Malaysia. Re: our two HK-aged — frame as BENEFICIAL proper
  humid aging (complexity, depth, transformation). NEVER moldy.
