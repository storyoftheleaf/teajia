# Teajia — the pillar

> What this site is and how it works, in one read. Written 2026-09-20 from a full survey of the code (four agents, every public and signed-in route, the worker API, the git history of the home page). Linked from the top of `CLAUDE.md` so every session sees it. When the site changes shape, change this file in the same commit; a pillar that lags the code is worse than none.

## What it is, in three sentences

Teajia is a home for fine tea: a place to source it, study it, and share it with the people who gather around the cup. It is one person's twenty years of sourcing relationships, turned into a shop, a magazine, a reference library, a calendar of sessions, and a set of professional tools that other tea people can run their own practice on. It is not a wellness app, not a social network, not a SaaS dashboard, and it never sits at the tea table.

## The four words a visitor is given

The whole public site hangs off four sections, and they are the mobile tab bar, the desktop sidebar, and the four grounding lines on the home page. Same four, three renderings, kept in sync by rule.

| Word | Route | What is actually there today |
|---|---|---|
| **Read** | `/read`, `/article/:slug` | The Art of Tea reading room. A hand-built list of fourteen long-form pieces, of which four are live to a visitor (ritual, atlas, tasting, porcelain and tea) plus the flagship leaf-to-liquor piece. Editor-written articles from the database render at `/article/:slug` and are what event recaps become. |
| **Craft** | `/craft` | The learning hub: a course, glossary, journeys, playlists, videos, visual guides, tea spaces, community wisdom. All static content in the repo. Links on to Tea Wisdom. |
| **Advise** | `/advise`, `/for-your-space` | Adrian's consulting page, built in the Read/Craft immersive frame: three services (design, sourcing, sessions), each explaining who it suits, Adrian’s work, and the first step. Service links open a guided inquiry with two tailored multiple-choice questions, useful starting advice (including for visitors who are unsure), and contact details last; projects and sourcing journeys show as coming soon until the portfolio is built. The inquiry form posts to `/api/inquiries`, recorded for the admin Activity view; the space form composes a WhatsApp message. |
| **Shop** | `/shop`, `/shop/product/:id` | The live catalogue from the worker: tea, teaware, sets, a collection tab. Products open in an alcove over the grid, or as a full page from a shared link, and show which tea master selected them. |

Behind those four, the phone reaches them through the site panel (since 2026-09-22): the left end of the bottom bar is a three-line glyph that raises a panel the way the right end raises Your Table. Left door is the site (search, sessions, people, places, tea wisdom, cart, about, and the Manage rooms for anyone who has them), right door is you, the four words stay between them. Search left the bar to make the slot; it sits first in the panel and in every page header. The panel and the desktop Manage column read one list, `src/components/manageNav.ts`, so they cannot disagree about which rooms exist.

- **Events** `/events`, `/event/:slug`, `/event/:slug/recap`: live sessions with seats, RSVP, waitlist, a public tea menu, the hosts in their own words, and a recap afterwards. "Sessions" in the site panel; on desktop only signed-in surfaces link here. This is the one genuinely time-bound thing the site has and the desktop nav does not show it.
- **People** `/people/:slug`: the tea masters and contributors, each with a profile, favourites (which they can name into a collection with its own page from their own account page), and a private pay page behind a gate. A person also appears on the pages that are not theirs: the store they host (`/store/:slug`, the people at that table), the sessions they host, and the teas they selected. Rail foot, site panel and footer.
- **Tea Wisdom** `/wisdom/*`: the reference: cultivars, regions, producers, marks, styles, named teas, tea types. Site panel, footer and Craft.
- **Spaces** `/spaces`: the network of tea rooms, each with a WhatsApp line. "Places" in the site panel; the desktop rail still says "Connections" until the connections page is decided.
- **Discover** `/discover`: a questionnaire that gives a visitor a level and threads to follow. Linked from the home page.
- **Start** `/start`: seven path cards for a newcomer. Nothing links to it.
- **Store fronts** `/store/:slug` or a subdomain (au.teajia.com): a partner's own shop, events and identity.
- **About** `/about`, **MCP** `/mcp`: thin and orphaned respectively.

## How a purchase happens

There is no card checkout, by decision. A visitor fills a cart, gives a name, a reply contact and an approximate delivery destination, then submits on the website. The site saves a pending request and draft invoice; opening WhatsApp or pressing Send in another app is not required. Adrian confirms the tea, delivery and payment personally. In Sales, opening the saved order brings shipping and payment management together: enter the delivery charge, destination and tracking beside the payment ledger, with the balance refreshed after saving. A blank shipping quote is refused; an entered zero means pickup or free delivery. Shipping charges lock after dispatch, while tracking can still be corrected. Payment and shipment are separate: confirmed payment can allocate the purchased stock while the order still awaits dispatch, and adding shipping later must never deduct that stock twice. The visitor follows the request at `/order/:ref`. A customer can explicitly opt into a WhatsApp order confirmation from the shop’s existing business number. It is sent to that customer, with their private order-status link; Coexistence can mirror the outgoing conversation into the owner’s WhatsApp Business app. This is not an incoming alert to the owner. Automatic delivery stays disabled until the same-number provider connection and approved template are ready. Orders without consent still save normally. See [WhatsApp order setup](WHATSAPP_ORDER_SETUP.md) for configuration and delivery-state limits. Every order is a conversation. The shop copy should say so plainly, never apologise for it.

**Agent-led purchases and arrivals (2026-10-28, no new migration).** An invoice finalized through MCP creates connected purchases and expected receipts by supplier; confirming a Curate order creates the same expected-delivery records. Recording physical arrivals through the agent or browser uses one guarded operation for stock, ledger, quantities and purchase state, including partial deliveries and safe retries. Purchases combines incoming tracking with compact supplier rows, Coming, Received and Needs attention filters. Opening a purchase reveals item quantities, Stock links, original invoice and arrival history. Manual receiving stays under Make a change. Partial delivery is normal; unfinished invoices, unresolved matches, older unlinked records and overdue deliveries remain visible for attention. Saved invoice evidence opens read-only in place; unfinished invoices retain a separate Review invoice action. The Stock Incoming entry opens this same tracking view. Invoice entry never implies that goods have arrived or that a private holding should be published.

**Supplier → logistics → studio (local implementation, migration `0052` required).** Purchases tracks each supplier's inbound courier and tracking number separately from a combined air or boat shipment to the studio. Each logistics company groups supplier parcels beside its onward shipments on wide screens, and in that same order on phones. Arrival checks and shipment preparation act within that company and the selected supplier; shipment contents are visible with a direct link back to the purchase. Parcel contents and paid costs, warehouse holdings, company rates, and outbound destination, ETA and freight calculations stay visible. Services, packing and corrections use focused task screens; there are no accordions. Chinese-and-English message drafts can be edited, copied or saved for the logistics company. Suggested packing volumes and freight estimates use the same calculation in the overview and message, without changing a confirmed bill. Pasted company messages are entered by agents into the matching purchase or shipment, retaining their source, reported time and history. Saved warehouse and destination addresses are reused explicitly and copied onto each route so later address-book edits cannot reroute an existing delivery (migrations `0053` and `0054`). This is message-based tracking, not a continuous courier feed. Goods arriving at logistics, being grouped, or being dispatched never increase shelf stock; only explicit verified studio quantities do. Logistics services keep reusable company rates in their quoted currency, per kg, per m³ or per shipment. A shipment retains its selected quote while its final bill and packing measurements can be corrected. Unknown costs remain unknown; planning volumes and freight shares based on them are labelled estimates. CSV and printable photo lists retain supplier costs, item quantities, reference links and inbound/outbound tracking. The browser also supports grouped hub-arrival checks, Mark selected ready, outbound tracking and direct Receive all at studio with editable quantities; these actions never wait for agent review. Actual shipment freight is accounting information, separate from the shop's existing retail freight policy. See [Purchases and logistics](PURCHASE_LOGISTICS.md) for the agent workflow and limits. This extension is not yet deployed.

**Curate → Stock → sale (implemented locally, 2026-10-28).** A capture keeps its quoted amount, currency and quantity basis together. Buy saves the capture before proposing a receipt; Library resumes that same purchase. Stock receipt is an explicit, repeat-safe acknowledgement of physical possession, and publication is a separate choice. Intake keeps incomplete rows and purchase follow-ups until confirmed. Network Carry and wholesale receiving connect their listings to the same local Stock holding. Tea moves in grams, teaware in pieces; neither a missing cost nor a missing exchange rate becomes a zero price. Confirmed payment consumes stock once. Cancelling an unshipped paid order restores it once, reverses the sale settlement and leaves a visible refund due; recording an actual refund clears the due without erasing payments. Shipped orders cannot be cancelled through this path. Customer history follows the authenticated buyer across shops, with a private tracking-link claim for guest orders. The agreed invoice, original request and current refund state remain distinct on tracking. Migration `0031` and deployment are required before these local repairs are live.

## What a signed-in person gets

**One sign-in, everywhere.** `/signin` handles Google, password and email code for the public site, Your Table, event reservations and Manage. Entry points carry only a validated `returnTo`; without one, sign-in opens Your Table at `/account`. The account panel and RSVP sheet contain no separate login form. `/signup` owns account creation and email verification and keeps the same return destination. Google always returns through `/signin`; an event return reopens the unfinished reservation without submitting it, and admin returns retain filters, anchors and pending consent requests.

**Your Table** (the person glyph at the right end of the mobile bar, the same glyph in the rail's foot on desktop) is the person's own room, and since 2026-09-22 only that. A visitor sees Discover, Read, Shop, Find a Table. A member sees a day line (for someone who runs a shop it names what is waiting, with one line that opens Today in Manage, where the list lives), and tiles that are all theirs: journal (tasting journal), sessions, remember (favourites, and collections shared with them), cellar (tea they own, with grams, placeable at a location), discover, orders (what they bought, and samples sent to them), journey, profile (name, sign-in, appearance, and the public Tea Master identity for those who have one), and switch when they sit at more than one table. Since 2026-09-29 each of these things has one tile: samples, shared collections and account folded into orders, remember and profile, and the platform owner's walk-throughs into library. Anyone with a Manage room gets exactly one more tile, "manage", the door to the shop; it appears only once the token is confirmed for the active table. Nothing else from Manage is named here, so the shop's rooms have one name each, in the Manage column and the phone's site panel, both read from one list.

The personal records are deliberately separate and stay that way: **Journal** is what you tasted and thought, **Favourites** is what you want, **Cellar** is what you own, **Journey** is where you have been (sessions, teas met, seals). No streaks, no scores, no feed.

**Manage** (`/admin/*`) is the operator's room. Its overview, Today (the old Dashboard, owner only), opens from the word Manage at the top of the column and starts with waiting requests, prices, payment reports and paid orders to send, followed by stock checks, low/incoming stock, fulfilled sales and customer follow-up. Inventory valuations sit below the action lists; currency, region and type breakdowns are available under Explore the inventory. Since 2026-09-29 the column is seven rooms named for the job, in the order of a working day: Curate (bringing tea in, with Samples, Purchases and Network); Stock (the shelf, with Drafts and Tea Glossary); Sales (tabs Waiting, Orders, Wholesale, Ledger; Waiting holds pending orders, RSVPs and inquiries, Ledger holds the stock ledger, activity log and sold-out archive); People (tabs Customers, Suppliers, Tags); Sessions (with Tastings and Venues); Publish (opens on the magazine, with Collections, Guest notes, Wisdom, and Tea Masters for the owner); Settings (with Members and Audit; an access manager who is not an owner lands straight on Members). A room's own screens show under it only while you are in it, on the laptop column and the phone's site panel alike. The Settings page carries the doors to Members, Agents (voice and agent tokens), and for the platform role Rates and Platform. The Tea Atlas left the column; it is read from Your Table and the site panel. The plan for the rest of the regroup: [manage-regroup.md](../todo/plans/archive/manage-regroup.md). Each room shows only to someone its route admits, and each route carries one word everywhere it is named, the phone's admin bar included. Every write is gated by one of six capability bundles: catalog, stock, publish, gather, sell, members. On a phone, Stock is a list grouped by supplier (or kind, stage, or nothing), with totals on expanded group headings; tapping the heading folds it while keeping the tea count and grams visible beside the supplier name; the retail total folds away, the arrow menu holds supplier editing, and selecting teas puts selection actions above the bottom nav. The top has two compact lines ([stock-phone-by-supplier.md](../todo/plans/stock-phone-by-supplier.md)). The laptop keeps the ledger.

Stock keeps Type, Supplier, Stock view and Tasting directly available as independent browsing choices. All saved views remain accessible, grouped by purpose; changing one filter preserves the others and the current arrangement. Search, grouping/order and a state-aware expand/collapse icon fit in the first header row. Incoming and the tasting library are in the actions menu. Closed supplier groups retain the name, tea count, grams and retail total; missing suppliers read Unknown and can be assigned per tea. Cards stay single-column on small phones, become two columns from 680–767px, and switch to the full spreadsheet at 768px. The spreadsheet keeps its original column order and proportions with quieter cell dividers, aligned numeric headings, and no repeated year beside a name when Year has its own column. Naming, photo, tasting, form/pricing, stock and full editing use the same records and actions as before.

Curate’s Library is the phone-first preparation workspace: supplier-grouped tea cards use the Stock’s Fine metalwork finish with subtle tea-type shading, full-width titles, editable given names, tea types and years, and three equal pricing fields beside photo and tasting icons. One extra slim row holds Choose, Reject and Add information. Supplier editing uses a pencil icon. The display currency stays separate from the recorded quote currency. Unknown per-gram prices display 0.0 without changing stored costs. Add information and the three-dot details control open one bottom sheet with Tea, Notes, Orders and Files tabs instead of accordions. Verified linked records provide direct navigation between Library and Stock. Orders contains ordered, received and incoming quantities plus existing delivery controls; Notes holds photos, voice, source material and public-description tools. Taste opens an immediately visible tasting shell while the wheel renderer loads separately, with a visible header Save action, a separate impression control, the Four chambers Effect layout, and motion that respects reduced-motion preferences. Reviewed AI extraction reads the same linked Source notes and voice transcripts, proposes missing facts, and changes nothing until each detail is selected and applied. A paired local AR Create connector lets its owner search talks by title or phrase, link a full transcript or reviewed passage with its original identity/version, and use the same review. Description proposals show exact source quotations and replace the public description only after explicit acceptance. Originals and Inventory publication remain separate. Source captures evidence; Ledger owns purchase drafts, confirmations and accepted arrivals. Explicit transfer creates a private Inventory draft and moves the tea out of the active Library into Transferred. Inventory opens the same linked tea record and starts reorders through Ledger; confirming an order never adds physical stock.

A tea can receive a chosen name while it is still being considered. `tea_compass_entries.given_name` is separate from the supplier's source name; naming does not create inventory, choose the tea, or alter price or stock. When the encounter becomes an inventory holding, the chosen name follows that same linked identity. Same-account name edits from either Library or Inventory stay synchronized, and clearing the given name preserves the original source name. This requires migration `0048_compass_given_name.sql` before the corresponding UI is released; the current working preview has applied it only to its isolated local database.


## How it is built

- **Accounts.** Every account is its own store: catalogue, stock, prices, currency, customers, invoices. Kinds are platform (Adrian), location (a tea room), master (a tea master with no address). One database, every table scoped by account id, one gatekeeper that resolves the acting account on every request.
- **Curate to stock.** A sourcing sample marked in Curate, in the app or through an agent, has one linked shelf portion in its vendor's open sourcing batch. The server owns sample lifecycle synchronization; a tasting never invents stock or a purchase decision. Reviewed supplier arrivals create private drafts with the tea's details and the exact order line's batch cost. Agents can also promote a Curate tea into a private, zero-stock inventory Draft before arrival, retaining its existing identity and samples. Vendor quotes alone never become batch costs. Recorded freight remains separate from the shop's freight setting.
- **Curate workspace.** Supplier facts have typed tea, vendor and quote fields; private role-labelled files attach to those records and arrivals. Stock's Samples only filter shows received/tasted Curate portions without full stock, opens their editor and reviews tasting deductions or orders. Authorized shop managers and agents share account-wide records, with actor history and guarded undo of a selected change. Photo unlinking preserves backups; agents can separately preview Drive trash or restore. [Operating details](CURATE_WORKSPACE.md).
- **Network.** A tea profile has an originator and a curator. A store carries a network tea by making its own listing over it. Cross-store movement routes through the platform account.
- **Money rules** (in full in `CLAUDE.md`): freight is a per-kilo rate on the account, inside the ×3 markup; one exchange-rate table refreshed daily; nothing entered is NULL and zero is zero; a tea is not created without its cost and its currency.
- **Events** are a closed state machine: draft, published, registration closed, completed, archived. A completed session can become a recap and then a magazine draft.
- **Agents.** Roughly seventy authenticated MCP tools (stock, sales, customers, intake, events, collections, articles) with preview-then-confirm on every write, plus four public read-only tools for the shopping public. `.mcp.json` connects a Claude session; the token lives in Infisical.
- **Stack.** React 19, Vite, Tailwind with CSS-variable tokens, Zustand for client state, React Query for server state, Cloudflare Pages for the site, a Cloudflare Worker on D1 for the API. Live at teajia.com (Pages project `teajiafinal`); the API at api.teajia.com.

## The boundaries, so nobody rebuilds them

No streaks, gamification, engagement notifications, algorithmic recommendations, social feeds, likes, followers, auto-replenish. No live tasting mode. No card checkout. Curation is by hand. Journal, Favourites, Cellar, Curate stay separate. Nav labels and routes change only with explicit confirmation.

## Where the detail lives

`docs/INDEX.md` routes everything. `docs/VISION.md` is the philosophy and the five layers (tools, sourcing, education, scaffolding, community). `docs/STATE_OF_THE_SITE.md` is the verified snapshot. `docs/SITE_MAP.md` lists routes by tier but its home page line is stale (it describes the April build). `CLAUDE.md` carries the styling rules and the money rules in full.


The tea record’s **Build this tea** workspace separates **Working material** (complete AR Create transcripts, attributed extraction, private notes, voice memos and research) from the **Public description** written from reviewed evidence. Desktop keeps both regions visible in independently scrolling columns; phones use the same reading order with a compact header and actions. Full transcript review processes every supplied character in overlapping parts, preserves exact source quotations and marks ambiguous tea references. While open and connected, full references check AR Create every thirty seconds and on return to the workspace. Changed originals refresh derived working notes after the first review; public drafts remain intact and must be reviewed again. Connection failures keep the last material visible with an explicit warning. The connector's computer must be available for current originals. Search, full review, recordings staged as text and description proposals do not save tea fields, notes or AR links. Audio uses existing private transcription/retry storage. **Save accepted changes** retains only chosen links, working notes, missing fields and the chosen public description in the same tea record; **Discard changes** leaves them untouched. Source edits never publish a description automatically.

AR Create's Transcript Library can initiate linking through the signed-in Teajia destination `?tab=library&arSource=SOURCE_ID`. Teajia reads the complete original through the paired connector, shows existing references and lets the person confirm one or several existing tea records. Before linking, **Review tea workspace** opens the existing tea with the full transcript staged beside Working material and Public description. This preview writes nothing until Save accepted changes. The receiver’s separate Link selected teas action writes source references only; it never creates a tea or changes a description. The review uses the same dialog focus and interaction stack as Tea details, so opening it from a drawer keeps every control usable. Many transcripts can build one tea, and a transcript may be linked to several teas. Transcript reader placement and installed AR Create activation remain owned by the AR Create project.

## Development-only Library review

The Library-only preview handoff is documented in [LIBRARY_PREVIEW.md](LIBRARY_PREVIEW.md). It gives the approved localhost preview an expiring, revocable read-only snapshot capability, never an admin login. Publishing the handoff is separate from publishing a Library design.

The account tasting flow on phones uses a floating site-style capsule: Texture, Effect, Taste and Storage, with a central emblem to leave through the existing save/discard protection. Storage records dry-to-wet character and unwanted notes; Appearance keeps liquor colour and clarity together beneath it. The opening Your impression view combines Love/Like/Neutral/Dislike, purchase intent, collection, a single-row rating and editable notes. Admin voice transcription appends to that same note. The centered tea-name header returns to this view; its compact recap links each chosen word back to its options. Texture and Effect show related vocabulary together without a prescribed order. Taste uses the approved full-size wheel: exploring a family records nothing, while Record Floral (or another family) saves a broad resemblance without requiring a flower, sensory channel or intensity. Specific taste/aroma halves remain independent. The five tastes is a category inside the same wheel: Sweet, Bitter, Sour / tart, Savory (umami) and Salty are directly selectable without Taste/Aroma halves. It distinguishes sip sweetness from honey-like resemblance and retains existing saved values. Returning sweetness is not offered in Taste; earlier saved values remain stored. Unwanted Incense belongs under Storage; existing saved flavor notes remain readable. These additions use the existing tasting JSON, without a database migration.

**Stock uses compact Reference Columns cards below 768px and the spreadsheet above.** Each tea keeps a separate container, its given name (Unnamed until entered), type, editable year, stock and retail close together. The Fine metalwork finish uses a fine tonal outline and a restrained tea-coloured strip, with Dark in brown and Shou in charcoal. The strip gives the name the remaining space on the left, with form and type grouped at the far right. The three-dot action sits within the title row. Camera and Chinese teacup icons sit flush left; Year, Stock and Retail values are centered above their labels in the remaining width. On-hand stock always displays the stock quantity, including on sample cards, without embedding a purchase quote. Camera and Chinese teacup actions open photo capture/library and the existing tasting flow; saved tastings reopen for editing, and notes stay inside tastings. The form opens a bottom sheet for piece/pack weight and the purchase-cost basis. Purchased grams determine cost per gram; piece weight determines full/half-piece retail; current stock remains separate. The whole-piece sales setting stays explicit. More holds freight, stock history, journal and the full editor. Photos fill an empty slot or explicitly replace one, and failed saves retain edits for retry.

Supplier name corrections are shared corrections: Edit supplier name in the supplier group’s arrow menu opens the rename flow for every linked tea and admin record. Editing a supplier contact’s name uses the same atomic correction. Choosing a different supplier for one tea remains a separate, explicit selection; typing and leaving the picker does not create or assign a new supplier. Supplier primary names are English or romanized; the optional original Chinese spelling lives in Chinese name on the supplier card.
