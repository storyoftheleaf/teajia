# Named-tea extraction notes

Source: `node scripts/audit-wisdom-coverage.mjs` output (185 tea products, 110 unresolved names), read alongside every product write-up whose name is a word or phrase rather than a tea-style description. Result recorded in `data/tea-wisdom-source/named-teas.csv`.

## The naming tradition itself

Two distinct traditions surfaced.

**1. Chinese private-collection naming (the dominant one, 14 of 17 rows).** In mainland Chinese tea culture, serious private collectors who store aged tea for years or decades will sometimes give a stored tea a name of their own choosing, e.g. a virtue, an emotion, a cosmic reference, rather than a place or grade. The name records what the tea meant to the person who kept it, not where it grew. Because the tea moved through private hands rather than a documented supply chain, the mountain, village, and often the vintage are never disclosed. This is stated explicitly in `products/sheng/courage.md`: "This tea arrived already named. Courage carries meaning from the hands that stored it, a private collection in mainland China where the practice of naming stored teas is common among serious collectors." The same framing (collector chose the name, provenance beyond the province is unrecorded) recurs verbatim in `wisdom.md`, `true-love.md`, `inspiration.md`, `mercy.md`, `passion.md`, `new-rainbow.md`, `old-rainbow.md`, `universe.md`, `galaxy.md`, `miracle-fate.md`, `true-love-new.md`, `tea-masters-cake.md`, and (a variant, naming an undocumented trade/export history rather than a personal virtue) `french-brick.md`. All 13 of these trace to the same vendor thread, `HangJia`, and are flagged `personal: true` in frontmatter, i.e. Adrian's own private holdings rather than shop stock.

**2. Chinese poetic/proverbial tea-naming (2 rows, a different and older convention).** Separately, Chinese tea culture has a long-standing practice of naming a tea for the feeling of its origin or its aging philosophy rather than a technical specification, e.g. "deep in the clouds" for a high-altitude white tea, or the aphorism "the older, the more fragrant" for a tea made with long storage in mind. `products/white/yun-shen-chu.md` states this explicitly: "It is a poetic name in the Chinese tradition of naming teas for the feeling of their origin rather than their technical specifications." `products/white/yue-chen-yue-xiang.md` uses a well-known pu-erh/white-tea proverb as its name. Both trace to vendor `Yunan Fam` and are restockable shop stock, not personal collection pieces, so I marked their provenance `partial` rather than `undisclosed`: the write-ups state a broad terroir character (high-altitude Yunnan cloud forest) even though no specific garden or vintage is given.

**Orange Master Box** is a third, minor variant of tradition 1: no name was ever given by a person, only a description of the box it arrived in ("An oolong known only by the box it came in... the most honest form of anonymous tea"). I logged its tradition as "anonymous or packaging-only naming" since calling it "private-collection naming" would overstate what's known, though it comes from the same HangJia/personal thread as the rest.

## Count and sourcing summary

17 named teas, all vendor `HangJia` (14, the private-collection group) or `Yunan Fam` (2, poetic/proverbial group), plus Orange Master Box counted within the HangJia group. No write-up names a specific proper-noun collection (e.g. "the Chen collection"); all just say "a private collection" generically, so the `collection` column is blank throughout, per the instruction to leave it blank rather than invent one.

Provenance split: 15 `undisclosed` (mountain/village/vintage unrecorded, even where the province is stated), 2 `partial` (Yun Shen Chu, Yue Chen Yue Xiang: broader terroir character given).

## Borderline calls (excluded, with reasoning)

- **Huakang Oolong** (`products/oolong/huakang-oolong.md`, HangJia, personal): the write-up hedges identically to Tea Masters Cake ("The Huakang name may refer to a brand, a location, or a maker"). I excluded it because "Huakang" has the two-character proper-noun shape typical of an actual Taiwanese tea company or brand name (parallel to Xinghai, Hengfengyuan, Qiaorui, all of which resolve to producers.csv territory), whereas Tea Masters Cake's name is a plain descriptive title with no brand-like proper noun. This is a judgment call; if Huakang turns out to be undocumented as a producer, it belongs in this CSV too.

- **Wan Zhi 1980** (`products/shou/wan-zhi-1980-1980.md`, vendor Lidia, not HangJia/personal): the name ("ten thousand branches") is genuinely undocumented in origin, but the file carries a `givenName` frontmatter field equal to its own product name, the same field used elsewhere (Liu Bao, Sheng Puerh 1990, Purple Bud Sheng, etc.) for Adrian's own supplementary mood-label on top of an already-identified tea. I read this as belonging to that separate "Adrian's own poetic overlay" system (out of scope per the task's opening correction) rather than the collector-arrived-already-named system, so I excluded it. Flagging here because the write-up's language ("a specific production batch" left undocumented) is close enough to warrant a second look.

- **Dragon Shou**, **Elephant Village**, **River Town Sheng**, **Pasha Village**, **Shui Island Mengqing**: all read initially as candidate given-names but each write-up either (a) names an actual place (a village or county within Yunnan, the same convention as Menghai or Yiwu, just less famous) or (b) states the naming is a known industry-wide convention rather than a one-off collector choice ("Dragon-themed pu-erh cakes are common in Chinese tea production, often created for festivals or commemorative purposes," vendor Yunan Fam, restockable, i.e. repeatable shop stock, not a unique private piece). Excluded as regional/format naming, not identity-only given-names.

- **Liao Fu** (5 variants across `products/dark/`), **Xiaguan Wild 2005**, **Xinghai Aged Sheng Cha**, **Golden Bull Hengfengyuan**, **Qiaorui Gong Jin**, **Zhong Cha Pai Yuan**, **Blue Seal Green Label**, **Red Seal Camphor Puerh**, **Yellow Label Tie Bing**, **Tongqinghao 1980**, **Wild Round Tea Classic Brand**: all are producer or historical-mark names, confirmed against `data/tea-wisdom-source/producers.csv` and `marks.csv`, which already carry Xiaguan Tea Factory, China National Tea Corporation (Red Seal, Blue Seal Green Label, Zhong Cha), etc. Excluded, per the instruction that other holdings handle these.

- **Ruicaokui**, **Zhuangyuan Hong**, **White Needle Golden Lotus**, **Bai Liang**, **Xiao Qing Gan**, **Qi Zi Bing**: real historical/regional Chinese tea style names (confirmed several already sit in `styles.csv`, e.g. Xiao Qing Gan, Qi Zi Bing, Bai Liang/Hua Juan). These describe a known style or grading convention, not a one-off collector's private name for an otherwise-unidentifiable tea. Excluded per the instruction not to include names that describe the tea itself.

- **Sheng Puerh 1990** (givenName "Hidden Treasure") and the other ~24 products carrying a `givenName` frontmatter field distinct from `productName` (Liu Bao/"Inward", Liao Fu Wan Yan AAA/"Silent Forest", Purple Bud Sheng variants, Hong Cha 1979, Ginseng Puerh 1976, Gushu Sheng 2023, 7572 Menghai, Zhong Cha Pai Yuan, etc.): these are teas whose primary identity already resolves (generic type, year, sometimes producer/mark) and which additionally carry a supplementary poetic label in the `givenName` field. This is exactly the "shop owner's own poetic labels" mechanism the task's opening correction distinguishes from the collector-naming pattern; it is not in scope. None of these were added to the CSV.

## What was NOT touched

No edits were made to `src/wisdom/**`, `scripts/**`, `producers.csv`, `styles.csv`, or `marks.csv`. Only the two new files listed above were created.
