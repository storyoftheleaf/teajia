# How a Wisdom page is written

The one written place for how Wisdom pages read: style kits, place pages, grades, producers, named teas. Every agent that writes or rewrites a page reads this first ([the briefs, step 2](agents/TEA-WISDOM-AGENTS.md#step-2-research-and-draft-each-missing-page-sonnet-one-agent-per-page)). The house voice underneath it is [PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION_STYLE.md](PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION_STYLE.md); where the two differ, this file wins for pages.

**How this file grows.** When Adrian sends back a section on the review page and ticks "Make this a rule for every page", his note becomes a rule here, in his words where possible and dated. Every page written after that follows it, and pages already written are reworked when they next come up for review. One rule, one place: never keep a page rule in a prompt, a brief or a memory instead.

## The rules

### 1. Say why people care, not only what happened (Adrian, 2026-11-27)

A page answers the question a tea buyer actually has: why does this place, style or grade matter, and what is it about it that people value? For a place that means its nature and ecosystem (the forest, the climate, the old trees, the soil) as much as its history. History belongs on the page, but as the reason the place is known, not as a timeline.

*Adrian's note, on Menghai County:* it should talk a little about the history, but why do people like Menghai? Is it the ecosystem, the nature, or just the history?

### 2. Few numbers (Adrian, 2026-11-27)

Too many numbers are not relatable. Keep a number only when the reader needs it to understand the tea: the decade ripe pu'er began, say. Leave out recipe codes, altitude ranges, founding years and counts unless the number is the point of the section. One number in a section is plenty; two is the limit.

### 3. Simple (Adrian, 2026-11-27)

Write it the way you would tell someone at the table: short sentences, everyday words, one idea per sentence. If a tea term is needed, say what it means in the same sentence.

## Before and after

The rules above, applied to Menghai County's "Where ripe pu'er began" section.

**Before:** Menghai Tea Factory was among the Yunnan state factories that brought wet piling into production in 1973. Its 7572 ripe cake, usually dated from 1975, became a reference recipe for the style. The number reads as a recipe: 75 for 1975, 7 for the main leaf grade in the blend, 2 for Menghai.

**After:** Ripe pu'er was first made at scale in the 1970s, and the Menghai Tea Factory was one of the places it began. One of its early ripe cakes is still the recipe other ripe teas are measured against, which is a large part of why Menghai's name carries weight in ripe tea today.

(The rewrite drops three numbers and keeps the one a reader needs; the recipe code moves to the named-tea page for that cake if one is written.)
