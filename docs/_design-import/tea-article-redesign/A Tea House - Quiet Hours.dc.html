Quiet Hours

How a homesick cup and a stack of reclaimed timber became a tea house at the far end of the world — told by the two people who made it.

The bowl & the plane

On a wide grey street in Brunswick, between a tyre shop and a bakery, there is a doorway most people walk straight past. Inside, the noise of Melbourne falls away. This is Quiet Hours — a tea house that began, improbably, with a homesick woman, a year in Bali, and a builder who had never finished a pot of tea in his life. I came to ask how a room gets made. It turns out it takes two very different people, talking past each other in the most productive way.
