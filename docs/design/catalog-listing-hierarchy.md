# Where things go on a Teajia listing

**Approved reading order for the standalone shop page · November 23, 2026**

Let a shopper see the leaf or piece, understand what they are looking at, then decide whether to taste it, buy it or read its history. For tea, the first editorial space belongs to the tea and the cup. For an object, it belongs to the piece in the hand or on the table. Both use one page construct.

## The page at a glance

| Reading order | Tea | Teaware |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Find your way | Back to Shop and category at the top left. View Order and its state at the top right. | Same. |
| 2 · Recognize it | Plaque with name, original name and specific type. Square leaf photos, then thumbnails. | Same plaque with the object category. One large identifying view. Thumbnails switch between front, base or mark, and alternate views. |
| 3 · Understand it | A sentence about this tea. **Taste** gives the approved flavors of this lot. **From the tasting journal** sits immediately beneath the taste box, outside its border. | A sentence about this piece. Lead with its form and the feature a buyer could recognize in the photos. A useful detail about handling or pouring belongs here when someone has actually observed it. |
| 4 · Decide | **Provenance** groups the known facts. The order area groups availability, amount, price and Add to order. | **The Piece** groups the known facts. Lead with form, capacity and condition where available. Order by the piece. |
| 5 · Discover the story | The named tea’s own history first. Then relevant place, maker, processing or brewing. Link each reusable passage to its evidence in Tea Wisdom. | The history, making and use of this piece first. Then a confirmed form, clay or maker can lead into Wisdom. |
| 6 · Follow a connection | Associated teas, articles, events and **Selected by** when present. An attributed style-level flavor reference is the **last reading on the page**. | Suggested teas, related articles and **Selected by** when there is a recorded relationship. |

The contribution controls sit with the photos and serve the whole listing. Keep the controls compact enough that the first editorial text is about the item. Availability and ordering have their own place and remain reachable during a long read.

## The opening on a tea page

**Start with a useful sentence.** Name the tea in ordinary language. Choose a detail that helps the reader recognize it, such as the loose leaf, a small hollow tuo or a particular village. The plaque already supplies the title. Avoid a second opening that repeats the title, Chinese name, type and year as a list. When only a name and type are known and the plaque says both, go directly to the next populated section.

**Give flavor the first reading position after that sentence.** Lead with what the author noticed in the cup. When available, a short sequence from aroma to flavor to finish gives more information than a string of adjectives. Body describes how the liquid feels in the mouth. “How I felt” belongs to the person who tasted it. Use only exact-lot words supplied by Adrian or explicitly approved by their guest author. A row of approved descriptors works on its own. Use middle dots only between terms that share a line.

A taster might identify stone fruit, citrus peel, toasted grain, flowers or spice. Keep the specific word they used. If they describe “apricot on the first infusion, then a honey finish,” the sequence is worth keeping as a sentence. These are examples of vocabulary, never defaults for a tea type.

**Keep the person’s voice intact.** Put a selected journal passage immediately under the taste box, with the author’s name. When there are no eligible descriptors, the passage can stand on its own after the introduction. Where the same words are both the tasting and the excerpt, show them once with their byline. “Why I love this tea” and “what I found unique” are possible subjects of the author’s passage. They need no empty fields on the product page. A curator can feature an offered excerpt on the listing, the main page, or both. Withdrawal removes the public placements. The author’s journal is where they record and edit the experience.

**When we know the type but have no lot tasting**, the opening can still explain what the leaves are, their format or what makes this tea identifiable. Go straight to the known facts or story. If there is an approved, cited style reference, place it last and name its scope, for example **What people often notice in ripe pu’er**. This gives a beginner a point of reference and lets an experienced reader move on. A blank tasting field says nothing about whether Adrian tried the tea.

## Gallery and account controls

| Situation | Recommended placement and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Shop photos exist | The shop gallery is the first content below or beside the plaque. Put one visible **+** beside the thumbnails, also when there is just one photo. The accessible label says **Add your photo**. Selecting a photo enlarges it or switches the featured view. |
| Only member photos exist | Keep the member photo row under the plaque, with attribution available on the photo. Put the one **+** beside those photos. |
| No photos exist | Collapse the image area. In its place, **Log in** and **+ Add your own picture** sit beside one another. For a member, the account control occupies the login position. No empty frame. |
| Visitor taps **+** | Open account creation with a return path to the same item. Keep a sign-in path for someone who already has an account. Browsing, reading about the item and opening Wisdom are available to a visitor. |
| Signed-in member | **+** opens the photo picker for this item. Uploads start private. The member controls sharing. Their personal cover preference applies to their own view. Keep **Taste** and **Collect** in the compact action group for tea, **Collect** for an object. |
| Visitor wants to write | **Add your tasting** on tea leads into the account and tasting flow for this lot. The feeling prompt lives in the journal. |
| Owner | Keep **Edit** and listing management subject to the owner’s permissions. **Drafts** is the signed-in management list for all work in progress. Publication is an explicit owner action. A review photo, approved claim or complete layout cannot itself publish a listing. |

For tea, use one square leaf view on a phone and at most two large square leaf views on desktop. Additional images become thumbnails. A wrapper can help identify a tea during preparation and can serve as identified evidence in Wisdom. A wrapper-only intake group stays a private reference. For objects, the full silhouette comes first. A base mark, handle, spout, lid or condition detail earns its place by showing something the first image cannot.

## The facts and the order

**Tea.** The compact panel answers where, when and in what form. Use a single Vintage field for a year, a range or a documented age. Give the shopper distinct destinations for the tea family and its geography. Pu’er is a tea family on a family link. Menghai County is a geographic reference. A more precise verified location can sit beside the county. Show cultivar, producer, mark, grade or elevation at the scope the evidence establishes. The village’s elevation or the age of trees in nearby gardens belongs in village reading unless verified for the lot.

**Objects.** Put recognition and use first within The Piece. Form, measured or supported approximate capacity, and a specific condition observation help a shopper judge fit. Next come verified material or clay, fired color, maker, mark and date. Acquisition place, previous owner, use history and dating basis can follow when relevant and authorized for display. Keep the private acquisition record in Stock. A visible inscription may be described before its maker can be identified.

**Purchase.** Put the actual buying choices together. Tea gets the available units or gram quantities and their prices. An object gets its per-piece price and available units. Keep View Order accessible in the existing navigation. On mobile, allow the existing order dock to remain reachable without covering the reading. A sold-out listing can still teach and accept eligible contributions. Show **Sold out**, or **Ask about availability** where the shop has configured that action. A missing price or deferred physical count never becomes a zero price or stock for sale.

## Layout across screen sizes

On a phone the plaque is compact. Photos, contribution controls, the item introduction and the cup or object detail follow in one reading column. Facts flow into the available width, and long place names wrap inside their cells. Keep the author icon beside their name. Empty groups take up no space.

On desktop retain the plaque at left and a compact information and reading column at right. Use the extra width for the second leaf view where available and for margins around the page. A second sidebar of repeated facts or a wide line of historical prose makes the tea harder to read. Keep a single column for long passages and a compact grid for facts. Use ordinary links to deeper Wisdom pages.

## Applying the approved order

The shared page places the item introduction and eligible cup or object details above the compact facts panel. Named history leads the historical reading below the facts. Earlier versions displayed Wisdom before the cup and could use uncredited tags or inferred potential character. The approved opening takes only evidence eligible for this lot.

The shared standalone layout owns the sequence and contribution controls. Tea and teaware supply content to its positions. The quick-view card can summarize the same verified identity, image, cup or object detail, price and availability, linking to the full listing for the long read.
