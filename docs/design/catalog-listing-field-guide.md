# How to populate a tea or object listing

**Editorial companion to the [approved page hierarchy](catalog-listing-hierarchy.md).** Use this during intake, writing or a requested review of a batch. The primary keys are the Stock lot or piece ID, the Wisdom identity and, for a personal contribution, the author and the item they selected.

## Which record supplies each part

| Shopper sees | Where it comes from | What an editor decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name, Chinese name and plaque label | Existing Stock product or given name, original name, recorded tea type or object category | Keep both names when they add meaning. Preserve meaningful recipe codes such as Y562. Use **Shou Puer** or **Teaware** at the edge of the plaque. Drop the redundant “Tea” suffix. |
| Shop gallery | Ordered, approved Stock gallery for that lot or piece | Choose an identifying main image. Use an additional view for a different detail. An intake photo becomes a shop photo only after the match and image choice are confirmed. |
| Member photos and **+** | Item-linked uploads, the viewer’s own records and photos eligible for sharing | Respect the contributor’s privacy, framing and attribution. A shop editor’s choice of a shared lead photo is separate from approving a Stock gallery. |
| First sentence | Public Stock identity, format and confirmed lot facts. A reviewed description may supply the wording. | Choose one thing that tells a beginner what is in front of them. This opening is optional. Its words should add something beyond the plaque. |
| **Taste** | Approved exact-lot tasting language, including verified structured flavor terms or explicitly approved wording from the taster | Retain the author’s distinctions among flavor, aroma, mouthfeel and finish. Select a few useful words. Authenticate legacy tags before promoting them. AI can organize supplied observations. A family name, recipe or place supplies no flavors to this slot. |
| **From the tasting journal** | The author’s exact offered passage and byline, curator approval and placement choice | Choose whether it appears on the listing, main page, or both. A guest can say why they love a tea or how they felt. Preserve the speaker and brewing context. Group an attributed difference of opinion with the person who experienced it. |
| **Provenance** on tea | Stock vintage, origin, cultivar and form. Confirmed Wisdom relationships supply family, type, region, named tea, producer, grade or mark. | A direct area link needs the right locality. Offer family and geography separately. Give vintage and age one field. Attribute regional growing conditions in Wisdom. An original pack weight can be useful purchasing context only when the actual lot match is known. |
| **The Piece** on teaware | Stock form, material, capacity and object details. A reviewed Yixing record may supply an owner-confirmed form, clay or fired color to the private preview. | Describe visible shape, writing and damage at the level the photo supports. Record the basis for a date or maker. A shape match, fired color or similar auction photo alone cannot identify clay composition, authorship or age. Publishing a confirmed fact to a shop item requires the correct Stock identity and public listing decision. |
| **About this piece** / use | Verified description, construction, use history, condition, pouring notes or pairing notes for the particular object | Where observed, say what a buyer can use, such as the capacity that was measured or how this lid sits. An object's shape family can have a Wisdom article even when its individual maker is unknown. |
| Amount, price and availability | Shop’s current sellable Stock and commerce settings | Let the existing order system compute totals and currency. Do not copy prices into editorial prose. A quoted historical basket or package weight stays in history. Drafts with missing counts stay in review. |
| Named history and general reading | Approved Wisdom claims at their documented scope, linked by verified lot identity or matching attributes. Lot-specific sourcing or craft can also come from verified Stock prose. | In the history group, go from the named item to its place, maker or process. Use a specific passage title such as **The three cranes**. Link the title to the cited section and give readers a path back to the listing. Keep the full story when each passage adds evidence. Resolve overlap with existing About copy. |
| Brewing / suggested teas | Supplied method or trial for this tea, or recorded pairing guidance for this object | A tested dose, vessel, water or infusion can be useful. Name the context. A tea type alone cannot establish a recipe for this lot. Link to relevant teas only when a real match or deliberate pairing exists. |
| **From the table**, **Follow the thread**, **Selected by** | Linked events or articles, useful shop filters and recorded curator selections | A selector’s name credits a selection. A tasting byline credits a cup. Carry over neither role by implication. Preserve the store and the originating item when following links. |
| **What people often notice in [style]** | Approved sensory reference with public sources, matched at the style or grade level | Keep this optional reading at the end. Name the sample or source where appropriate. Use “often” only when the evidence supports frequency. A single documented example can instead be titled **One maker’s account of [style]**. |

## What the first reading would actually say

**San He Liu Bao · a real lot without an approved cup note.** The shop records Wuzhou, Guangxi, a 1990s vintage and loose leaf. A suitable opening is *“This is loose Liu Bao from Wuzhou in Guangxi. San He means ‘Three Cranes,’ the mark of Wuzhou Tea Factory.”* The compact facts panel can supply the decade. Then **The three cranes** can begin with new information about Hegang Tower and the October 1991 registration. The July 1959 export circular describes a color buyers requested in tea from Cangwu. It belongs in trade history. It gives no evidence about the liquor in Adrian’s cup. Omit the taste box and journal byline until a taster supplies eligible words. [Brand register](https://lzhbwg.mofcom.gov.cn/edi_ecms_web_front/thb/detail/748abf88616c408a9790ed760bfca3dd)

**A tea with both a tasting and a featured note · illustrative wording only.** Suppose the taster approves *dried apricot* and *honey* for one lot and separately offers *“What I loved was the way the sweetness stayed after the second cup.”* The opening shows **Taste** with **Dried apricot · Honey**. Beneath its border, **From the tasting journal** carries the exact sentence and the taster’s approved byline. Provenance and named history follow. Withdrawing the sentence removes the excerpt and byline. The descriptors stay only if they have independent permission. If the excerpt is the sole approved material, show that once and omit the separate descriptor row.

**Pot 04 · a private object with a confirmed design.** In the owner preview the identifying sentence can read *“A six-sided teapot with an angular spout and the inscription 長樂.”* Lead with the full pot, then use the base and alternate views for inspection. Form and inscription can link to the documented 六方井壺 comparison. This identification supports the design and writing. The reference pot’s maker, date, clay and 170 ml capacity cannot populate this piece. The unconfirmed Stock match, physical count and price keep the order area and public listing closed. A future confirmed capacity and condition report belong above the shape’s longer history.

**A simple public object.** The Caomu Temple White Clay Side Handle has two useful structured fields, white clay and 750 ml. Those facts tell a shopper more at the top than an essay about clay. Keep handles, pouring behavior, condition and an individual maker’s story only when the piece has evidence for them. [Caomu Temple White Clay Side Handle](https://www.teajia.com/shop/product/caomu-temple-white-clay-side-handle-750ml)

## What to do when data is sparse

| Situation | Result in the proposed layout |
| --- | --- |
| Name and type only | Plaque, compact account/photo action and any valid commerce controls. One specific, matched Wisdom reference can add a useful reading. Repeating the plaque in prose adds nothing. |
| Approved cup, no photo | The empty-gallery action sits beside the account control. The cup becomes the first editorial reading. |
| Guest excerpt, no shop tasting | The signed excerpt stands under the opening. Its first-person voice and placement can change only through the author/curator workflow. |
| Rich named history, no tasting | Short introduction and known facts, then the full named history. Flavor on a style reference remains at the end with its source. |
| Sold out or private | A published sold-out tea keeps the reading and eligible contributions. A private draft and its media are available only to the authorized owner. |
| Clay suggestion or uncertain lot match | The owner review retains the suggestion, evidence and approve/deny choice. The public facts panel uses only confirmed public facts. |

## Where the recommendation belongs

- **Product design** owns the one reading order in the [listing hierarchy](catalog-listing-hierarchy.md). The shared layout is the implementation point for both categories.
- **Catalog and research** use this guide when composing Stock copy and Wisdom passages. Research and owner review can run in batches on demand. Publication remains a distinct decision.
- **Tasting** owns the author’s original words, offer, byline and withdrawal. **Tea Wisdom** owns reusable historical and style claims, citations and revisions. A listing projects the approved material from each source.
- **Future UI verification** should compare one sparse tea, one authorized tasting with and without a featured excerpt, one priced object, and one owner-only draft on phone and desktop. Check the empty-gallery plus, exact-cup ordering, withdrawal, a narrow wrapped descriptor row, and a round trip through Wisdom.
