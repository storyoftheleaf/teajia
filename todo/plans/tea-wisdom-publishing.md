# Tea Wisdom across Shop, Read and tasting

Build plan · updated 2026-11-23. Uses [Tea Wisdom](../../docs/TEA_WISDOM_BASE.md) for reference authoring and the [existing editorial workflow](../../docs/CONTRIBUTOR_EDITOR.md) for article drafts and publication.

## Outcome

Tea Wisdom holds the approved, editable knowledge about tea types, leaf grades, material, places, makers, styles, and processes. Research and AI drafting enrich those entries once. Product pages populate readable context from matching entries. Articles use those entries to compare, explain, and teach. Optional tasting suggestions draw on the same sensory vocabulary. Adrian's own tasting, when recorded, leads the description of that exact lot.

## What belongs in an entry

- Identity and aliases, with clear distinctions between axes. Gong Ting is a leaf grade. Menghai is a growing place when the lot records it as such. Old-tree and wild-arbor labels describe material claims and need their own evidence.
- A concise description plus substantive sections for history, terminology, selection or processing, and sensory range wherever the evidence supports them. Add brewing or comparison guidance where it helps the reader.
- Claim-level sources with author or publisher, exact source location, sample or batch and relevant conditions, and whether the source is a producer, taster, historical source, or study. Preserve the attribution when describing differing observations, including reports that disagree.
- Stable entry and passage IDs, review state, and revision. Keep private material and source excerpts within their authorized scope. Published summaries use original wording and citations. Capture refreshes produce reviewable changes against the current text.

## How readers use it

| Surface | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Shop | Use the strongest supported matches from recorded product attributes. Show a short, substantive explanation in place and link to the relevant Wisdom entry or comparison article. Use broader type context when the precise match is unsupported. Keep sensory wording scoped to the grade, region, or cited examples. Give the lot's personal tasting first position when it exists. |
| Wisdom | Hold the full entry with sources, related subjects and approved deeper reading. Examples may link back to eligible public lots. |
| Read or Craft | Draft a comparison of documented ripe pu’er leaf grades from the relevant Wisdom entries. Explain how to compare like for like, show attributed examples, and link back to the definitions and public teas readers can explore. Use the existing article review and release path. |
| Tasting | Offer optional flavor words from the applicable Wisdom entries. The person chooses what they noticed and can use their own words. Their saved tasting belongs to their session. |

An article should record which Wisdom entry revisions it used. Referenced fact blocks can display the current approved version when intended. A change that affects a published article's editorial prose should flag it for review. When a claim is withdrawn, remove its shared projection and identify published prose that still cites it.

## Portable pilot examples

- **Naka Gushu** uses the live lot’s recorded type, cake form, and Mengsong/Menghai origin. Suggested grid-linked text: “TeaDB places Naka in Mengsong, one of Menghai County’s tea-growing areas.” Keep maker history in Wisdom and omit sensory context in this example. [Public lot record](https://www.teajia.com/api/products/public/dddd11d2-7357-4d91-86e8-52a99bb47c51) · [TeaDB, Mengsong section](https://teadb.org/menghai-county/#:~:text=A%20Mengsong%20village)
- **Documented Gong Ting** uses the external Jingmai loose ripe example. Define Gong Ting as a bud-rich grade in the Menghai group standard for loose ripe pu’er. If the final style reference mentions sensory differences, credit the seller’s fruity creaminess observation on the Jingmai example and its fruit and light hardwood observation on the aged Nannuo cake. This exercise does not establish the grade of any Teajia lot.
- **Type-only ripe pu’er** supplies only the recorded type from the public input. Describe wet piling in plain language. An optional last style reference could cite Yang Hongyan and colleagues, who compared twelve ripe pu’ers from four areas and found different changes in floral and fruity aromas across their samples. [Public source record](https://www.teajia.com/api/products/public/d8b6bcc0-e5d1-4ed1-8cab-67b3bd62d0dc) · [Yang et al., 2025](https://www.spgykj.com/en/article/Y2025/I5/218)
- **The comparison article** opens with the Jingmai and Nannuo examples, then explains what the Gong Ting, special grade (特级), and seventh grade (七级) rows of Table 5 say about loose material. For the exercise, compare two documented grades using the same dose, water, temperature, and steep times. Compare dry leaf, warmed-leaf aroma, and first and third infusions. Record observations before reading the source notes.
- **The tasting interaction** starts with the exact lot and a private note. The author deliberately offers an exact excerpt and byline. Approval starts with both placements off. The editor can choose either, both, or neither. The author can withdraw the offer and preserve their private note. An edit to the public excerpt or byline goes back to the author for approval.
