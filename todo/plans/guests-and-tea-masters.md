## The model in one screen

**Everyone is a guest. A session has a host, and others may pour beside them.**

Wherever this file still says "tea master" for someone at a session, read "host" or "pouring". The file keeps its name so that links hold.

| Word | What it means from now on | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Guest** | Anyone at a session who is not pouring. Away from a session, everyone: you read, you put things on the tea table, you keep favourites, you find tea. | A lower rank. A signed-out visitor is called a visitor, not a guest. |
| **Host**, and **pouring** | The host created the session and is its master of ceremonies. Others may be pouring beside them. The same person is a guest at the next one. A session says "hosted by" and "pouring", and never calls anyone a master. | A rank, or a permanent class of account. |
| **Tea master** | A master of ceremonies, not a master of tea. Adrian, 2026-11-26: "Tea master has been master of ceremonies, not mastery over tea." It names someone who holds a table well, and says nothing about rank or knowledge. It stays the word Adrian grants on a person's page, as decided 2026-11-19, and wherever the site explains the word it says this in one plain line. It is not used for a role at a session (Adrian, 2026-11-26: "We don't want to create any weirdness around that word, to call someone a master"). | Something a person can call themselves. |
| **Session** | A time when people sit down for tea. It names its host, anyone else pouring, where it is, its seats and its teas. It is either an ordinary sit or a tasting. | A place. |
| **Table** | A tea master's own place to pour, usually their home. Private. The public sees only the area, and a confirmed guest sees the address. | Something listed or searchable. |
| **Tea house** | A public place where people can sit down and drink tea. It has a keeper and chose to be seen. Tea masters pour there, including travelling ones who have no table of their own. | A tea master's home. A shop: a house may also have one, and many will not. |
| **The Tea Table** | What people chose to share after sitting or tasting, in time order. | A feed to scroll at the table. |

Three things were tangled and are now apart: **who a person is** (the word on their page), **what they are doing at this session** (tea master or guest), and **where it happens** (a public tea house or someone's private table).

The word on a person's page stays as Adrian decided on 2026-11-19: Teajia family for anyone, tea creator and tea master granted by him. Pouring one session does not change that word. It adds the session to the person's page under Sessions.

## The loop

The whole site is one circle, and every section is a part of it.

1. **Invite.** A host opens a session and sends its invitation to people they know, before the day. The invitation is to the session, not to the site.
2. **Sit.** People meet at a table or in a tea house. The app asks for nothing. Someone may take a photograph.
3. **Put on the table.** Afterward the host sends a thank-you. Guests add their words, their photographs, and what they thought of the teas.
4. **Keep.** Guests and tea masters keep favourites: articles, teas the shop has, teas in their own Tea Chest.
5. **Find the tea.** A favourite or a tasting leads to the tea itself, in the shop or from the person who holds it.
6. **Host.** A guest who wants to pour opens their own session and invites people. They are its tea master.

Someone who is only ever online still has steps 3 to 5 in full: read, taste at home, share, keep, find tea. The site should be worth their time without a single session, and should keep quietly showing them step 6.

## The six core functions

Adrian, 2026-11-26: "The goal is to use it at many different levels. One level is just managing who's coming and the directions to the house: event invitation management. So when he says confirm it shows a walkthrough on how to get to the house. That should be a really good looking, sophisticated system. Then one is to share the tea, one is to share the playlist, one is to actually sell the tea, and one is to let the guests interact with tasting the tea and building their profile, and another is to have photos put together. These are the core functions to build it around."

A session is built from six functions. **Each works without the others**, so a host uses as many levels as they want, and the first one alone is a complete, useful thing.

| # | Function | What it is | Works alone? | Built today | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Invitations and arrival** | Who is coming, seats, the door, and after a guest is confirmed, a walkthrough of how to get to the house | Yes. This is the level most hosts will start and many will stay at | RSVP, approval, address shown after approval. No group invitation and no walkthrough | 2 |
| 2 | **Share the tea** | The teas poured, each with its name and, where it has one, its page | Yes | A session can carry teas; the post-event form lists them | 3 |
| 3 | **Share the playlist** | What was playing, as a link the guests can open | Yes | A playlist appears in the post-event views | 3 |
| 4 | **Sell the tea** | A poured tea that is for sale says so, with its price, from the shop or from the host's own shelf. What is bought is tracked to the host | Needs function 2 | Shop checkout; the host's storefront; no tracking to a host | 3, then 9b |
| 5 | **Taste and build a profile** | A guest notes what they thought of each tea. Those notes build their own page and journal over time | Needs function 2 | The tasting screen, the journal, the Share step, family pages | 4 |
| 6 | **Photos put together** | Everyone's photographs of the session gathered in one place, for the people who were there, and on the Tea Table when shared | Yes | Photographs per tea and per person; Guest Book photos reviewed by the host. No gallery of a session | 4 and 5 |

The thank-you after a session is simply functions 2 to 6 delivered together: the teas, the playlist, what is for sale, the ask for tasting notes, and the ask for photographs. A host who used only function 1 sends a thank-you that is just their own words.

### Function 1 in detail: invitations and the arrival walkthrough

This is the front door of the whole product, and Adrian wants it to be "really good looking" and "sophisticated". It has to be better than a WhatsApp message with a pin, or nobody will switch.

**For the host**

- One screen to make the session (a day and a time), one link to send.
- A plain list of who is coming: confirmed, asked and waiting for a yes, and seats left. One tap to confirm or decline. One tap to message a guest in WhatsApp.
- **The walkthrough is made once per table and reused for every session there.** The host walks it once with their phone: a photograph and one line at each point that matters. "Turn at the warung with the blue sign." "Park here." "The gate is the wooden one on the left, it is unlocked." "Shoes off, come straight up." A map pin for the start. Each photograph goes through the one photo editor, as the standing rule requires.
- Optional notes that travel with it: what to bring, what to wear, when to arrive, whether to eat first, a number to call if lost.

**For the guest**

- Before the host's yes: the day, the area, the host, and seats open. Nothing that locates the house.
- At the moment of the yes: the page changes. It now shows the walkthrough, step by step, large photographs, one at a time, with the map pin and "open in maps" at the top. It reads well on a phone held in one hand on a scooter stop. It can be saved to the phone and works without signal, because the last part of the way is often where signal ends.
- One tap adds the session to their calendar. One tap says "running late" or "can't come", which frees the seat and tells the host.
- After the session, the same page becomes the session's record.

**Privacy.** The walkthrough is the most sensitive thing in this plan: it is photographs of the way into someone's home. It is shown only to confirmed guests, only until the session has ended, and it is never on a public page, never in a recap, never on the Tea Table. If a host removes a guest, that guest's page stops showing it. A link passed to someone else shows nothing, because the walkthrough belongs to the confirmed guest and not to the link.

**Why it sells the rest.** A host who comes only for this, to stop answering "where is your house?" six times, is now one tap from listing a tea, and a guest who opened the walkthrough is already holding the page where the tea list and photographs will appear.

## A session: before, during, after

This is the heart of the build. One session page carries all three, and it changes with the clock.

| When | The host | A guest | What the page shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before** | Opens the session, names who else is pouring, sets the seats, sends one invitation link | Opens the link, gives a name, has a seat. No sign-up needed | To the public: the day, the area, the host, seats open. To confirmed guests: the address and who is coming |
| **During, an ordinary sit** | Pours. May add the teas as they are served | Nothing is asked. The camera is one tap for the space, a pot, the mess at the end. A video now and then | Nothing new. The app sends nothing and asks nothing |
| **During, a tasting** | Has chosen "this is a tasting", so writing is the point | Notes each tea, on paper or in the app | The teas in order, one at a time |
| **After** | Sends the thank-you: their own words, the teas poured with links, the playlist, and three asks | Answers any, all or none: how the teas felt, a few words on what it was like, photographs they took | The session's record, for those who were there. What each person chose to share appears on the Tea Table |

**Why a guest answers the thank-you.** They get something first: the list of what they drank, each tea with its page, its price and whether any is for sale, the playlist, everyone's photographs, and the host's note. Their own words and pictures complete a record that is theirs to keep in Journey. It is a gift exchanged, never a form.

**Why a host sends it.** They get their guests' words about what it meant, photographs of their own session, and a page that lets the next guests "look in and get excited about the future ones".

**Seats.** The host and confirmed guests see who is coming. The public sees how many seats are open and never a list of names. The events decision stands: no public attendee lists.

**What is already built for this:** RSVP and approval, the after-sit link, the Guest Book entry, the post-event form with the session's teas, the tasting screen, a host's recap, and a structured tasting the host opts into. A playlist appears in the post-event views. What is missing is one invitation for a group, more than one tea master on a session, the thank-you as a single composed message, and one screen for the guest's answer.

### Hosting has to be really easy

Adrian, 2026-11-26: "I want it really easy to host the event. Maybe some people won't show their tea in it. Maybe some people will."

**The whole of what a host must give is a day and a time.** That makes a session and its invitation link, ready to paste into WhatsApp. Everything else is optional and can be added before, afterward, or never:

| Optional | If left out |
|---|---|
| A name for the session | It is called by its day and its host: "Saturday at Mei's table" |
| The teas | The session has none listed. The thank-you simply has no tea list, and nothing nags the host to add one |
| Seats | No limit is shown |
| The place | The host's own table if they have one, otherwise "ask the host" |
| The door | By request at a private table, by link otherwise |
| Others pouring, a photograph, a few words | Not shown |

The test for the build: from tapping "Host a table" to holding a link takes one screen and under a minute, and a host who never touches the session again still has guests who could take a seat and a record of the day in their Journey.

Teas are worth adding because of what they unlock, not because the screen demands them: the guests' tea list in the thank-you, links to the teas, and credit when one is bought. A host adds a tea by typing a few letters. A Teajia tea is found first, a tea from their own Tea Chest second, and any other tea is just a name they type, with no page behind it. A host who would rather not show their teas shows none.

By voice it is one sentence: "Saturday at four at my table."

## The phone at the table

The first draft of this rule said "thirty seconds, then away". Adrian corrected it on 2026-11-26: people take a picture of the room midway, of a pot they liked, of the mess at the end, and some sessions are tastings where everyone writes. "We're not trying to control people, but we don't want people in their mind or on the app."

> **The app never asks during a session.** It sends nothing, prompts nothing and shows nothing to scroll while people are sitting. What a person chooses to do is quick: the camera is one tap and saves by itself. A tasting is the one kind of session where writing is the point, and the host chooses it.

What follows from it:

- Taking a seat from an invitation: open the link, give a name, done.
- A photograph during a sit: one tap to open, one to save. Words, crop and sharing wait until later.
- The thank-you is sent after the session ends, by the host's hand, not on a timer during it.
- The Tea Table, Read, the shop and the people list are not linked from a screen meant for the table.
- In a tasting, each tea is one screen, and paper is as welcome as the app. A host can enter a guest's paper notes afterward, or the guest can.

This is a proposal for the standing rules. It would replace the phrase "never during (no phone-at-the-table features)" in `CLAUDE.md` once Adrian confirms the wording.

## Quiet acknowledgement

Adrian wants active people respected without anyone feeling they must post often or attend often to earn a title. He calls it "a slight bit of gamification without being obvious". The rules against gamification stand, so this is a proposed third exception, and it is made only of things one person gives another.

| What | Who gives it | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| A thank-you after a session | The host, to each guest | Automatic |
| Being quoted: your words on the session's page and the host's page, with your name | The host chooses the words | A count of how often |
| "Photograph by" on a picture that is used | Whoever uses it | A tally |
| "Poured with" on a session, when you served beside the host | The host | A rank |
| The word on your page: tea creator, tea master, founding member | Adrian, by hand, for what a person has grown | A threshold reached |
| A place nearer the top of the family list | Order only, as decided 2026-11-21 | Explained anywhere |
| Your own Journey: the tables you sat at, the teas you met | Yourself, privately | Shown to anyone else |

Nothing here counts, levels up, expires or nudges. No number of sessions, posts or photographs appears on anyone's page.

## Favourites, as Adrian described them

One idea in three kinds, the same for a guest and a tea master:

| Kind | What it is | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | Pieces you want to keep or recommend | Saved stories exist but are the open question in track 1. This answers it: make them real |
| Teas the shop has | Teas you chose, each with a line on why | Built, private and public |
| Teas in your own Tea Chest | Teas you hold and love, whether or not the shop has them | The Tea Chest is private. Showing one as a favourite is new |

Each favourite is private until its owner shows it on their page, as now. A shown shop tea keeps "Selected by" on the tea's page.

A Tea Chest tea has three states, in Adrian's words of 2026-11-26, and its owner picks one: **kept** (private, the default), **shown** as a favourite and not for sale ("not served"), or **offered**, when a tea master lets people buy some for a while. Offered uses the pack and storefront that exist, with the full payment going to that person. Shown says plainly that the tea is from the person's own chest and has no price.

The four personal records stay separate, as the pillar requires. A favourite is a pointer to an article, a tea or a chest tea. It does not merge the journal, the chest and the journey.

## A tea master's shelf, and credit for tea sold through them

Adrian, 2026-11-26: "The tea master will also have a profile in a place where he is sharing his teas. He may even use this to sell his teas or create promotion." And: "If a tea master doesn't have a website, this is a place where he can sell his tea also. Maybe the tea master doesn't have teas he sells, he uses other teas that other people source. This website will be the source for that tea master and they might not even have to stock it. If they buy it from the site, they can get credit. Think of it like affiliate. Maybe it's credit based on tea. We can tune this in later. It's important that it's in the plan. And it is tracked."

This answers blind spots B3 (what a host gains) and B4 (where a host's tea comes from) in one move. A tea master's page is their shelf, and the shelf can hold three kinds of tea:

| Kind of tea on the shelf | Who stocks it | Who is paid | Today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Their own tea, sold by them** | The tea master | The tea master, in full, directly | Built: the personal storefront and offered packs, with your approval |
| **Their own tea, shown and not for sale** | The tea master | Nobody | The Tea Chest's "shown" state, in this plan |
| **Tea from Teajia that they pour and recommend** | Teajia. The tea master holds none | Teajia is paid. The tea master earns **credit** | Their chosen teas show "Selected by". Nothing is tracked or credited |

The third row is the new thing. A tea master with no stock and no website pours Teajia's tea, names it on their page and in their thank-you, and when a guest buys it the sale is counted to them.

**Two parts, built at different times.**

1. **Tracking, early and quietly.** Every order that begins from a tea master's page, their session, their thank-you, their favourites or their invitation carries that tea master's name. It is recorded from the first day the thank-you exists (stage 3), before any credit rule is chosen, so that when the rule is tuned there is real history to tune it on. An order keeps the name it started with. Invoices already record who sold a line, whose stock it was and how it settled (`sold_by_user_id`, `stock_owner_user_id`, `sales_settlements`), so this adds one more name to a record that exists: who it came through.
2. **Credit, later, tuned by Adrian.** The rule is his to set: how much, on which teas, for how long after a session. The recommended form is the one he named, credit in tea. A tea master's credit is spent on tea from the shop, which they then pour for the next table. That keeps the 2026-08-31 decision intact, "money never moves through Teajia", and it feeds the loop instead of paying out of it. A cash commission would need that decision reopened, along with payouts and tax, so it is noted and not planned.

**What each person sees.** The tea master sees, privately, which of their teas guests went on to buy and their credit balance. Adrian sees every tea master's figures in Manage. A guest sees nothing about credit, only "Selected by" and "poured at Mei's table". No figure appears on any public page, which keeps this inside the quiet acknowledgement rule.

**Why this needs curation.** Adrian: "Another reason to curate and facilitate good content, quality content rather than cheap, crappy content." Once a page can earn, some people will fill it to sell. The guards are the ones already chosen: the word on a page is granted by hand, a storefront opens only with his approval, a session's reach is decided by hand, and credit is earned only on tea a guest actually bought, never on clicks, posts or invitations sent. A plain line where a tea master recommends tea says they receive credit when it is bought, so guests are not misled.

## Tables and tea houses

Two kinds of place, and they must never be confused.

- **A table is private.** A tea master adds their table: a name, an area, a photograph if they wish. It is usually their home. It is never listed, never searchable and never on a map. A session at a table shows the area to the public and the address only to confirmed guests, as sessions do now. A travelling tea master may have no table at all.
- **A tea house is public.** It is a place that chose to be seen: a name, where it is, a photograph, a line, its keeper, and the sessions held there. It joins the Visit list when Adrian says yes.
- **A session picks its place** from the host's own table, a table or house whose keeper agreed, or a place named only for that day.
- **Tea masters pouring in other houses** is simply sessions: a tea house page lists who has poured there, taken from its sessions. A private table shows no such list.
- **A shop is a branch.** A house that wants a shelf gets an account as today, by Adrian's hand.
- **Building a house with Adrian** stays the offering in [tea-houses.md](tea-houses.md). It is one of three ways a public house comes to exist: it is already there, its keeper adds it, or it is built with him.

## What this is worth to someone who already hosts

Adrian, 2026-11-26: "Think a bit more about what value this actually offers to people who do tea regularly and host gatherings regularly... I want this to be of service to the greater tea community. Also, it would be nice to be able to explain what this does to people as well. A big portion is also the articles."

### The honest starting point

A person who pours every week already has a system: a WhatsApp group, a notes app, a camera roll, a playlist, and their own memory. It works, it is free, and their guests are already in it. Teajia does not win by doing invitations slightly better. It earns a place only by giving what that pile of apps cannot. There are five such things.

1. **Memory.** After three years of weekly tables a host cannot say what they poured for whom, what a guest said about the 2012 shou, or when a cake was last opened. Every session kept here becomes a record of a tea life: the people, the teas, the words, the photographs, in one place, by date, by person and by tea. This is the deepest value and almost nobody else offers it.
2. **A tea's own story.** A cake in a host's Tea Chest gathers every session it was poured at and every word said about it, year after year. For people who age tea this is the thing they wish they had started ten years ago.
3. **Something beautiful left behind.** A WhatsApp thread scrolls away. A session's page, with its teas, its photographs and its guests' words, stays and looks good. A host can show it to the next person they invite.
4. **Knowledge at the elbow.** The tea on the invitation opens onto its cultivar, its region and its maker in Tea Wisdom, and onto an article where one exists. The host does not have to be the encyclopedia, and the guest can keep learning after they go home.
5. **A way to be found by the right people.** A host's page, an article about them, and their past sessions say who they are better than any profile. A traveller who drinks tea can find a table in a city they do not know.

Everything else in the six functions (invitations, the walkthrough, the playlist, the thank-you) is convenience. Convenience gets a host to try it once. These five are why they stay.

### Not only the hosts: what each person gets

Adrian, 2026-11-26, straight after: "It's not only the hosts... maybe the guest also wants to use it to learn and be educated. Some of the hosts may not sell tea or not be able to tell people what their tea is, or maybe everyone will ask him what the playlist was. I want it to be something that everyone would be happy to use and feel like it is adding something valuable into their life, integrating this into their flow. Because if it's too hard or it's not adding something valuable or making it easier, people won't use it. At the end of the day, if people are buying tea from me, it works as my tea storage management. However, I do want this to be valuable in the way it's valuable to me, to other people."

**The test for every feature.** It must replace something the person already does with something shorter, or answer a question they are already being asked. If it adds a step to their evening, it is cut. Three examples of the test passing:

- Everyone asks the host what the playlist was. Today the host answers six messages. Here the host pastes one link once and it is on the session for good.
- Everyone asks "what was that third tea?" Today the host types it out from memory. Here it is on the session, with the wrapper photograph.
- Everyone asks "where is your house?" Here the walkthrough answers it on confirm.

**What a guest gets, with no host needed.** A guest may use it for years without hosting, and that has to be a full use, not a waiting room.

| A guest wants to | What answers it | Exists today |
|---|---|---|
| Remember what they drank | Every session they sat at, and every tea, kept on Your Table without them doing anything | Partly: sessions and tastings exist, not gathered per guest |
| Learn what it was | Each tea opens onto its type, region and maker in Tea Wisdom, in plain words first | Yes for Teajia's teas; thin for others |
| Learn to taste | The tasting, which builds their own profile of what they like over time (function 5) | Yes |
| Learn to brew it at home | Craft: the course and the brewing guides | Course yes, guides waiting to be written |
| Find that tea, or one like it | The shop, or the host's note on where it came from | Shop yes |
| Keep their own tea in order | Their own Tea Chest: what they have, what is left, what they thought of it | Yes |
| Be invited again | Their name on the host's list (the host's guest book) | No |

The last line of the middle rows is the important one. Adrian uses this to know what tea he has, where it came from, what it cost and what is left. A guest with twelve teas in a cupboard has the same need at a smaller size, and the Tea Chest already gives it to them. That is the plain meaning of "valuable to other people in the way it is valuable to me": everyone gets a small version of Adrian's own shelf, and a session is where two shelves meet.

**A host who does not sell tea.** Nothing asks them to. Their teas show with no price and no buy link, and where it came from is theirs to say or not. They lose nothing in the tool.

**A host who cannot say what their tea is.** This is common: a gift, a cake with a wrapper they cannot read, a bag from a market. Three answers, lightest first.

1. "A tea from my shelf" is a complete entry. A photograph and a nickname is enough. Nothing refuses it.
2. One photograph of the wrapper. The reader that already reads tea packages for Adrian's own intake (it runs on the server and reads Chinese wrappers) suggests what it is: factory, year, type, region. The host accepts, corrects or ignores it. Today that reader is owner-only; opening it to hosts is a cost decision for Adrian because each read costs a little.
3. "Does anyone know this tea?" The host may put the wrapper on the Tea Table with that question, and Adrian or another host may answer. It is a real service to the community and exactly the kind of exchange he described as people learning from each other. It needs the Tea Table to allow a reply from a known person, which today it does not, so it is a later step and his to decide.

**Where it has to meet people's flow.** The invitation and the thank-you travel as one link inside the host's own WhatsApp, because that is where every table already lives. No guest needs an account to accept, to see the walkthrough, to open the thank-you or to read about a tea. An account is asked for only when they want to keep something. A host makes a session in under a minute, or by saying it aloud (idea I9).

### What follows from "of service to the greater tea community"

These are the choices that separate a service to the community from a shop with a party attached. Each needs Adrian's yes.

- **Any tea, from anyone.** A host can pour and name tea from any seller or farmer, and say where it came from, with a link out if they wish. Teajia's tea is simply the easiest to add and to buy. If only Adrian's tea can be shown properly, serious hosts will see a sales tool and leave. This is the single choice that most decides whether the wider community trusts it.
- **A host's record belongs to the host.** They can take all of it away at any time: sessions, guests' names, teas, notes, photographs, as plain files. Saying so on the first screen costs nothing and buys a great deal of trust.
- **Free to host.** No fee to open a table, invite, or keep a record. What sustains it is the tea people choose to buy and, later, the box. Say it once, plainly.
- **The reference is open.** Tea Wisdom and the articles stay free to read with no account. They are the gift to people who will never host or buy.
- **Private by default.** A host who never shares anything publicly still gets all five values above. The Tea Table is a choice, never the price of using the tool.

### Aspects worth switching on, in order of value to a regular host

Not already in the plan unless marked.

1. **The host's own guest book, private.** For each person who has sat at their table: when they came, what they drank, what they liked, and one private line (no caffeine after four, loves aged white, bringing a friend next time). Only the host sees it. This is what a good host carries in their head, and losing it is what limits how many people they can host well.
2. **The cellar that knows what was poured.** The Tea Chest already holds a host's teas. Add: grams left after each session, the sessions it appeared in, and a short line on how it is changing. A tea running low shows quietly on the host's own page, with the same tea or its nearest in the shop if there is one.
3. **The brew, kept if wanted.** Pot or gaiwan, grams, water, how many infusions. One optional line per tea, entered after, never during. It also connects sessions to teaware, which is most of the shop's shelf (117 pieces against 20 teas) and has no part in the loop yet.
4. **A table that meets again** (idea I5), grown slightly: a named circle, such as "Thursday table", with its own running record. Most real tea life is the same six people returning, and the record of a circle over a year is worth more than any single session.
5. **A card for the table.** One printed page or one image: tonight's teas in order, a line on each, and one small code that leads to the session afterward. It keeps phones away better than any rule, and it is the kind of object hosts already make by hand.
6. **A tea for the table, shared.** A host orders one cake or a few teas for the circle and the guests each take a share. Tea people already do this informally. It is the most natural way Adrian's tea enters a table, and it joins the host's credit and the host's box (idea I1). Payment stays between the people, as everywhere else.
7. **Series.** A teacher runs six evenings on oolong. A series is a few sessions under one name with one page, a reading for each evening, and a guest's notes gathered across all of them. It serves the people who teach, who are the most regular hosts of all.
8. **Sitting in.** Someone who wants to host can ask to pour beside an experienced host once. "Pouring" already exists as a word on a session, so this needs only a way to ask. It is how the craft has always passed on.
9. **A waiting list and a released seat.** When a table of six is full, the seventh waits, and takes the seat if someone withdraws. Small, and it removes the most common message a regular host has to answer.
10. **The host's handbook, in Read.** How to host a first table, how much tea for six people, how to pour for people who have never had gongfu tea, what to do when someone dislikes a tea. Written by Adrian and, in time, by other hosts. See the next section.

Recommended to switch on first: 1, 2 and 5. They are small, they need no one but the host, and they give the memory that nothing else gives. Number 6 is the one closest to Adrian's own payback.

### The articles are the third leg

The site has three things of value: the tea, the tables and the writing. The plan so far treats Read as a shelf to one side. For a regular host it is closer to the centre, in five ways.

- **Before a session:** the invitation can carry one piece to read, chosen by the host. "We are pouring Wuyi rock tea on Saturday; here is ten minutes on where it comes from."
- **After a session:** the thank-you links each tea to its reading. A guest who liked a tea can go deeper that evening, which is when they are most curious and most likely to buy.
- **About people:** the standing series on who someone is and why they love tea (already under "Later"). For a host this is the best introduction they could have, and it is the quiet acknowledgement Adrian wanted, given by one person to another.
- **From sessions:** a finished session can already become a draft article (`draft_article_from_event`). A good tasting of five Dancong, with the guests' words, is a real piece that no magazine has.
- **From hosts:** experienced hosts know things that are written nowhere. A host may offer a piece; Adrian edits and decides. Their name is on it. This is how Read grows past what one person can write, and it is service to the community in the plainest sense.

The catch, stated plainly: there are five published articles today, all from one April batch. The articles can only carry this weight if they keep coming. One piece every two weeks, half of them about people and sessions that happened, would be enough, and sessions are what would supply them.

## Decisions

**Answered by Adrian, 2026-11-26.**

- The words: yes. A session has a host, and others may be pouring. The word master is not used for a session role.
- A tea master's place is a private table, never public.
- A Tea Chest tea may be kept, shown as a favourite, or offered for a while.

- Who may host: "Anyone can host. There is moderation and curation depending on the people who are hosting. This is where gamification helps, but it's covert gamification."
- Quiet acknowledgement: yes. It is now the third exception in `CLAUDE.md`.

**What "anyone can host" means in the build.** Every session can exist, and its host picks its door: by request, by link, or open. How far it is shown depends on its host, and that is decided by a person, never by a counter. Three reaches: by invitation only (the default for a new host), open to people already on Teajia, and listed publicly. Adrian, or the keeper of a tea house for sessions there, moves a host from one reach to the next. The host is told plainly, in private, that listed sessions are chosen by hand. Nothing on anyone's page shows which reach they have.

Still open from the first pass: the four bar words (read, table, houses, shop), the Tea Table rule exception, the share wording, review or hide, Your Table as a page, and Adrian's facts for the build offering. They are listed in [docs/SECTIONS.md](../../docs/SECTIONS.md). New questions raised by the review below are gathered at its end.

## Adrian's answers to the review (2026-11-26, later the same day)

So every session has one of three doors, chosen by its host: **by request** (the host says yes to each seat), **by link** (anyone holding the invitation has a seat), or **open** (anyone may take a seat). A private table starts at by request. The report line stands.

- **B3 and B4, what a host gains and where their tea comes from.** "Either they will buy it or they will have their own." And the reason for the whole thing, in his words: "My goal is to make this app or program valuable to them too, but then make it easy for my teas to be used and purchased. The more people who use and integrate this into their system, the more visibility I will have. That's the payback, the visibility for my tea and slowly selling more." This is the business sentence of the plan. It means the tools must be worth using for a host who never pours a Teajia tea, and that Teajia's teas must be the easiest ones to add to a session, a shelf or a thank-you.
- **B5, one kind of session.** He asked for a recommendation. It is below.
- **B7, photographs of other people.** Yes to the line on every invitation and the tick when offering a photograph.
- **B9, what the Tea Table is.** "The table's not supposed to be an event calendar, it's supposed to be what has happened in the past and look really good." He is weighing whether likes on past sessions and photographs should keep some higher, or whether it stays in time order: "It's kind of like a feed." See "The Tea Table is the past" below.

### Recommendation on B5: one kind of session

Build everything on the richer of the two, the sessions that already have seats, RSVP, hosts, teas, recaps and a thank-you path, and fold the member's private sit into it.

- **Every host gets a small private account behind the scenes**, the same one a Tea Chest already creates for a person. Their sessions belong to it. This keeps the standing rule that every record has an account, and it means a host's sessions, shelf and Tea Chest live in one place. Nothing about it is shown as a "shop".
- **A private sit becomes a session with the door set to by link** and one seat or more. The private sits already recorded are carried over, not lost. That is a change to stored records, so by the standing rule Adrian is shown a page of before and after rows first.
- **A person never needs Manage to host.** Hosting is done from Your Table: date, seats, door, teas, invite. Manage stays for people who run a shop or a house.
- **The tools follow the one model**, so there is one invitation, one thank-you and one set of voice and agent tools, not two.
- **The cost of not doing this**: every later stage is built twice, and a member's sit can never have seats, more than one invitation, a thank-you or credit.

One check before building: read how much of the session code assumes a shop account with staff, so that the size of this is known before stage 2 is promised.

### The Tea Table is the past

Corrected from the earlier draft, which put the coming sessions under the same word. The Tea Table shows what has happened: sessions poured, the words people gave, the photographs, the teas. It should be the best-looking page on the site, because it is what makes a visitor want to be at the next one. Coming sessions are not on it. They are found from Home's "Come to a table", from a host's page and from an invitation.

**Decided by Adrian, 2026-11-26: time order, held up by hand, with a private keep.**

- The table is newest first.
- The host of a session, and Adrian for the whole table, can hold a session or a photograph at the top for a while.
- Anyone can **keep** a session or a photograph. It saves to their own favourites.
- Nobody sees how many people kept anything: not the person who made it, not other guests, not the host. Adrian sees it privately in Manage and may use it when choosing what to hold up. Nothing rises by itself.
- Likes that show were considered and not chosen.

## Blind spots and ideas (review of 2026-11-26)

Adrian asked for one more pass over the whole thing: the holes, and what he had not thought of. These are in order of how much they could hurt or help.

### Blind spots

**B1. Strangers in homes.** "Anyone can join" plus "anyone can host" plus "a table is someone's home" is the one place this could go badly wrong for a real person. Proposed floor, none of it optional: a seat at a private table always needs the host's yes; the address appears only after that yes; a new guest arrives with a line saying who invited them or that nobody did; every session and every person has a quiet report line that reaches Adrian; Adrian can pause a host or a guest in one tap; under-18s sit only with an adult. A guest is shown to other guests by first name and can choose not to be shown.

**B2. The word "tea master" is about to be worn thin.** If anyone can host, then on the site anyone is "the tea master" of their session, while "tea master" is also the word Adrian grants by hand. A first-time host calling themselves a tea master on a public page will look wrong to the very people Adrian most wants to respect. Proposed: a session says "hosted by" and "pouring"; "tea master" stays the granted word on a person's page. Adrian's meaning, master of ceremonies in the moment, is kept by the word host.

**B3. What a host gains is not written anywhere.** A guest's why is clear. A host's is thinner than it looks: when their guests buy Adrian's tea after a session, the host receives nothing and is not even told. The rule that money never moves through Teajia can stay, and the host can still be honoured: an order placed from a session's thank-you carries "from Mei's table" to Adrian, the host sees that their guests found tea through them (no amounts), and Adrian has a real basis for the things he gives by hand: the wholesale tier, the page word, a wider reach. This is where the covert part lives, and it is made of his judgement, not a formula.

**B4. Where does a host's tea come from?** The loop has no step for it, and it is the step that is Adrian's business. A host needs tea to pour. See idea I1.

**B5. Two kinds of session exist in the code, and "anyone can host" lands on the weaker one.** Sessions with RSVP, seats, hosts and recaps belong to an account and are made in Manage (`worker/src/eventDomain.ts`). A member's private sit is a different table (`member_private_sits`, migration 0109) with one invitation and no seats. Building invitations, the thank-you and the tools twice would be the costliest mistake available here. The first engineering decision of stage 2 is one session model that both kinds use, with the account being optional.

**B6. A guest with no account has to be reachable and has to be the same person later.** The thank-you needs somewhere to go, and the site does not message people. The honest channel is the one already in use: the host shares one link in their own WhatsApp, and each guest opens their own page from it. What a guest adds before signing up must attach to them when they do. Parts of this exist (the personal after-sit link, the phone-verified journey). It needs to be one path.

**B7. Photographs of other people.** Guests photograph each other and Adrian posts to Instagram. The current consent covers the photographer, not the people pictured. Proposed: one line on every invitation ("photographs may be taken; tell your host if you would rather not be in them"), a tick when offering a photograph that the people in it are fine with it, and an "offered for Instagram" state separate from "on the site", since the reach is very different. Adrian dropped the lawyer's read on 2026-11-21; pictures of guests in private homes is the case that would justify one short read after all.

**B8. Adrian becomes the moderator of everything.** Anyone hosts, anyone shares, and today every hide and every approval is his. Hosts should review what appears on their own session (the Guest Book already works this way), keepers their own house, and Adrian only what is listed publicly and what is reported.

**B9. An empty table looks dead.** Read from the live figures in the pillar: no upcoming sessions, one tea master page, five articles. If the Tea Table and the Home strips open now, they open empty. The bar word "table" should not go live until there is a real flow: for example ten sessions with something shared, from at least three hosts. Until then it is reached from Home and from sessions.

**B10. Language.** Bali, China and English-speaking guests share these tables. Words on the Tea Table will arrive in several languages. Decide now that a person's words are shown as written, with nothing auto-translated in place, and that the invitation and thank-you screens are the first things worth translating.

**B11. Seat price.** `TODO.md` already says sessions have no seat price, so close-outs land as zero-priced orders. With many hosts this gets worse. A session should be able to say "free", "by contribution" or a price, paid to the host directly, with the site only recording the host's word that it was settled.

**B12. Contact details pile up with every host.** If each host collects names and numbers, every home table becomes a small customer database. Keep a guest's contact in the host's WhatsApp, where it already is, and store only a name on the site unless the guest signs up.

**B13. This is a second product, and the first one is not yet proven.** The working plan's priority is one real sale and one real sit. Nothing above should pass that queue. See idea I6 for a way to learn most of it without building.

### Ideas

**I1. The host's box, sooner than "later".** Adrian placed the tasting box six to twelve months out. Its smaller form may be the real front door for hosting: three or four teas, enough for six people, sold from the shop, arriving with the session already made. The teas are listed, the invitation is ready to send, the thank-you is drafted. A guest who wants to pour buys one and is a host that weekend. It answers B4, it is the August brief's "Second Table in a Box", and it makes every new host a customer. It needs only sets that can be bought, which is already an open line in `TODO.md`.

**I2. A sit of one.** A person at home with one tea is a session with one seat. If the journal treats it that way, the online-only person is inside the same loop from their first day: they sit, they note, they may share, and the day they invite one friend nothing about the screen changes.

**I3. Taste along.** The same tea, the same week, many tables. No live video and nothing during. Afterward the Tea Table shows what each table made of that one tea, side by side. It is the gentlest way for scattered people to learn from each other, and it sells one tea deeply instead of twenty thinly.

**I4. Paper first.** For a tasting, a printed sheet per session with the teas already on it. Guests write by hand. Afterward someone photographs the sheets and they are read into each person's notes, using the reader that already scans tea packages and receipts. The phone stays in the bag for the whole session and nothing is lost.

**I5. Same table again.** Most real tables are the same few people returning. One action for a host: "same table, next date", which makes the next session with the same guests invited. No following and no notices from the site, only the host's invitation as before.

**I6. Run the next sit by hand.** Before any of stages 2 to 4 is built, do one of Adrian's own sessions entirely by hand: a WhatsApp invitation, then a thank-you sent as a plain message with the teas, the links, the playlist and the three asks. See what comes back, in what form, from whom. One evening of evidence will reshape the thank-you screen more than any drawing, and it is the "one real sit" the closeout plan has been waiting for.

**I7. Travelling tea masters and open tables.** A tea master says "I am in Ubud for March and glad to pour". A keeper or a host with a table says "my table is open to a visiting tea master". Adrian introduces them. No matching engine: a short private list and his hand. It is the lineage made practical, and it is how a house hears of the people who might one day keep it.

**I8. The path to a tea house runs through the table.** The first thread's centre was building tea houses with Adrian. It has not been lost: the person who has hosted at home for a year, whose guests return and buy, is the founder that offering is for. The private view of hosts (B3) is also the list of people to have that conversation with.

**I9. Voice before tools.** Other tea masters connecting their own assistant is real but far. Most will not set anything up. The nearer version of the same wish: a host says into their phone "Saturday at four, six seats, the two oolongs and the aged white", and the session is drafted for them to confirm. The transcription and the tea word library are already there.

**I10. Instagram as the far end of the same photograph.** A picture offered for Instagram arrives in one place for Adrian with its credit line and the session it came from, ready to post. The Tea Table is the record and Instagram is the announcement, and the same consent step governs both.

## Not building

- Ranks, levels or badges for guests or tea masters. No count of sessions poured or attended on anyone's page.
- A public list of who attended a session. The events decision stands: no attendee directories.
- Following a tea master, or being told when they open a session.
- A sign-up wall in front of an invitation.
- Anything that prompts, notifies or scrolls during a session.
- A public address, map or listing for a tea master's own table.
- A thank-you sent by a timer, or a reminder to a guest who did not answer one.
- A rating of tea masters, houses or sessions.
- Credit for anything other than tea a guest bought: no credit for clicks, posts, invitations or sign-ups.
- A public figure of what anyone sold or earned, and any chart that ranks tea masters by sales.
- An application form to become a tea master. The word on the page stays Adrian's to give.
